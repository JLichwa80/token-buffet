## Description
Straddle rules under IRC Section 1092 govern tax treatment when an investor holds offsetting positions in personal property, designed to prevent the selective realization of losses while deferring offsetting gains. Under post-1984 stock straddle rules, standard exchange-traded collar structures introduce notable tax inefficiencies: call premiums received are treated as currently taxable short-term gains, while put losses are disallowed and must be capitalized into the underlying stock's tax basis rather than deducted immediately. These statutory loss deferral and basis adjustment mechanics create significant tax drag for concentrated equity holders. To bypass these straddle rule inefficiencies, practitioners favor fused single over-the-counter (OTC) collar instruments or variable prepaid forward contracts (VPFs), which integrate the collar components to avoid piecemeal tax straddle distortions.
