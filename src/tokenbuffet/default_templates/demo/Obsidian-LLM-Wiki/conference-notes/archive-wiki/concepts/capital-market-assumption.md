## Description

A Capital Market Assumption is a portfolio construction input, and the keynote frames it as the first place where taxes should be incorporated. Sam’s taxes-first philosophy argues that advisors should adjust CMAs for tax drag at the asset class level before running portfolio optimization, rather than building a pre-tax allocation and addressing taxes afterward. This requires analyzing each asset class’s source of return, such as income versus capital appreciation, and its holding style, such as active versus passive management. The result is an allocation process based on realistic after-tax return expectations within a broader [[After-Tax Investing]] framework. In the keynote, this CMA-level adjustment is part of a whole-portfolio approach that starts with tax-aware public market solutions like [[municipal-bond|municipal-bond]] and [[Preferred Securities]] before extending into private market structures.
Formally, Capital Market Assumptions (CMAs) are long-term forecasts of expected risk, return, and covariance across asset classes utilized in strategic asset allocation. Whereas traditional wealth management models CMAs strictly on pre-tax returns—relegating tax considerations to product wrappers or post-construction adjustments—incorporating taxes directly at the CMA stage fundamentally reshapes the baseline optimization.
