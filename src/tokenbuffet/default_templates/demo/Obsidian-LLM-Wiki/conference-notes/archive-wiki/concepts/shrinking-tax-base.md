## Description
A shrinking tax base is a structural economic phenomenon where broad categories of income and economic activity are increasingly carved out or exempted from taxation, concentrating the revenue burden onto a progressively narrower segment of taxpayers. Politically expedient and populist proposals—such as "no tax on" initiatives targeting tips, overtime, or retirement pay, alongside targeted wealth taxes—accelerate this contraction. By 2026, projections indicate that the top 20% of earners will bear 71% of federal income taxes, whereas the bottom 60% will contribute only 11%. This ongoing narrowing of taxable activity significantly reduces overall fiscal resilience, heightens volatility in government revenue, and compounds long-term deficits.
