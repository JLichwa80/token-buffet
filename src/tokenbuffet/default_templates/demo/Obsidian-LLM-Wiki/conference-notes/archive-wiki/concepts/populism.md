## Description
Populism in modern fiscal debates describes the widespread preference for simplistic, politically appealing tax proposals over structurally sound, pro-growth economic reforms. Present on both sides of the political aisle, this trend is significantly exacerbated by a low level of policy expertise within legislatures, allowing catchy slogans to triumph over rigorous analysis. Consequently, it manifests in progressive efforts such as wealth taxes as well as conservative campaigns like "no tax on" exemptions. By prioritizing immediate political appeal or targeted relief and punishment over sustainable fiscal mechanics, populist tax policies ultimately drive significant tax base erosion and long-term fiscal instability.
