# Wall Street vs. Washington
## Description
The “Wall Street vs. Washington” concept describes the institutional and conceptual gap between financial-market innovation and government tax-policy design. As FM Investments frames it, “Wall Street vs. Washington is 230 miles and an infinite number of misunderstandings,” because Washington writes tax policy in fragments, schedules sunsets, and implements rules through extensive regulation, while Wall Street designs products and operations around those constraints. This gap creates opportunities for policy arbitrage and tax alpha through intelligent wrappers, including dual share class structures, AMT-aware designs, proxy funds, in-kind creation and redemption, step-up basis, the “sawtooth pattern,” and tokenized T-bills designed to reduce operational costs, tighten spreads, enable continuous liquidity, and preserve or improve tax outcomes. Catchphrases such as “Washington designed your portfolio,” “Harry Potter-length tax code,” “the spider’s web of acronyms,” “race to die,” “Cobra effect,” and “ETF not done yet” emphasize the complexity and unintended consequences of the policy environment, while FM Investments’ broader strategy turns temporary and frequently revised tax rules into enduring portfolio advantages for investors with 20–30 year horizons.
FM Investments pioneered dual share class structures that deliver ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers, and it presents intelligent wrappers and innovation as the antidote that makes the investor always win. In this framing, tax policy is implemented through millions of words of regulation. The concept’s working vocabulary also includes “CPMG/CPHY,” named alongside proxy funds, the “sawtooth pattern,” and the other intelligent-wrapper terms that convert fragmented tax rules into portfolio design choices.
