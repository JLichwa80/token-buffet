# Cobra effect in tax policy
The Cobra effect in tax policy is an economic theory describing instances where a legislative or regulatory incentive exacerbates the exact problem it was designed to fix. The term is a cautionary metaphor for unintended consequences, originally referencing a colonial-era bounty scheme that produced more of the problem it aimed to solve (paying for rat tails produced more rats). In tax policy and financial markets, the concept highlights the gap between Washington's intentions and Wall Street's reality: policymakers design tax rules without foreseeing how sophisticated investment professionals will respond and navigate around them. Illustrated through historical policies like the Alternative Minimum Tax, designed to target 155 wealthy families but now capturing millions of everyday taxpayers, it demonstrates how static regulatory interventions create second-order behavioral distortions that sophisticated investors can systematically anticipate and navigate. [[fm-investments|FM Investments]] builds its business model partly on identifying Cobra-effect dynamics in tax policy, then designing structures that preserve investor returns while navigating the unintended consequences Washington created. The term underscores that a "Harry Potter-length tax code" and the "spider's web of acronyms" it creates leave infinite space for policy to misfire.
