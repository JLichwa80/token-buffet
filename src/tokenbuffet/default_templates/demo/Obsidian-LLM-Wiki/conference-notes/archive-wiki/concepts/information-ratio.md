## Description
The Information Ratio (IR) is a risk-adjusted performance metric that quantifies portfolio manager skill by dividing pretax active alpha by active target tracking error risk. In empirical simulations spanning 21 rolling 15-year periods from January 1989 onward, IR is systematically varied across a range from -1.0 to +1.0 to establish the sensitivity of after-tax wealth and cumulative net capital losses to manager execution across long-short portfolio archetypes (such as 150/50, 200/100, and 250/150 with informed tracking errors of 2%, 4%, and 6%). Gross manager skill serves as a critical prerequisite in tax-aware long-short strategies because zero gross skill can deteriorate into net IRs as low as -0.6 once financing costs, transaction friction, and fees are accounted for. When framing tax overlay and trade structures in information-ratio terms, a reliable after-tax benefit makes trades compelling, situating IR as the horizontal axis of the alpha slope relating manager skill directly to loss generation and tax outcomes.
While a higher Information Ratio diminishes long-term tax-loss harvesting capacity in long-only portfolios by accelerating unrealized capital gains, it paradoxically steepens the generation of realized tax losses in leveraged long-short strategies through continuous dynamic position creation.
