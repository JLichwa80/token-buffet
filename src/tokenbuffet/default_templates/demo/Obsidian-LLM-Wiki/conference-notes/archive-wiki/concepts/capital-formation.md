## Description
Capital formation is the macroeconomic process of accumulating and expanding physical, intellectual, and financial capital to enhance an economy's productive capacity. In fiscal policy analysis, particularly within the macroeconomic modeling utilized by the [[Tax Foundation]], it serves alongside [[Labor Supply]] as a foundational metric for determining whether tax legislation is genuinely pro-growth. Rather than equating tax reductions broadly with economic expansion, this framework evaluates reforms by their empirical capacity to boost long-term investment, emphasizing full deductibility for business investment and [[R&D Expensing]]. Because temporary rate adjustments do not drive sustained economic expansion, durable capital formation relies critically on policy [[permanency]] to incentivize long-horizon capital commitments.
