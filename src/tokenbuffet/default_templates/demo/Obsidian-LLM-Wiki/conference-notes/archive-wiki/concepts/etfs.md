# ETFs
## Definition
ETFs (exchange-traded funds) are a portfolio wrapper: a legal container that sets who can buy a strategy and how tax reaches the holder. It provides fund exposure through shares traded in a market. The talk treats the ETF as large, still unfinished, and politically durable. The market figure given is about $14 trillion, against about $31 trillion in [[Mutual Funds]]. Among [[investment-wrappers]], the ETF is the vehicle expected to take the next changes first: tokenization, synchronization with a [[Mutual Funds|mutual fund]] through [[Dual Share Class]], and income solutions such as [[cpmgcphy|cpmgcphy]]. The same discussion also ties the ETF to tax alpha and intelligent wrappers.
## Key Characteristics
- Market size is given at about $14 trillion, against about $31 trillion in [[Mutual Funds]]. The structure is described as large and still unfinished.
- Operating cost at scale is put at roughly 3 to 6 basis points. [[Tokenized T-Bills]] are supposed to cut that cost and show the saving in NAV, with tighter spreads and 24/7 trading.
- [[in-kind-creationredemption|In-kind Creation/Redemption]] is described as politically safe. Attacks on the mechanism are called dead on arrival, in the same breath as carried-interest repeal being a perennial nonstarter.
- [[Dual Share Class]] puts one portfolio in an ETF and a [[Mutual Funds|mutual fund]] at the same NAV and the same rights. [[vanguard]] is the approval standard cited for that structure.
- The income use rests on ETF accounting. [[cpmgcphy|cpmgcphy]] rotates a bond position around ex-dates so appreciation stays in NAV while ordinary-income distributions are avoided.
- [[investment-wrappers]] as a category are given a "last good gasp" while products are built beyond them. The ETF is the wrapper described as absorbing those changes first.
## Applications
[[FM Investments]] presents treasury ETFs across the yield curve, "[[t-bill]] through thirty," as tax-efficient access with high trading efficiency. [[t-bill]] and [[tbmax]] are the paired lines on the dual-share chart: one portfolio, so a [[Mutual Funds|mutual fund]] buyer and an ETF buyer share NAV and rights. The stated client use is to bring [[etf-trading-efficiency|ETF trading efficiency]] to the mutual-fund buyer. [[vanguard]] is cited as the approval standard for that [[Dual Share Class]].
[[tokenization|Tokenization]] is the operating application. The pitch is to lower the roughly 3 to 6 basis-point cost of a scaled ETF, remove counterparties, break the penny spread, trade 24/7, reflect the saving in NAV, and preserve or improve tax outcomes. [[Tokenized T-Bills]] are the first case named.
The income application is [[cpmgcphy|cpmgcphy]]. A bond ETF accrues income in NAV each day, and the tax impact is described as occurring when a dividend is received. The rotation moves into a substitute ETF around the ex-date and returns afterward, so the holder keeps price appreciation and avoids the ordinary-income distribution. Stated results include no 1099 capital-gains distribution, ordinary income avoided, long-term treatment if the position is held 366 days, roll cost of about 1 basis point a month, and an add-on fee of about 25 basis points.
The closing application is structural. The ETF is to be tokenized, synchronized with the [[Mutual Funds|mutual fund]], and used to solve for income. [[investment-wrappers]] are described as having a last phase while products are built beyond them, with the ETF taking those changes first. [[in-kind-creationredemption|In-kind Creation/Redemption]] is the mechanism treated as politically durable underneath that role.
