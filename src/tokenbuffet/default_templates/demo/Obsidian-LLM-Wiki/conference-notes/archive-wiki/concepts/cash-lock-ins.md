## Description
Cash lock-in occurs when an investor holds onto highly appreciated, suboptimal positions due to the reluctance or fear of triggering substantial capital gains taxes upon liquidation. This embedded tax liability freezes capital in legacy investments—such as concentrated private business holdings or legacy mutual funds—preventing necessary rebalancing and diversification while exposing the portfolio to uncompensated idiosyncratic risk. Identified as a major component of layered, compounding tax drag, cash lock-in represents a core challenge when shifting from generic pre-tax product models to custom after-tax portfolio design. Rather than relying on binary sell-or-hold decisions, advisors address cash lock-in as after-tax architects through multi-period transition roadmaps, gain budgeting, and lot pairing across the broader household balance sheet.
