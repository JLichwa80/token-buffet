## Description
After-tax benchmarking is an evaluation framework that measures portfolio performance net of all investment fees, ongoing taxes, and terminal liquidation costs rather than relying on conventional pre-tax hurdle rates. In real-world taxable investing, achieving annualized excess returns ahead of a headline benchmark net of all costs and taxes is exceptionally difficult due to implementation friction. The practical benchmark accounts for an estimated 170 basis points of tax drag and liquidation costs; beating the headline benchmark minus 1.70% indicates strong performance across all tax layers. Applying an after-tax hurdle exposes the reality that many factor and [[smart beta]] strategies that appear to generate alpha gross of fees ultimately fail after tax. Furthermore, after-tax comparisons highlight the structural advantages of vehicles such as [[ETF versus mutual fund tax efficiency|ETFs over mutual funds]], revealing how implementation choices determine the portion of return an investor actually keeps.
