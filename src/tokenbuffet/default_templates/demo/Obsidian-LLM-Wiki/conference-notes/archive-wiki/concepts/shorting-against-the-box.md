## Description
Shorting against the box is an investment technique where an investor shorts the identical number of shares of a stock they already own long, historically utilized to lock in unrealized capital gains without triggering an immediate sale. When mishandled, the technique can trigger onerous tax consequences under constructive sale rules, including disallowed losses that the tax code will not recognize. Because of these severe penalties, hedging concentrated wealth positions cannot rely on isolated shorting against the box. Instead, broader tax-aware long-short implementations must be carefully structured across a client's core U.S. or global equity exposures rather than managed as a small, isolated sleeve to preserve valid tax offsets.
