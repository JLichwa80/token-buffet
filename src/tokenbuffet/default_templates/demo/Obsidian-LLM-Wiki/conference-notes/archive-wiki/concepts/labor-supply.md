## Description
Labor supply represents the aggregate volume of labor individuals are willing and able to offer across varying wage levels and net after-tax incentives. Within macroeconomic modeling, such as that utilized by the [[Tax Foundation]], labor supply serves alongside [[Capital Formation]] as a foundational pillar for determining whether tax policy adjustments produce authentic, long-term economic expansion rather than temporary market fluctuations. Policies designed to spur [[Pro-Growth Reform]] explicitly focus on optimizing incentives to work, as poorly structured or distortionary measures can penalize marginal effort, suppress labor force participation, and hinder sustained economic output.
