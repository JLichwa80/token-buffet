# Tax-Aware Long-Short Equity

> Stub created by the ingest candidate gate (aside+covered): [[wiki/sources/at this tax conference-panel-discussion_-variable-prepaid-forwards-tax-aware-long-short-an_58d676]] names this without treating it. Will be filled by the next ingest of a source that does.
Tax-aware long-short equity is a quantitative investment approach executed via separately managed accounts (SMAs) or series LLCs that matches a broad market equity benchmark while systematically harvesting persistent net capital losses. These generated losses serve as a tax shield against capital gains recognized across the client's broader wealth structure. Accounts can often be funded in-kind using appreciated single-name stocks to transition exposures gradually without front-loaded tax drag.
> "Core idea: AQR-style quantitative long-short equity, now accessible in SMAs/single-member series LLCs, generates persistent capital losses to offset gains." ([[wiki/sources/at this tax conference-panel-discussion_-variable-prepaid-forwards-tax-aware-long-short-an_58d676]])
## Description

As an advanced portfolio implementation strategy, tax-aware long-short overlays long and short equity extensions (such as 130/30, 150/50, 200/100, or 250/150 structures) over pledged collateral to harvest cross-sectional dispersion and enhance after-tax returns. By shorting individual securities alongside long equity exposures, managers systematically harvest realized and unrealized capital losses, generating estimated excess tax alpha exceeding 300 basis points. Originally an institutional technique, modern retail vehicles allow individuals to access these strategies without IRC Section 465 at-risk limitations, making them a functional utility tool for high-net-worth households facing concentrated wealth or pending liquidity events. Prudent deployment requires rigorous pre-implementation screening across four criteria: underwriting pretax alpha, confirming tangible client utility for harvested loss inventory, evaluating risk tolerance for leverage, and securing client willingness to restructure core equity exposures.
The strategy targets active returns and tax efficiency while maintaining a portfolio beta of approximately one. By incorporating leverage and short positions that are negatively correlated with the broader market, it enables continuous tax-loss harvesting across both rising and falling market environments. Between 2023 and 2026, tax-aware long-short strategies saw rapid institutional and retail adoption, scaling from approximately $9 billion to over $150 billion in assets under management.
In wealth transition scenarios, this technology-enabled strategy is framed as a "third path" beyond either retaining an overconcentrated holding or liquidating it and immediately incurring a substantial tax liability. By systematically generating short-position losses to offset realized capital gains from selling appreciated assets or business equity, it facilitates a [[tax-neutral-transitions|tax-neutral transition]]. Leverage parameters can be customized (including higher extension levels such as [[275-long-275-short|275 long, 275 short]]) to calibrate transition velocity alongside [[daily-tax-loss-harvesting|daily tax-loss harvesting]]. However, because its long-short risk profile is distinct from standard market risk, disciplined execution demands a planned [[deleveraging-path|deleveraging path]] once initial tax objectives are met, operating as part of dynamic, integrated portfolio management ([[integrated-portfolio-management|Whole Portfolio Unification]]) to systematically de-risk holdings toward the client's target asset allocation.
