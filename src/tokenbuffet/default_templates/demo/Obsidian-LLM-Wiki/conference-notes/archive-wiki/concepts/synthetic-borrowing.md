## Description
Synthetic borrowing is an options-based financing mechanism—most commonly structured as a short box spread combining a bull call spread and a bear put spread with identical strikes and expiration dates—that replicates a zero-coupon bond to secure cash at institutional rates without requiring a traditional margin loan from a prime broker. Because the terminal payoff of the box spread is deterministic, borrowing costs are effectively fixed and insulate the borrower from broker margin calls. Mechanically, the structure generates capital losses upon expiration that can be harvested to offset recognized gains in a tax-aware portfolio. It serves as a viable, non-hedging alternative when an investor seeks efficient leverage or liquidity without establishing downside price protection on an underlying asset.
