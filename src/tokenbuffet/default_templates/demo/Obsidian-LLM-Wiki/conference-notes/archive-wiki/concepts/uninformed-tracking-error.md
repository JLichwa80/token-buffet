# Uninformed Tracking Error
## Definition
**Uninformed tracking error** refers to deliberate deviations from a benchmark index introduced specifically for operational, execution, or tax-management objectives rather than to capture active return or pretax alpha. Unlike informed tracking error, which is driven by active return forecasts or factor tilts, uninformed tracking error accepts a degree of benchmark divergence purely to facilitate tax optimization mechanisms such as tax-loss harvesting and capital gains deferral.
## Key Characteristics
- **Absence of Alpha Forecasting:** Deviations from benchmark weights are not informed by return predictions; the expected active pretax return from these deviations is zero.
- **Beta Preservation:** Portfolios are constructed to maintain approximate benchmark beta while accepting a targeted tracking error budget (typically around 1%).
- **Tax-Driven Variance:** Volatility and divergence relative to the benchmark serve as an operational mechanism to generate realized losses against taxable gains.
- **Long-Horizon Diminishing Returns:** Over extended investment horizons, relying solely on uninformed tracking error leads to portfolio lock-up with built-in gains as cost bases rise and opportunities for loss harvesting decline.
## Applications
- **Direct Indexing Portfolios:** Utilized in long-only direct indexing strategies where managers introduce a small tracking error allowance (e.g., 1%) to systematically realize tax losses while tracking benchmark indices like the [[russell-1000|Russell 1000]].
- **Tax-Loss Harvesting:** Employed to harvest tax offsets without significantly altering the portfolio's core market risk profile.
- **Capital Gains Deferral:** Used to avoid liquidating appreciated holdings, accepting minor tracking mismatch instead of incurring tax liabilities.
