## Description

Return of capital is a tax-deferral mechanism in which part of an investment distribution is treated as a recovery of invested capital rather than current ordinary income. In the private real estate context, [[Perpetual Non-Traded REIT]] structures can use depreciation pass-through to create return of capital treatment, deferring ordinary income taxes until the position is sold. The source describes the deferred amount as being taxed as a long-term capital gain upon sale, while also reducing the investor's cost basis during the holding period. This makes return of capital important as a tax-advantaged feature of private real estate vehicles, beyond their role as ordinary income generators. It is part of a broader after-tax investing approach that starts with tax-aware portfolio construction rather than treating taxes as an afterthought.
Beyond private real estate structures, return of capital functions as a tax accounting classification for fund distributions exceeding current and accumulated earnings and profits. In high-yielding or derivative-based strategies, aggressive return of capital distributions can mask underlying principal erosion while generating severe internal tax imbalances or massive unrealized gains that disproportionately penalize remaining shareholders upon subsequent redemption.
Casey presents return of capital from depreciation pass-through as one of the perpetual non-traded REIT’s two key tax advantages, alongside the QBI deduction. This treatment reduces the investor’s investment cost while deferring ordinary income taxes until sale.
