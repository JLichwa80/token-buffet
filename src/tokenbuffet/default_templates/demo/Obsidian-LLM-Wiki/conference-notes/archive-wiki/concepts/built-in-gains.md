## Description
Built-in gains represent the aggregate unrealized capital appreciation embedded within a portfolio, measured as a percentage of gross notional exposure. A position opened at [[Full Cost Basis]] carries cost basis equal to market value and initially has no embedded gains. In [[Long-Only Direct Indexing]], higher manager alpha accelerates portfolio lock-up by expanding built-in gains over multi-year horizons, which diminishes ongoing tax-loss harvesting opportunities. Conversely, in leveraged long-short strategies such as [[15050-long-short|150/50 Long-Short]] and 200/100, [[Position Creation]] introduces fresh, unappreciated cost basis that flattens or actively reduces built-in gains as the information ratio rises. Maintaining lower percentage built-in gains preserves structural flexibility, rendering future portfolio rebalancing or shifts in leverage, beta, and tracking error materially less tax-costly.
