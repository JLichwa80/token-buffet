## Description
Tax alpha is the enduring portfolio advantage created by exploiting the gap between Wall Street and Washington—reframing the tax code as a toolkit rather than a constraint. It is generated by engineering product [[investment-wrappers|wrappers]] that turn complicated, often temporary tax rules into permanent efficiencies, reducing taxes, operational friction, and trading costs. In this framing, wrappers are the mechanism that converts investment alpha into holdable, tax-aware outcomes. [[fm-investments|FM Investments]] builds products that exploit this policy space to create tax alpha, turning temporary tax rules into enduring advantages through structures such as the [[dual-share-class|dual share class]] and [[tokenized-t-bills|tokenized T-bills]]. The broader keynote theme is that advisors and investors leave significant tax alpha on the table by focusing narrowly on [[equity-tax-loss-harvesting|equity tax loss harvesting]], while the takeaway is simple: tax alpha matters, and it matters hugely.
Tax alpha is particularly valuable for long-term investors operating on 20–30 year horizons, where temporary tax rules and sunsets create recurring waves of opportunity to engineer structures anew as rules expire and reset. This time dimension transforms episodic tax inefficiencies into a durable advantage—each cycle of legislative sunset provides another occasion to architect new wrappers and unlock fresh tax alpha before the window closes again.
The gap between Wall Street and Washington is a rich field of arbitrage for creating tax alpha, offering repeated opportunities to turn policy inefficiencies into durable portfolio advantages.
[[fm-investments|FM Investments]]' dual share class structures aim to combine ETF trading efficiency with mutual fund access, while tokenization can reduce operational costs, tighten spreads, and enable 24/7 liquidity while preserving or improving tax outcomes.
Tax alpha therefore comes from exploiting policy space rather than relying only on asset selection or market timing. Its distinct source of value lies in tax-aware structure and policy engineering alongside investment decisions.
Intelligent wrappers are a practical response to fragmented and changing tax rules, enabling portfolio design to capture tax and operational advantages while preserving or improving investment outcomes.
[[fm-investments|FM Investments]] pioneered [[dual-share-class|dual share class]] structures that deliver ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers. The antidote is [[investment-wrappers|intelligent wrappers]] and innovation that make the investor always win.
The source frames this claim under the title "Wrappers, Washington, and What's Next: Turning Tax Policy into Portfolio Power," whose subtitle, "Turning Tax Policy into Portfolio Power," is the talk's own shorthand for tax alpha.
Quantitatively, tax alpha denotes the excess return generated solely through proactive tax management, loss harvesting, optimal [[asset-location|asset location]], and lot-level execution relative to a standard pre-tax baseline. Empirical expected ranges span 50–100 bps for ETF and fund overlays, 15–200 bps for [[direct-indexing|direct and custom indexes]], and in excess of 300 bps for leveraged tax-aware long-short portfolios.
Specific operational mechanics for generating structural tax alpha include avoiding dividend distributions, deferring recognition, and strategically rotating around fund ex-dividend dates into proxy ETFs to capture NAV appreciation while shielding investors from taxable ordinary income distributions.
