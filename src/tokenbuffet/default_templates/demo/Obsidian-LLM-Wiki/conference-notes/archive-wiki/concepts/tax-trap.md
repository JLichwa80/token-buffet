## Description
The Tax Trap refers to a systemic wealth management failure in fixed income portfolios caused by neglecting proactive, individualized after-tax optimization. Historically, advisors treat bonds as a static "set it and forget it" allocation driven by career-preservation defaults ("I'm not going to get fired for this"), such as uniformly placing investors into 100% home-state municipal bonds. This passive, risk-averse habit leaves substantial after-tax returns on the table by ignoring dynamic market inefficiencies and relative value across municipal, corporate, and treasury debt. Rather than merely describing tax liabilities, the trap encompasses the compounding missed opportunities of failing to tailor bond selection to an investor's precise tax situation.
