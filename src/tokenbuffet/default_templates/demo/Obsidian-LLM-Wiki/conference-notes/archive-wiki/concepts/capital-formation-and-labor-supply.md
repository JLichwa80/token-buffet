# Capital Formation and Labor Supply
## Definition
Capital Formation and Labor Supply is a macroeconomic analytical framework used to assess the real economic impact of tax policy. Rather than presuming that tax reductions inherently produce growth, this framework evaluates tax legislation by measuring how specific provisions alter marginal incentives for business capital investment (capital formation) and workforce participation (labor supply).
## Key Characteristics
- **Incentive-Based Quantification**: Focuses on marginal tax rates and after-tax returns to determine whether policy changes stimulate productive capacity or merely distribute financial windfalls.
- **Supply-Side Drivers**: Evaluates economic growth through two primary factor inputs: investment in machinery, equipment, and structures (capital formation), and net hours/participation in the workforce (labor supply).
- **Structural vs. Temporary Evaluation**: Emphasizes structural reforms—such as permanent full expensing—over temporary or demand-side rate reductions, analyzing long-run potential output rather than short-term consumption boosts.
- **Empirical Grounding**: Provides an empirical and quantitative counterweight against populist, politically expedient, or revenue-arbitrage tax measures.
## Applications
- **Tax Policy Evaluation**: Serving as the analytical foundation for macroeconomic scoring used by organizations such as the [[Tax Foundation]] to evaluate federal tax packages and reforms.
- **Cost Recovery Modeling**: Assessing the economic viability of cost recovery policies (e.g., bonus depreciation vs. full expensing) on long-term capital intensity and output.
- **Pro-Growth Policy Design**: Formulating tax legislation aimed at sustainably expanding real gross domestic product (GDP) and wage growth by lowering the cost of capital.
