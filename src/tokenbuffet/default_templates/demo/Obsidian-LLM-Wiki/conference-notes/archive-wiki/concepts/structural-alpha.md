## Description
Structural alpha refers to systematic excess return generated through legal, structural, jurisdictional, and tax-efficient portfolio engineering rather than relying solely on manager selection or market timing. In wealth management and concentrated asset transitions, it focuses on maximizing net after-tax wealth capture by eliminating tax drag, such as shielding reinvested assets from ongoing capital gains and ordinary income taxes. A primary implementation engine is [[Private Placement Life Insurance]], which eliminates tax friction during asset accumulation and facilitates tax-free liquidity through policy loans during life or income-tax-free death benefits. Over multi-decade investment horizons, this compound tax insulation produces substantial performance pickups; for instance, investing $10M over 4 years compounding at a 10% gross IRR over 30 years can yield approximately $125M inside an institutional PPLI wrapper versus roughly $50M in a standard taxable account, delivering an annualized net return advantage of approximately 335 basis points.
