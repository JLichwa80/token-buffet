## Description
The "two bad options" dilemma represents the conventional, binary trap confronted by high-net-worth investors holding significant concentrated low-basis assets, such as legacy stock or proceeds from a business sale. Traditional wealth advisory offered only two flawed paths: liquidate the holding and forfeit substantial wealth to immediate capital gains taxes, or "let it ride" by holding indefinitely, thereby absorbing severe, uncompensated idiosyncratic market risk. The dilemma is illustrated by the experience of [[riley]] following the 2006 advice on her concentrated [[wachovia]] position ahead of the 2008 financial crisis. Modern wealth management resolves this dilemma by introducing a tech-enabled third option—such as a [[tax-aware-long-short-equity|tax-advantaged long-short strategy]]—enabling a systematic, [[tax-neutral-transitions|tax-neutral transition]] to de-risk portfolios without triggering massive upfront tax liabilities.
