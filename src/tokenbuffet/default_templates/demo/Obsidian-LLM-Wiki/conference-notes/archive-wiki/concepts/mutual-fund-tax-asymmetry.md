## Description
Mutual fund tax asymmetry refers to the structural tax inefficiency under the 1940 Act and Subchapter M of the Internal Revenue Code, where registered investment companies are mandated to distribute realized capital gains and net investment income to shareholders but cannot pass through capital losses for tax-loss harvesting. This asymmetry forces taxable investors into a structural disadvantage, as taxable distribution events are triggered upon the actual distribution of dividends rather than daily NAV accrual. Investment managers like [[FM Investments]] exploit this policy friction and the gap between daily NAV accrual and 1940 Act distribution mechanics to capture yield while mitigating unwanted taxable distribution events. Through innovations such as dual share class structures combining mutual fund and ETF classes into a single portfolio, asset managers bridge this divide to deliver ETF-level tax and trading efficiency directly to mutual fund investors.
