# tax arbitrage
## Definition
Tax arbitrage is the strategic use of differences between tax rules, market structures, and financial product implementations to improve portfolio outcomes through lawful structures and policy details. It extends beyond simple price discrepancies to the interaction of [[tax-policy|tax policy]], regulation, and financial product design, and is also described as policy arbitrage or tax-policy arbitrage. Misunderstandings and rule complexity between [[wall-street|Wall Street]] and [[washington-state]] can themselves create arbitrage opportunities. The gap between [[wall-street|Wall Street]] and [[washington-state]] is framed as a rich field of opportunity in which [[fm-investments|FM Investments]] turns policy differences into [[tax-alpha|tax alpha]] through product design, wrappers, and long-term portfolio construction.
## Key Characteristics
- Exploits gaps between Wall Street practices and Washington policy.
- Focuses on lawful structural advantages rather than simple tax avoidance.
- Provides a broader strategic framework for [[tax alpha]], [[investment-wrappers]], and [[dual share class]] innovation.
- Requires careful attention to tax policy, product design, and regulatory constraints.
- Designs investment wrappers around fragmented, temporary, and heavily regulated tax policies, especially for investors with 20–30 year horizons.
- Links [[tax alpha]] to product engineering and long-term investment planning, not only to account structures and [[investment-wrappers]].
- Is not limited to price discrepancies and can arise from misunderstandings, rule complexity, tax rules, and implementation details.
- Treats the policy gap as involving not only formal rules but also the misunderstandings, acronyms, and implementation complexity surrounding them — connecting product design with the practical effects of a lengthy tax code and a web of regulatory terminology.
- Emphasizes that complicated or temporary tax rules can be converted into durable portfolio advantages.
## Applications
Tax arbitrage can guide the selection or design of investment products, account structures, ownership arrangements, and portfolio strategies that improve after-tax returns. It may also support the development of tax-efficient wrappers and dual share class structures.
Concrete applications include  AMT considerations, in-kind creation and redemption, and step-up basis planning. Dual share class structures can combine ETF-like trading efficiency with mutual fund access.
Tokenized T-bills may reduce operational costs, tighten spreads, and provide continuous liquidity while preserving or improving tax outcomes.
[[fm-investments|FM Investments]] pioneered dual share class structures that deliver ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers, and uses intelligent wrappers to convert complex or temporary tax rules into durable portfolio advantages. Intelligent wrappers are presented as a practical response to fragmented tax rules, scheduled sunsets, and extensive implementation regulations — the antidote and innovation intended to make the investor always win.
