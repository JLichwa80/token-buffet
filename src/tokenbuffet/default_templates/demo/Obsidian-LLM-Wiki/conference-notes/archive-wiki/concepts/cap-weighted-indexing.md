## Description
Cap-weighted indexing is a portfolio construction methodology where asset weights are proportional to their market capitalization rather than their fundamental business size or economic footprint. By weighting holdings directly by market price, cap weighting behaves as a momentum-driven mechanism that systematically overweights overvalued assets and underweights undervalued ones. This structural mechanic creates macro and sector distortions, such as the late-1980s Japanese asset bubble, the dot-com bubble, and high tech market concentration. Because the approach mechanically buys frothy growth stocks at premium multiples as their market caps rise and sells deep-discount value stocks as their valuations decline, it effectively functions as an active trend-following strategy that buys high and sells low at the margin.
Consequently, selecting a market-cap framework represents an intentional, active design choice rather than an inherently neutral or passive baseline, operating in practice as an active trading strategy embedded within an index mandate.
