## Description
Equitability is the fiduciary and fund accounting principle dictating that all fund shareholders are treated fairly without the structural transfer of tax liabilities between entering, existing, and exiting investors. In fund structuring, aggressive Return of Capital (ROC) distributions compromise equitability: while ROC defers taxes for early redeemers, it allows massive unrealized capital gains to accumulate inside the fund, ultimately leaving the final remaining shareholder "holding the bag" with an outsized tax burden. Upholding equitability guided the structural decision to design funds as taxable rather than relying on aggressive tax-deferral mechanics, ensuring that product architecture does not penalize long-term holders.
