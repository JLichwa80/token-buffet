## Description
The Three P's Lens serves as the primary analytical framework introduced to diagnose and evaluate modern U.S. fiscal policy shifts. Proposed by the Tax Foundation, the framework posits that contemporary tax legislation is increasingly dictated by the convergence of Partisanship (rigid party-line votes and ideological oscillation), Populism (simplistic political slogans such as "no tax on X" or targeted wealth taxes), and Protectionism (tariffs and trade barriers that distort commerce). Operating together, these three political drivers displace rigorous, evidence-based economic modeling, decouple major tax policy from durable bipartisan consensus, and pose significant risks to long-term fiscal sustainability. Evaluating ongoing and emerging fiscal debates through this diagnostic lens directs analysts, planners, businesses, and policymakers to anticipate legislative volatility, determine which underlying forces are driving the debate, and assess whether proposed reforms can provide lasting economic stability.
