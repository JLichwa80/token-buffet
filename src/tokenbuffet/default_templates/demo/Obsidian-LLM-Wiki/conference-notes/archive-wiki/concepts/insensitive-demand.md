## Description
Insensitive demand is the non-price-responsive purchasing behavior of tax-sensitive investors and wealth managers who reflexively acquire home-state municipal bonds to avoid state and local taxation. Because municipal debt issuance is tied to specific capital projects and typically takes 6 to 9 months to come to market, supply remains highly inelastic. This persistent, rigid demand chasing constrained supply inflates in-state bond prices and compresses yields, frequently inverting the market. Consequently, out-of-state national municipal bonds or taxable securities like corporate bonds and Treasurys often generate higher after-tax returns—such as national munis outperforming Massachusetts munis on an after-tax basis for local residents roughly 53% of the time over a 25-year period.
