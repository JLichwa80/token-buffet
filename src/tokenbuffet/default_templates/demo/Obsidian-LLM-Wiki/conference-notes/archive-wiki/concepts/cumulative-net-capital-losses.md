## Description
Cumulative Net Capital Losses (CNCLs) represent the aggregate net realized capital losses generated over an investment horizon, measured as a percentage of initial net asset value (NAV). In tax-aware long-short investing, higher manager skill and pretax alpha substantially steepen the CNCL slope, generating larger cumulative net realized losses and enhanced tax benefits over rolling 15-year horizons as gross leverage amplifies harvesting capacity. This stands in sharp contrast to long-only direct indexing, where higher alpha accelerates portfolio lock-up and diminishes loss-harvesting opportunities as built-in gains accumulate. By turning alpha into an ongoing tax engine through dynamic position creation, CNCL serves as a primary metric for assessing loss-harvesting efficacy alongside pretax return and after-tax wealth metrics.
