## Description

Tax-loss harvesting is the deliberate realization of capital losses so those losses can produce tax benefits, while gains are deferred when possible. Aaron reframed it beyond a mechanical exercise: harvested losses are fuel for tax benefits that, once time-valued and compounded at the portfolio’s pretax return, raise after-tax wealth. In long-only direct indexing the mandate is explicit—a beta-one Russell 1000 portfolio (100 long / 0 short) uses about 1% uninformed tracking error, without an alpha model, to realize losses and defer gains inside that tracking-error budget. Higher skill works the other way in that structure: a higher information ratio and higher pretax alpha raise built-in gains, lock the portfolio up, and shrink harvesting opportunities. In tax-aware long-short portfolios (150/50, 200/100, and 250/150, with about 2%, 4%, and 6% informed tracking error) the same activity is larger, because leverage puts more dollars at work—“more dollars at work means more dollars of losses”—and because alpha-driven under-leverage prompts fresh positions that arrive at full cost basis, while shorts’ negative correlation with the market creates realizable losses in both rising and falling markets. Of the three effects of higher pretax alpha—larger built-in gains (a drag on future tax benefits), a higher NAV (a bigger base to harvest), and position creation (fresh cost basis, unique to long-short)—the net is positive for long-short and negative for long-only, so over long horizons a higher information ratio reduces harvesting in long-only books and increases cumulative net capital losses, tax benefits, and after-tax wealth in long-short books.
Loss harvesting is the continuous practice of realizing losses so they offset realized gains, and the speaker presents it as the operational core of tax alpha. Done deliberately, selling some gains while harvesting losses can net out so that gains are effectively tax-free. Done sloppily, or after a large gain realization, that advantage disappears and can turn into negative tax alpha. The talk says 98 percent of people fall into that negative camp, and that the government thanks them for the carelessness. Harvesting is paired with cash-flow rebalancing: contributions and withdrawals are the preferred moments both to reset weights and to book losses, and the speaker treats the habit as controllable implementation rather than a market view. Simulated magnitudes of about 170 basis points for cap-weighted US large-cap and about 190 for RAFI across 2008–2023 are what this process is claimed to be worth when turnover stays low and gain realization stays controlled.
Speaker 3 said LPL does not have the tax-loss harvesting strategy Rich saw at another firm, and that the engagement should not seek those proprietary details.
In Jamie of Canby Capital's fixed-income keynote, tax-loss harvesting is a primary reason to own bonds directly: mutual funds must distribute capital gains and cannot pass losses through to the investor, so a core harvesting tool stays trapped inside the pool. Separately managed accounts, used by billionaires since the 1950s and 1960s, hold bonds in the client's name and can realize losses against that client's own tax persona. Those losses are useful only when they match the investor's federal, state, and local situation, which a shared ETF or fund NAV cannot do. Harvesting is why the minimum-account line matters: smaller accounts are steered to ETFs, while larger taxable accounts are candidates for an SMA that can harvest, and in Canby's framing daily technology-driven bond selection is what makes that harvesting repeatable across thousands of accounts.
Harvesting losses across dispersed individual securities further allows advisors to build an ongoing tax asset inventory, which facilitates low-cost portfolio transitions and systematically prepares clients for future liquidity events.

The platform performs daily tax-loss harvesting and pairs generated losses with offsetting gains from appreciated assets. In long-short strategies, losses from short positions can help facilitate a tax-neutral sell-down. Tax-loss harvesting is one component of integrated, dynamic, personalized after-tax portfolio management.
