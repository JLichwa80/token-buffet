## Description
The Active Side of Indexing is a theoretical framework and critique showing that market-cap weighted index funds are not entirely passive. While typical low-turnover indices leave roughly 95% of assets untouched in a given year, the remaining marginal ~5% turnover operates as an active momentum strategy that systematically purchases stocks whose prices have risen and liquidates those that have fallen. Introduced and detailed in research published through the CFA Research Foundation, the concept demonstrates that index design is an active investment decision rather than a neutral default, as standard cap-weighting systematically buys frothy growth equities at premium valuations while shedding deep-discount value stocks. Addressing this hidden active trading drag through disciplined rebalancing mechanisms—such as fundamental indexing (RAFI) or equal weighting—allows investors to harness rebalancing alpha while mitigating the severe tax and implementation frictions that erode returns.
