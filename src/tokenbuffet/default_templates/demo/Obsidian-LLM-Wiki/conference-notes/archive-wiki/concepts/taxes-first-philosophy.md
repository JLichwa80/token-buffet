# Taxes-First Philosophy

## Definition

The taxes-first philosophy is [[sam]]'s central argument in the public-markets section of the at this tax conference keynote on [[after-tax-investing]]. Taxes enter at the beginning of the investment process, as tax-adjusted inputs, before an allocation is chosen. The keynote calls this ordering both the philosophical and the practical starting point for everything else in the talk.
## Key Characteristics

- Construction starts from tax-adjusted inputs. A tax wrapper applied after an allocation is chosen is the sequence this philosophy rejects.
- Each asset class is studied for its source of return, income versus appreciation, and for its holding style, active versus passive.
- [[tax-drag]] is computed at the [[capital-market-assumption|Capital Market Assumption]] level before optimization.
- The immediate test for an advisor is whether the construction process starts with taxes.
- [[municipal-bond|municipal-bond]], [[preferred-securities|preferred securities]] that distribute [[qualified-dividend-income]], [[tax-advantaged-private-real-estate|Tax-Advantaged Private Real Estate]], and the [[tax-aware-long-short-equity|Tax-Aware Long-Short Equity]] are presented as downstream of this ordering.
## Applications

The keynote's first action point tells advisors to review at once whether their process puts taxes at the beginning or the end, and to calculate [[tax-drag]] at the [[capital-market-assumption|Capital Market Assumption]] level so portfolios rest on after-tax return expectations. In [[sam]]'s public-markets section, that review is what makes [[municipal-bond|municipal-bond]] the low-risk anchor for high-bracket clients and what supports a sleeve of [[preferred-securities]] whose distributions are largely [[qualified-dividend-income]]. The same starting point is what the rest of the [[after-tax-investing]] keynote builds on: [[tax-advantaged-private-real-estate|Tax-Advantaged Private Real Estate]] structures, then a [[tax-aware-long-short-equity|Tax-Aware Long-Short Equity]] for tax-neutral transitions.
