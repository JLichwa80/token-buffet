## Description
Fundamental Indexing (RAFI) is an index construction methodology introduced in 2004 that weights securities by their [[Economic Footprint|economic footprint]] rather than market capitalization, effectively divorcing portfolio weights from share prices. The strategy relies on four non-price accounting metrics—sales, cash flow, dividends plus buybacks, and book value plus intangibles—to systematically downweight overvalued companies and upweight undervalued companies. By resetting weights to fundamental economic size instead of allowing market prices to dictate portfolio construction, it avoids inheriting sector and country bubbles embedded in capitalization-weighted indexes. Described as having spawned the [[Smart Beta|smart beta revolution]], the approach collects [[Mean Reversion|mean reversion]] and delivers substantial outperformance, generating approximately 2% in [[Fama–French Alpha|Fama–French alpha]] and ~2.8% per year in gross outperformance while retaining roughly 50 bps of excess returns even after all fees, taxes, and post-liquidation.
