## Description
Tax policy is the body of rules and decisions that determines how investment gains, transfers, structures, and transactions are treated. It is fragmented, temporary, and implemented through extensive regulation, including a tax code likened to “Harry Potter-length” legislation and a “spider’s web of acronyms,” with provisions that sunset on schedules and produce effects such as the “sawtooth pattern,” the “race to die,” and the “Cobra effect.” The gap between Wall Street and Washington creates opportunities for arbitrage, allowing portfolio designers to use wrappers, tax alpha, 351/1031 exchanges, AMT rules, step-up basis, dual share classes, proxy funds, and in-kind creation and redemption to improve tax outcomes. FM Investments applies this policy knowledge to build products that preserve or enhance tax efficiency, including dual share class structures that combine ETF trading efficiency with mutual fund access and tokenized T-bills intended to reduce operational costs, tighten spreads, and enable continuous liquidity. The “CPMG/CPHY” framework and the idea that the “ETF [is] not done yet” illustrate how ongoing policy and product innovation can shape long-term portfolio design. The broader objective is to invest for 20–30 years in a changing regulatory environment where intelligent wrappers and innovation help the investor win.
Tax policy can shape portfolio design even when investors are not directly focused on taxes.
FM Investments pioneered dual share class structures that deliver ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers.
