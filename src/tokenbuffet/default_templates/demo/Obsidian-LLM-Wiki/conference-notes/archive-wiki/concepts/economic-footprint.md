## Description
Economic footprint measures a corporation's true scale within the macro economy based on operational and accounting fundamentals rather than market capitalization or share price. Within [[Fundamental Indexing]] (such as Research Affiliates Fundamental Indexation, or RAFI), weighting by fundamental size ensures that portfolio allocations remain anchored to business reality instead of market popularity. This footprint is determined by waiting until a company's business is sufficiently large to matter and measuring it across four metrics: sales adjusted for debt-to-equity ratios, operating cash flow adjusted for R&D expenditures, dividends plus share buybacks, and book value plus intangibles. By remaining substantially steadier than market cap weights, economic footprint avoids exaggerating speculative bubbles, prevents narrative-driven concentration, and provides an objective benchmark against which cap-weighted sector and country distortions become clearly visible.
