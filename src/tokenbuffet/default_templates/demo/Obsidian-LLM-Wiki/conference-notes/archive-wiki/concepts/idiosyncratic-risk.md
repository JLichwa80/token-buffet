## Description
Idiosyncratic risk is the firm-specific, non-systematic risk inherent in holding an individual security or concentrated asset that can otherwise be diversified away across broader markets. In after-tax wealth management, it represents a primary hazard when clients hold concentrated stock, founder equity, or private business interests alongside complex balance sheets. Managing this risk requires balancing quantitative risk reduction against the tax drag of realizing embedded capital gains as well as navigating the client's behavioral attachment to the position. Rather than defaulting to pre-tax model portfolios, advisors address idiosyncratic risk through deliberate after-tax design and multi-year transition roadmaps that integrate tax budgets with risk mitigation.
