## Description
A sunset clause is a statutory provision that establishes an automatic expiration date on specific tax policies unless they are explicitly extended or enacted into permanency by subsequent legislation. Within the [[Tax Cuts and Jobs Act]], key pro-growth provisions—most notably the full deductibility of R&D expenses and immediate capital investment cost recovery—were enacted with sunset dates rather than permanent status. These temporary horizons introduce fiscal uncertainty and diminish the long-term macroeconomic incentives for corporate capital formation, as temporary tax changes fail to stimulate sustained long-term growth. Overcoming sunset provisions requires years of targeted macroeconomic modeling, groundwork, and policy education to socialize the value of permanency before major tax provisions lapse amid partisan and populist pressures.
