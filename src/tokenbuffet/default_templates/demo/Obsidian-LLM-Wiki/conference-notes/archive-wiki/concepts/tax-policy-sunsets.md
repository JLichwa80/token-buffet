# Tax Policy Sunsets
## Description
Sunset provisions are policy rules designed to expire or change on a scheduled date unless they are extended or replaced. Scheduled sunsets are a defining feature of the tax environment for long-term investors, who may plan across 20–30 years while tax policy is written in fragments and implemented through millions of words of regulation. They are imposed through draconian parliamentary procedures and typically come with one year’s notice, which is not enough time for a full re-architecture. This creates uncertainty because investment strategies may depend on temporary rules rather than permanent ones.
Because the benefits of temporary tax provisions depend on existing legislation and may not persist, managing them requires active monitoring of legislative changes and scheduled sunsets. This dynamic favors adaptable portfolio structures over strategies dependent on permanent rules. The gap between Wall Street and [[washington-state]] creates opportunities for [[tax-arbitrage|tax policy arbitrage]], allowing product designers to build [[intelligent-wrappers|intelligent wrappers]] and other structures that remain useful as individual tax rules change.
[[fm-investments|FM Investments]] applies this approach to create [[tax-alpha|tax alpha]], turning complicated and temporary tax rules into enduring portfolio advantages. For example, FM Investments pioneered [[dual-share-class|dual share class]] structures that deliver [[etf-trading-efficiency|ETF trading efficiency]] to mutual fund buyers, and is pursuing [[tokenization|tokenization]] to collapse operational costs, tighten spreads, and unlock 24/7 liquidity while preserving or improving tax outcomes. These product innovations illustrate how intelligent wrappers can convert temporary tax rules and sunset provisions into lasting portfolio value.
