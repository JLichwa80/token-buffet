## Description
Under-leverage is the mathematical condition in a tax-aware long-short portfolio where successful pretax alpha generation causes net asset value (NAV) to expand faster than gross market exposure, causing effective leverage ratios to fall beneath target mandates (such as dropping from 200/100 to 186% long / 86% short). Unlike market drift, which leaves leverage ratios largely static, manager alpha drives longs to appreciate and short liabilities to shrink, directly lowering gross debt-to-equity exposure. This compression functions as the primary operational catalyst forcing rebalancing transactions that trigger fresh position creation. Consequently, under-leverage acts as a key transmission mechanism through which higher pretax alpha establishes new cost-basis lots, systematically driving cumulative net capital loss harvesting rather than locking up the portfolio with built-in gains.
