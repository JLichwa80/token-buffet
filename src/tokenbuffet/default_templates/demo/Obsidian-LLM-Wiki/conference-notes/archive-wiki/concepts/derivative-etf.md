## Description
Derivative ETFs (or Derivative-Based ETFs) are registered 1940 Act exchange-traded funds that utilize options, swaps, and futures contracts to achieve tailored payoff profiles, downside buffers, or enhanced distribution yields. By packaging options-based and synthetic strategies directly inside a regulated fund envelope, asset managers eliminate direct options trading friction, account authorization limits, and operational compliance hurdles for financial advisors. This structure enables scalable access to outcome-oriented investing and portfolio hedging without requiring bespoke derivatives documentation. However, these vehicles require heightened operational scrutiny regarding single-counterparty pricing risks, return-of-capital distribution reporting, and path-dependent oscillation decay in choppy markets.
