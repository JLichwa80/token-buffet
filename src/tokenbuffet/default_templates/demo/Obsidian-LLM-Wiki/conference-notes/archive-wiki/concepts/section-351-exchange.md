## Description
A Section 351 exchange is a long-standing provision of the U.S. Internal Revenue Code enacted in 1954 that allows investors to transfer property—specifically appreciated exchange-traded stocks and ETFs—into a corporation (such as an ETF) in exchange for its shares without triggering immediate capital gains recognition. In wealth management, it provides an operational, tax-deferred path to transition complex, "ossified" taxable portfolios accumulated over decades into diversified broad-market ETFs, treating received ETF shares effectively as receipts for the contributed assets. Qualification is strictly evaluated at the household level, requiring the contributor's largest position to be no more than 25% of contributed assets and the top five positions to comprise no more than 50%. The strategy is restricted to U.S. persons contributing qualifying equities and ETFs (evaluated on a look-through basis of their underlying holdings), expressly excluding mutual funds, crypto, commodities, government securities, and cash. Upon contribution, custodians map over tax lots so that the original cost basis and acquisition dates carry forward into the newly issued ETF shares.
