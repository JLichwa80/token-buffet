## Description
Pretax alpha represents the excess return generated by an active manager relative to a benchmark prior to deducting taxes, fees, and transaction costs. In tax-aware long-short investing, it acts as a dual-action mechanism: beyond increasing compounding capital, it functions as a primary [[Tax Engine]] that systematically drives tax efficiency. While market return by itself does not result in [[Position Creation]], active alpha alters gross exposure ratios and triggers rebalancing, which continuously replenishes the loss-harvesting fuel needed to build [[Cumulative Net Capital Losses]]. A sufficiently high pretax alpha is also essential to overcome the drag imposed by short financing, borrow fees, and the overall [[Cost Stack]].
In traditional long-only frameworks, high pretax alpha typically suppresses tax-loss harvesting by accumulating built-in capital gains. Conversely, in long-short structures, it functions as an operational tax engine by inducing portfolio under-leverage and prompting [[Position Creation]] to drive continuous loss harvesting.
