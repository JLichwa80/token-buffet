## Description
Rolling 15-year simulations are a multi-decade empirical framework used to evaluate portfolio tax mechanics, compounding wealth, and manager skill across distinct historical market cycles. The simulation methodology spans 21 overlapping 15-year holding horizons beginning in January 1989, designed to provide regime-independent findings that resolve a literature gap regarding the tax consequences of pretax alpha in long-short strategies. The framework tests long-only direct indexing against three long-short leverage archetypes (150/50, 200/100, and 250/150) across varying levels of informed tracking error and manager information ratios. By testing over extended horizons rather than isolated single market paths, the simulations demonstrate that long-short alpha acts as a tax engine—amplifying position creation and loss harvesting while avoiding the built-in gain lockup seen in long-only portfolios.
