## Core Content

This keynote frames [[after-tax-investing|after-tax investing]] as a portfolio-construction discipline: tax effects should shape assumptions and allocations from the start. [[sam|Sam]] discusses tax-adjusted capital market assumptions, municipal bonds, and preferred securities. [[casey|Casey]] presents private real estate approaches, including REITs and exchange structures, as potential tools for income, tax deferral, and moving from active property management to passive ownership. [[taylor|Taylor]] describes technology-enabled long-short strategies for transitioning concentrated holdings or liquidity-event proceeds toward a target allocation. The speakers also emphasize planning how to reduce leverage after an initial tax-offset period. These are strategies and claims as presented in the source; the summary does not independently verify their tax treatment, performance, or suitability.

## Main Points

- Incorporate taxes into capital market assumptions and portfolio construction from the outset.
- The keynote presents municipal bonds and preferred securities as possible income tools for tax-sensitive investors.
- Private real estate structures and exchanges are discussed as ways to pursue income, defer taxes, or transition toward passive ownership.
- Long-short strategies are presented as tools for managing concentrated holdings and gains from liquidity events while shifting toward a target allocation.
- Model a path to reduce leverage as part of implementing these strategies.
