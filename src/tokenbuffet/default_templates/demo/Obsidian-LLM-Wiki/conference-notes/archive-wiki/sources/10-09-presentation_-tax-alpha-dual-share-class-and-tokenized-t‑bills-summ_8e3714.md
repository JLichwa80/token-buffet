## Core Content
This presentation by [[fm-investments|fm-investments]], titled "Wrappers, Washington, and What’s Next: Turning Tax Policy into Portfolio Power," explores how asset managers can capitalize on the persistent friction between regulatory policy crafted by [[congress|congress]], federal regulators, and financial engineering. Rather than relying solely on asset allocation or market timing, the presentation introduces [[washington-designed-your-portfolio|washington-designed-your-portfolio]], asserting that statutory compromises and sunset provisions create arbitrageable gaps that can generate substantial [[tax-alpha|tax-alpha]].
The core framework evaluates modern [[investment-wrappers|investment-wrappers]], focusing on the pioneering [[dual-share-class|dual-share-class-structure]] inspired by [[vanguard|vanguard]] and sanctioned by the [[sec|sec]], which unites traditional [[mutual-funds|mutual-funds]] like [[tbmax|tbmax]] and ETFs under a single NAV. Additionally, the presentation details income-avoidance rotation strategies in fixed-income funds like [[cpmgcphy|cpmg]] and [[cpmgcphy|cphy]]. By exploiting [[mutual-fund-tax-asymmetry|1940-act-distribution-rules]] through temporary rotations into a [[proxy-fund|proxy-fund]] around ex-dividend dates, managers can flatten the traditional [[sawtooth-pattern|sawtooth-pattern]] into smooth capital gains. Finally, the discussion outlines the institutional advantages of [[tokenized-t-bills|tokenized-t-bills]], which eliminate operational layers to provide sub-penny spreads, streamline middle-office operations, and unlock continuous 24/7 liquidity for [[treasury|treasury]] debt instruments.

## Main Points
- Tax policy is structurally fragmented, temporary, and slow to reform, creating persistent opportunities for financial policy arbitrage.
- Investment wrappers dictate tax liabilities, liquidity, and distribution rules to a greater degree than underlying asset selection alone.
- Dual share class structures combine mutual funds and ETFs into a single portfolio, providing mutual fund holders with ETF tax efficiency, identical NAV performance, and shared tax-advantaged redemption channels.
- Bond dividend-avoidance strategies (such as CPMG and CPHY) capture daily interest accrual in NAV while rotating into proxy funds during ex-dates to eliminate taxable ordinary income distributions.
- Tokenization of Treasury bills enables spread compression beneath penny thresholds, streamlines middle-office operations, eliminates administrative layers, and unlocks continuous 24/7 liquidity for cash management.
