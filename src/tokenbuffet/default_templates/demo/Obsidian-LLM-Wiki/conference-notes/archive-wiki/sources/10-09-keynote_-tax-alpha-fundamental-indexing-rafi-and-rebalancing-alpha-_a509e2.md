## Core Content
This keynote presentation addresses the reality that most investment outperformance evaporates once management fees, ongoing taxes, and post-liquidation taxes are deducted. Traditional [[smart-beta|smart-beta]] and factor-tilting strategies frequently underperform after taxes, exposing the critical necessity of [[after-tax-benchmarking|after-tax-benchmarking]] and minimizing [[tax-drag|tax-drag]]. Investment vehicle structure plays a decisive role: while a traditional [[mutual-funds|mutual fund]] suffers significant tax friction due to forced capital gains distributions, exchange-traded vehicles offer substantial tax efficiency. 
The speaker critiques conventional [[cap-weighted-indexing|cap-weighted indexing]], demonstrating through [[active-side-of-indexing|the active side of indexing]] framework that market-cap weighting functions as an active momentum strategy that routinely buys overvalued assets and sells discounted ones. To resolve this, the presentation advocates for [[fundamental-indexing|fundamental indexing]] (RAFI), which sizes companies by their accounting [[economic-footprint|economic footprint]] rather than market price. By systematically breaking the link between price and weight, both fundamental indexing and equal-weighting strategies capture durable [[rebalancing-alpha|rebalancing alpha]] through [[rebalancing-alpha|contrarian rebalancing]] and [[mean-reversion|mean-reversion]]. Furthermore, utilizing [[cash-flow-rebalancing|cash-flow rebalancing]] and disciplined loss harvesting generates meaningful [[tax-alpha|tax alpha]], enabling investors to preserve net portfolio returns.

## Main Points
- Most gross alpha and factor premiums fail to survive real-world frictions, namely management fees, ongoing taxes, and terminal liquidation costs.
- A realistic hurdle for taxable investors is the benchmark minus roughly 170 basis points, reflecting standard tax and implementation drag.
- Structural vehicle choice is paramount: mutual funds lagged the benchmark by ~3% after taxes in cited historical data, whereas ETFs lagged by only ~40 basis points.
- Cap-weighted indexing is not purely passive; its marginal turnover acts actively by systematically purchasing overpriced growth stocks and dumping discounted value stocks.
- Fundamental Indexing (RAFI) decouples portfolio weights from market prices by sizing companies according to their economic footprint across four core accounting metrics.
- Sizing portfolios independently of price avoids the correlation trap and harvests [[rebalancing-alpha|rebalancing alpha]] driven by contrarian rebalancing and mean reversion.
- Low-turnover strategies executing disciplined tax-loss harvesting and [[cash-flow-rebalancing|cash-flow rebalancing]] can generate ~170 to 190 basis points of sustainable [[tax-alpha|tax alpha]].
- When evaluated after fees, RAFI and cap-weighted indexing may produce comparable tax alpha alone, meaning true alpha (such as the ~2% Fama–French-relative alpha) serves as the primary performance differentiator.
