## Core Content
This keynote address examines the increasingly volatile landscape of United States tax policy, driven by the intersecting forces of [[three-ps-lens|The Three P's of Modern Tax Policy]]: [[partisanship|Partisanship]], [[populism|Populism]], and [[protectionism|Protectionism]]. Presented by a representative of the [[tax-foundation|Tax Foundation]] following opening remarks from [[alex|Morgan]], the talk highlights how non-partisan macroeconomic modeling historically guided pro-growth legislation, such as the [[tax-cuts-and-jobs-act|Tax Cuts and Jobs Act]]. Leaders like [[paul-ryan|Paul Ryan]], [[kevin-brady|Kevin Brady]], and [[steve-daines|Steve Daines]] engaged with think-tank research—carrying findings directly to the [[white-house|White House]]—to secure [[permanency|permanency]] for capital expensing and avoid temporary growth gimmicks. However, declining legislative tax expertise combined with populist measures like the "no tax on" movement and [[wealth-tax|wealth tax]] initiatives drives [[shrinking-tax-base|shrinking tax base dynamics]]. With major provisions facing an impending [[sunset-clause|sunset clause]] and federal interest consuming 20% of income tax revenues, the presentation emphasizes that [[pro-growth-reform|pro-growth reform]] targeting [[capital-formation|capital formation]] and [[labor-supply|labor supply]], alongside vigilance over [[state-level-tax-experiments|state-level tax experiments]], is vital to avert long-term fiscal instability.

## Main Points
- Modern US tax policy is increasingly dictated by the "Three P's"—Partisanship, Populism, and Protectionism—rather than sound economic analysis.
- Multi-year intellectual groundwork and macroeconomic modeling are crucial for guiding tax legislation through fast-tracked, truncated congressional sessions.
- Temporary tax rate reductions fail to foster sustained economic growth; long-term growth requires permanent expensing for research and development and capital investments.
- Bipartisan populist tendencies, spanning "no tax on" exemptions to wealth taxes, erode the federal revenue base and concentrate 71% of the income tax burden onto the top 20% of earners.
- Federal debt service now absorbs one out of every five income tax dollars, risking a fiscal doom loop if tax policy cannot preserve structural revenue and promote growth.
