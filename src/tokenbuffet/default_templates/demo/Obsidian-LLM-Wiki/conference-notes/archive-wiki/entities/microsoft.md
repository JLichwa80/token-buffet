Microsoft is a global technology company cited in the presentation as an example of a high-quality taxable corporate debt issuer. The speaker notes that investing in a taxable Microsoft bond or a Treasury bill and paying income tax can frequently provide a superior net after-tax return compared to holding lower-yielding, supply-constrained home-state municipal bonds. This counterintuitive dynamic underscores the necessity of dynamic cross-asset class optimization rather than dogmatically defaulting to municipal paper.
> "The limited and slow issuance of municipal bonds, which are tied to specific projects, creates supply constraints that can make other taxed assets, like a Microsoft bond or T-bill, a more optimal and profitable choice for a client even after taxes are paid." — [[wiki/sources/at this tax conference-keynote-the-tax-trap-in-bond-investing_ae0375]]
