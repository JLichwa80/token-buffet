Bill Sharpe is a Nobel Memorial Prize-winning economist famous for the Capital Asset Pricing Model and the concept of active management arithmetic. In the source, his arithmetic of active management is invoked by Rob Arnott to illustrate that if index funds own the market, remaining active managers collectively replicate the market before expenses. Consequently, fees and taxes inevitably drive aggregate active management below benchmark returns.
> "He tied this to Bill Sharpe’s arithmetic of active management: if index funds own the market, the remaining active managers collectively also own the market, before costs." — [[wiki/sources/at this tax conference-keynote_-after-tax-wealth-management-and-tax-stack-design-summary-1_b17566]]
