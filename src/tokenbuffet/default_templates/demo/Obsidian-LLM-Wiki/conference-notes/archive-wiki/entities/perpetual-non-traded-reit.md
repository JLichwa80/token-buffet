## Description

Perpetual non-traded REITs are private real estate vehicles used in after-tax portfolio construction for high-net-worth clients. The keynote describes two tax advantages: investors may deduct 20% of eligible dividends through the [[Qualified Business Income Deduction]], and depreciation pass-through may cause part of distributions to be treated as [[Return of Capital]]. That return-of-capital treatment can defer ordinary income taxes until sale, although it reduces the investor's cost basis and may affect the eventual gain calculation. The structure is also presented as part of a broader private real estate toolkit alongside [[Section 1031 Exchange]] planning, [[Delaware Statutory Trust]] ownership, and [[upreit]] transitions. In that sequence, the perpetual non-traded REIT can become the eventual destination after a DST holding period in an upREIT or 721 exchange structure.
As unlisted real estate investment trusts, these vehicles offer retail and accredited investors ongoing exposure to private commercial real estate alongside periodic liquidity features. Notably, the 20% [[Qualified Business Income Deduction|QBI deduction]] available on eligible REIT dividends operates as a permanent provision within the tax code.

After moving into the REIT from a DST structure, investors may have monthly liquidity of up to 2% of NAV.
