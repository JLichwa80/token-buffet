## Description
Wall Street represents the institutional financial services and investment industry, focused on markets, trading efficiency, liquidity, spreads, and implementation. It is contrasted with [[washington-state]] as the financial-market side of the relationship between policy design and portfolio execution. The distance between Wall Street and Washington is presented as a source of misunderstanding and a fertile field for arbitrage. In this context, Wall Street is connected to wrappers, ETFs, mutual funds, tokenization, and portfolio engineering, including products developed by [[FM Investments]] to create tax alpha through policy-aware design.
[[FM Investments]] pioneered dual share class structures that provide ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers while preserving favorable tax outcomes. Tokenization can reduce operational costs, tighten spreads, and enable continuous liquidity for Treasury bill exposure while preserving or improving tax outcomes. Wall Street product innovation responds to fragmented and temporary tax rules over a 20 to 30 year investment horizon by turning policy complexity into durable portfolio advantages.
