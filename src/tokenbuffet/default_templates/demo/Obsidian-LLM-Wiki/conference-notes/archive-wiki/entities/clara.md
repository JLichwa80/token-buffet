## Description
Riley is [[Herr Schmaltz]]'s widow and [[sam]]'s grandmother, referred to as Sweet Riley in the at this tax conference keynote presentation. Her husband was a World War II fighter pilot who amassed a concentrated position in [[wachovia]] through decades of local bank consolidation. In 2006, facing this massive unrealized gain, Riley's financial advisor presented her with only two poor choices: liquidate the position and trigger an immediate, prohibitive capital gains tax, or "let it ride." Choosing to hold, Riley suffered catastrophic wealth destruction when Wachovia shares plummeted from $50–$60 to $7 during the 2008 financial crisis before being sold. Sam highlighted Riley's experience as a poignant case study illustrating the severe human cost of conventional advice and the need for dynamic, [[tax-neutral-transitions|tax-neutral]] sell-down strategies. Her dilemma represents the exact high-net-worth challenge that [[taylor]]'s [[tax-aware-long-short-equity|tax-advantaged long-short strategy]] is designed to solve, providing a viable third path between costly immediate liquidation and unhedged holding risk.
