## Description
Mutual funds are pooled investment vehicles whose buyers generally access underlying assets through fund shares rather than continuous exchange trading. The presentation places the mutual fund market at approximately $31 trillion and presents mutual funds as an investor-access format that can receive ETF-like trading efficiency through dual share class structures. FM Investments pioneered structures designed to deliver this efficiency to mutual fund buyers and is pursuing tokenization to reduce operational costs, tighten spreads, enable 24/7 liquidity, and preserve or improve tax outcomes. This product architecture connects mutual funds with wrappers, tax alpha, in-kind creation and redemption, and related tax concepts such as 351/1031 exchanges, AMT, and step-up basis. The source does not discuss a particular mutual fund or provider beyond its discussion of FM Investments, and places mutual funds within a broader landscape that includes rising CIT adoption in retirement accounts and emerging tokenized shares.
A mutual fund pools investor assets into a professionally managed portfolio. FM Investments builds products that exploit the policy space between Wall Street innovation and fragmented tax rules to create tax alpha, turning complicated and temporary tax rules into enduring portfolio advantages.
For taxable investors, mutual funds can generate significant structural tax drag because portfolio rebalancing, redemptions, and forced capital gains distributions may trigger taxable events regardless of whether an individual investor sold shares or had an unrealized loss. Furthermore, legacy mutual fund holdings with substantial embedded gains often result in an "ossified portfolio"—assets that are not underperforming enough to justify selling, yet carry too high of a tax burden to easily reallocate without a structured transition budget.
Quantitatively, mutual funds trail benchmark indices by roughly 30 basis points annually on a pre-tax basis, but their structural tax inefficiency widens this gap to approximately 300 basis points (~3%) after taxes and roughly 350 basis points (~3.5%) post-liquidation as a result of cash redemptions and forced taxable distributions.
