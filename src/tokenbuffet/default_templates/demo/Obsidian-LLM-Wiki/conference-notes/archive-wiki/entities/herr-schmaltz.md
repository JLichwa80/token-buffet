## Description

Herr Schmaltz is [[sam]]'s grandfather and a World War II fighter pilot whose legacy forms the cautionary historical narrative anchor in the at this tax conference keynote on after-tax investing. He originally accumulated a substantial equity stake in a local bank that evolved through decades of regional and national bank consolidation into a massive, concentrated, and highly appreciated holding in [[wachovia]]. Following his passing, the legacy holding was left to his widow [[riley]] without an active tax-risk management framework in place. In 2006, Riley opted to retain the shares rather than liquidate and incur a severe capital-gains tax liability; the stock, once trading between $50 and $60, subsequently collapsed and was liquidated at $7 during the 2008 financial crisis. [[sam]] shares this story to demonstrate the dangers of wealth concentration lacking a tax-efficient exit and to critique traditional planning before presenting a [[tax-neutral-transitions|Tax-Neutral Transitions]] as a superior alternative to "two bad options."
