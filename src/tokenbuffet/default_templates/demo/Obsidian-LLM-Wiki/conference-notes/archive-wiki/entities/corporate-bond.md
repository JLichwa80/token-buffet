## Description
Corporate bonds are taxable debt obligations issued by private and public corporations to finance operations, capital expenditures, or strategic acquisitions. Although interest earned on corporate debt is fully taxable across federal, state, and local jurisdictions, high-yielding corporate issues can generate superior after-tax returns compared to tax-exempt municipal bonds. Due to structural supply constraints in the municipal market—where project-linked issuance takes months to materialize—inelastic investor demand often inflates muni valuations, creating opportunities where taxed corporate assets (such as Microsoft bonds) yield a higher net return for high-net-worth investors. Consequently, corporate bonds serve as a vital instrument in active, cross-asset class tax-optimization strategies within fixed-income management.
