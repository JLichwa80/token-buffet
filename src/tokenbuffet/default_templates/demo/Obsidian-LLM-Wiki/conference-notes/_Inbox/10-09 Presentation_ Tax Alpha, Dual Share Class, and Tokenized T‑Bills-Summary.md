# at this tax conference Presentation: Tax Alpha, Dual Share Class, and Tokenized T‑Bills
## 1. Main Theme & Core Message
• Title: “Wrappers, Washington, and What’s Next: Turning Tax Policy into Portfolio Power”  
• Key terminology: wrappers, tax alpha, dual share class, 351/1031, AMT, tokenized T-bills, in-kind creation/redemption, step-up basis, “CPMG/CPHY”, “proxy fund”, “sawtooth pattern”  
• Catchphrases/metaphors: “Wall Street vs. Washington is 230 miles and an infinite number of misunderstandings,” “Washington designed your portfolio,” “Harry Potter-length tax code,” “the spider’s web of acronyms,” “race to die,” “Cobra effect,” “ETF not done yet”
Core message: The gap between Wall Street and Washington is a rich field of arbitrage. FM Investments builds products that exploit that policy space to create tax alpha—turning complicated, often temporary tax rules into enduring portfolio advantages. The firm pioneered dual share class structures that deliver ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers and is pushing forward with tokenization to collapse operational costs, tighten spreads, and unlock 24/7 liquidity—all while preserving or improving tax outcomes. The mission is simple: invest for 20–30 years in a world where tax policy is written in fragments, sunsets on schedules, and gets implemented through millions of words of regulation. The antidote is intelligent wrappers and innovation that make the investor always win.
Why it matters now: As of 2026-at this tax conference, tax policy remains volatile, incrementally updated, and rarely reformed in a durable way. Most planning done today may need revision within years. That uncertainty is an opportunity for products and methods that convert regulatory complexity into practical, permanent efficiencies—reducing taxes, operational friction, and trading costs. FM’s approach reframes the tax code as a toolkit, not a constraint, offering investors a path past perverse incentives toward stable, efficient wealth compounding.

## 2. Structured Breakdown (Chapters & Subthemes)
1) “Why Washington Matters More Than We Admit”
- Core idea: Tax policy is not made with investor precision. It’s a fragmented, fast-changing process involving multiple players, and our industry spends more time on implications than Congress does.
- Importance: Recognizing how policy is made helps us design wrappers and strategies that harvest tax alpha.
- Arguments: Congress allocates little bandwidth to capital gains policy; 230 miles separate Wall Street and Washington—and an infinite number of misunderstandings; macro thinkers monetize this gap.
- Engagement: Humor about being brought to Seattle—“as far away as possible from the capital”—to discuss Washington.
2) “Who We Are: FM Investments in the Policy Engine Room”
- Core idea: DC proximity and background in diplomacy/government give FM Investments an edge translating policy to product.
- Products: Treasury ETFs across the yield curve (“T-Bill through thirty”) with tax-efficient, high-trading efficiency access; first Vanguard-approved/issued dual share class concept.
- Importance: Dual share class brings ETF efficiency to mutual fund buyers; mutual funds and ETFs can share a single portfolio, same NAV, same rights.
- Example: T-Bill and TBMax two-line chart overlapping on total return—proving equivalent investor experience.
3) “How Policy Gets Made: The Long Road from Bill to Basis”
- Core idea: Policy formation is complicated, slow, and often temporary; significant reform hasn’t happened since the mid-1980s.
- Players & percentages:
  - 40%: 30 career government professionals architect the technical tax scaffolding.
  - 30%: Elected politicians (Ways & Means and staff) answer and shape the narrative.
  - 20%: Lobbyists/special interests—“that’s us in this room.”
  - 10%: Think tanks (e.g., Tax Foundation).
- Constraints: Sunsets via draconian parliamentary procedures; typically one year’s notice—not enough for full re-architecture.
- Anecdote: Treasury converts laws to forms and code—“Spoiler alert: they don’t always.”
4) “The Burden of Acronyms and the Reality of Sunsets”
- Core idea: A spider’s web of acronyms (351, 1031, AMT, etc.) creates perverse incentives and constant change.
- Importance: Planning must anticipate sunsets and policy churn; industry should seek permanence.
- Engagement: Meme about the acronym spider web; accountant year-end struggles.
5) “Votes, Districts, and the Backseat Status of Taxes”
- Core idea: Representation arises from uneven vote distributions; taxes rarely top the political agenda.
- Data points: ~200,000 votes typically send someone to Congress; only 18 districts often determine control; low US voter turnout vs. global standards.
- Assertions: Carried interest “elimination” is a perennial nonstarter; ETF in-kind creation/redemption attacks are DOA.
6) “Washington Designed Your Portfolio (Accidentally)”
- Core idea: The tax code and its regulations shape investment wrappers and behavior.
- Visualization: Tax code length comparable to the Harry Potter series; regulations are millions of words—7.7 million cited—that reps now literally feed to ChatGPT to parse for constituents.
- Importance: Scale of complexity demands productized intelligence.
7) “Cobra Effects and AMT: Good Intentions, Twisted Outcomes”
- Core idea: Policy can backfire—like paying for rat tails producing more rats; AMT targeted 155 wealthy families, now hits more everyday taxpayers.
- Insight: Government responds personally when cash flow is off; long-term data (Rognlie’s “Missing Billionaires”) shows original targets largely vanished.
8) “The Wrapper Game: Mutual Funds, ETFs, SMAs, CITs, Hedge Funds, Tokenized Shares”
- Core idea: Legal containers distribute assets, responsibilities, taxes; who can buy and how taxes flow separates these.
- Market sizes: Mutual funds ~$31T; ETFs ~$14T; CITs rising in retirement; tokenized shares emerging.
- Thesis: We must master wrappers while building the bridge to post-wrapper innovation.
9) “Tax Alpha: Converting Investment Alpha via Wrappers”
- Core idea: Wrappers convert investment alpha into holdable outcomes—and can be engineered for tax alpha.
- Dual share class breakthrough: One portfolio, same NAV/rights, investor always wins; operational and tax efficiencies converge.
10) “Tokenization: Cutting Basis Points and Breaking the Penny”
- Core idea: Tokenizing T-bills and other assets removes counterparties and operational layers, improves NAV efficiency, and enables fractional spreads and 24/7 trading.
- Policy tailwind: Executive order recently pushed SEC/CFTC to immediately create tokenization schemes; FM filing a second application “tomorrow.”
11) “CPMG/CPHY: Income Without Income”
- Core idea: Deliver total return without ordinary income by exploiting 1940 Act distribution rules and ETF accounting.
- Method: In bonds (e.g., AGG, high yield), NAV accrues daily income, but tax impact only occurs on receipt of dividends. Strategy avoids dividend capture, rotates into proxy funds around ex-dates, then reverses—collecting price appreciation while minimizing distributions.
- Outcomes: No 1099 capital gains distribution; ordinary income avoided; hold for 366 days to get offsettable long-term capital gains; roll costs ≈ 1 bp/month; fee add-on ≈ 25 bps; treasury and BDC versions coming within weeks/months.
- Analogy: From “sawtooth” dividend pattern to smoothed total return.
12) “What’s Next: ETFs Not Done Yet”
- Core idea: ETFs will be tokenized, mutual-fund-synchronized, and solve for income; wrappers have a “last good gasp” while we build beyond them.
- Call to action: Focus on well-run strategies; consume innovation that saves taxes and delivers better outcomes.
13) Q&A Highlights
- Tokenized T-bill savings:
  - Operational cost: ETFs at scale cost ~3–6 bps; tokenization reduces this, reflected in NAV.
  - Trading: Break the penny spread; 24/7 trading; spreads drop from basis points to fractions.
- CPMG mechanics:
  - Modern “dividend avoidance”: Use proxy ETF during ex-date window; return after two days; avoid ordinary income distributions; keep NAV accrual.
  - Cost/efficiency: ~1 bp/month roll cost; no distributed cap gains in the year; convert gains to long-term if held 366 days.

  - Learning: Wrappers convert investment alpha into holdable, tax-aware outcomes. Master them to generate tax alpha.

  - Foundation: Vanguard-approved, single portfolio with multiple wrappers; same NAV and rights.
  - Learning: Delivers ETF trading efficiency to mutual fund buyers and unifies investor experience across wrappers.
- Policy Formation Map
  - Framework: 40% career technocrats, 30% elected politicians, 20% lobbyists, 10% think tanks.
  - Learning: Sunsets and incrementalism dominate; expect one year of notice for changes.
- Acronym Spider Web (351, 1031, AMT, in-kind mechanisms)
  - Insight: Rules were not written together, creating perverse incentives and arbitrage opportunities.
  - Learning: Build strategies that exploit inconsistencies rather than fight them.
- Cobra Effect in Tax Policy
  - Metaphor: Incentives backfire (rat tails → more rats), like AMT harming unintended populations.
  - Learning: Evaluate second-order effects; don’t assume policy fixes are permanent or targeted.
- “Washington Designed Your Portfolio”
  - Reframe: The tax code and its regulations dictate portfolio behavior more than investment theory.
  - Learning: Translate regulation into product architecture that favors investors.
- Tokenization of T-Bills and Beyond
  - Model: Reduce counterparties and operational layers; tighten spreads; enable 24/7 liquidity; reflect savings in NAV.
  - Data: Traditional ETF cost at scale ~3–6 bps; tokenization targets fractions of that in trading spreads.
- CPMG/CPHY Dividend-Avoidance Strategy
  - Mechanism: Exploit 1940 Act distribution rules—taxable only upon receiving dividends—while NAV accrues income daily.
  - Steps:
    1) Hold bond ETF to accrue income in NAV.
    2) Rotate into a proxy ETF before ex-date to avoid dividend receipt.
    3) Reverse trade post ex-date; continue accrual without ordinary income distribution.
  - Outcomes: No 1099 capital gains distribution; ordinary income avoided; long-term cap gains if held 366 days; roll cost ≈ 1 bp/month; add-on fee ≈ 25 bps.
