# Value tilt

## Definition

Value tilt refers to the systematic tendency of fundamental indexing strategies to overweight relatively inexpensive companies and underweight expensive ones because portfolio weights are determined by fundamental economic footprint rather than market price. It represents a dynamic valuation posture that is conceptually distinct from the rebalancing alpha earned when that exposure is systematically reset.

## Key Characteristics

- **Dynamic Exposure**: The magnitude of the tilt fluctuates over time; fundamental index multiples can trade close to the broad market or at steep valuation discounts (reaching discounts as deep as ~60% during extreme market bubbles like the dot-com era).
- **Predictive Outperformance**: Historically, the size of the valuation discount correlates with subsequent excess returns; larger discounts across US, international, and emerging markets have preceded larger five-year value added.
- **Distinction from Growth**: Decouples price from business expansion by rejecting the premise that expensive companies are synonymous with growth companies.
- **Separation from Rebalancing Alpha**: Merely holding a static tilt toward cheap assets does not account for the additional alpha generated by periodic rebalancing back to fundamental metrics.

## Applications

- **Systematic Portfolio Construction**: Designing non-cap-weighted strategies that capture excess returns from mispriced securities without taking on uncompensated active manager risk.
- **Valuation Timing & Horizon Forecasting**: Assessing multi-year expected outperformance based on the prevailing spread or discount between a fundamental index and cap-weighted benchmarks.
- **Disciplined Contrarian Rebalancing**: Providing a rules-based mechanism to reduce exposure to overvalued market darlings and reallocate toward undervalued firms.
