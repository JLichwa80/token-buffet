# Tax Stack

## Definition

The tax stack is an after-tax implementation platform and operational framework designed to sit alongside an advisor's conventional technology stack (such as CRM, planning, reporting, custody, and portfolio management systems). It shifts wealth management focus from pure pre-tax performance and reporting toward comprehensive after-tax execution across the entire household.

## Key Characteristics

- **Comprehensive Scope**: Spans multiple wealth management layers including the [[Household Balance Sheet]], tax-status awareness, asset-class placement, asset location, transition management, concentrated-stock management, liquidity-event planning, and ongoing governance.
- **Implementation-Centric**: Focuses heavily on the execution layer rather than reporting alone, driving value through [[Lot-Level Implementation]], loss harvesting, and pairing capital losses against capital gains.
- **Customization & Direct Indexing**: Employs [[direct-indexing]] to build and manage personalized portfolios tailored to existing client holdings, legacy positions, and bespoke restriction mandates.
- **Mitigation of Friction**: Designed specifically to systematically reduce [[Tax Drag]] across client portfolios and accounts.

## Applications

- **Systematic Tax Loss Harvesting**: Actively scanning portfolios to harvest capital losses at the tax-lot level and offset real-world capital gains.
- **Transition Management**: Transitioning legacy client portfolios into target allocations over time with controlled tax budgets, avoiding sudden realization events.
- **Concentrated Stock Unwinding**: Structuring phased disposition programs for concentrated executive or equity holdings alongside offsetting harvesting strategies.
- **Holistic [[Tax Design]]**: Coordinating asset location and asset placement decisions across taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-exempt accounts on the [[Household Balance Sheet]].
