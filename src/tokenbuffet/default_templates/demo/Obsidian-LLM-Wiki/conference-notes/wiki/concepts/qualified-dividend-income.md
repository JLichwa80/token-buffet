# Qualified Dividend Income

## Definition

Qualified Dividend Income (QDI) refers to ordinary dividends meeting specific tax criteria that allow them to be taxed at the lower long-term capital gains tax rate (0% to 20%) rather than ordinary income rates. Within preferred securities and fixed income strategies, it serves as a classification demonstrating that different yield sources carry distinct after-tax consequences.

## Key Characteristics

- **Favorable Tax Rates**: Taxed at preferential capital gains rates ranging from 0% to 20%, rather than at higher federal ordinary income tax rates.
- **Preferreds Universe Distribution**: Roughly half of the preferred securities universe distributes income classified as QDI.
- **Taxable-Equivalent Yield Enhancement**: Portfolio managers can actively tilt preferred securities allocations toward QDI to boost taxable-equivalent yield for investors in high tax brackets.
- **Distinct from QBI**: QDI is distinct and separate from Qualified Business Income (QBI) deductions, which apply to pass-through entities and non-traded REIT dividends.

## Applications

- **Municipal Bond Portfolio Augmentation**: Allocating approximately 15% to 20% to QDI-focused preferred securities within a municipal bond portfolio can enhance taxable-equivalent yield without taking on unwarranted additional risk.
- **High-Bracket Tax-Advantaged Yield**: Structuring fixed-income and equity-income sleeves specifically to maximize QDI distributions to optimize after-tax returns for high-net-worth clients.
