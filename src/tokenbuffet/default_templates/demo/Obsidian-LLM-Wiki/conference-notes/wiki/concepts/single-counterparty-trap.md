# Single Counterparty Trap

## Definition

The Single Counterparty Trap refers to the operational and pricing risk incurred when a derivative fund or structured strategy executes trades exclusively with a single institutional counterparty. Without competitive dealer tension, the sole counterparty lacks economic incentives to provide fair pricing, frequently resulting in severe markups and excessive extracted spreads at the strategy's expense.

## Key Characteristics

- **Absence of Competitive Bidding**: Execution without multi-dealer quotes removes price discovery and competitive discipline.
- **Excessive Spread Extraction**: Counterparties can capture oversized dealer margins ("extra juice") due to captive flow.
- **Asymmetric Information and Power**: The sole institutional dealer commands significant pricing leverage over the fund manager.
- **Structural Vulnerability**: Reliance on a single institution introduces heightened exposure to counterparty solvency and execution quality.

## Applications

- **Derivative Strategy Due Diligence**: Evaluated by investment advisors and portfolio managers to assess whether structured notes or derivative ETFs rely on competitive panels versus bilateral sole-dealer relationships.
- **Execution Quality Analysis**: Used to monitor transaction costs, bid-ask spreads, and potential fee drag within complex options-based strategies.
- **Fund Risk Governance**: Applied in fund design to mandate multi-counterparty execution frameworks and competitive dealer bidding protocols.
