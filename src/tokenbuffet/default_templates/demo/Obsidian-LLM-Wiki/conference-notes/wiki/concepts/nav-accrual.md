## Description

NAV accrual is an accounting mechanism in fixed-income funds and bond ETFs where earned interest and coupon payments automatically increase the fund's net asset value on a daily basis prior to any cash distribution. Under the Investment Company Act of 1940, taxable liabilities for shareholders only materialize upon the actual receipt of dividend distributions rather than as income accrues within the fund. This structural divergence separates continuous capital return from immediate taxable events, enabling strategies such as rotational proxy trading and dividend avoidance to compound capital untaxed at the portfolio level. By actively trading around distribution dates, investment managers exploit this mechanism to generate tax alpha from the predictable price appreciation and subsequent ex-dividend adjustments across fixed-income instruments.
