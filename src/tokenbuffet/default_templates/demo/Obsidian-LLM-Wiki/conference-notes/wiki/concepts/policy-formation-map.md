# Policy Formation Map

The Policy Formation Map is the speaker’s working model of who actually writes tax outcomes. About 40 percent is assigned to roughly thirty career government professionals who architect the technical scaffolding; about 30 percent to elected politicians, specifically Ways & Means and staff, who answer and shape the narrative; about 20 percent to lobbyists and special interests, which the speaker identifies with the room; and about 10 percent to think tanks such as the Tax Foundation. The map is meant to explain why policy is slow, fragmented, and usually temporary: significant reform has not happened since the mid-1980s, parliamentary sunsets dominate, and a typical change arrives with about one year of notice. Treasury’s conversion of statutes into forms and code is offered as a caution that implementation does not always follow the bill. The learning the talk wants is practical. Do not bet on a clean reform; design wrappers that harvest tax alpha inside a process owned more by technocrats, narrative, and incremental procedure than by investor precision.
