# Asset location

## Definition

Asset location, in this panel, is the decision of which strategies belong inside private placement life insurance (PPLI) rather than in a taxable account. It places income-heavy and high-turnover holdings where ongoing tax character is deferred during accumulation, with tax-free policy loans available during life and a tax-free distribution at death. It complements tools that address monetization and gain recognition, because it changes the tax treatment of the portfolio that remains invested.

## Key Characteristics

- The stated ideal holdings are income-heavy private credit, high-turnover funds that realize short-term gains, and public strategies that would otherwise distribute taxable income when markets are turbulent.
- PPLI is described as solving the back half of the tax problem: tax is deferred while the policy accumulates, and distribution at death can be tax-free.
- The case study illustrates a $10 million investment at 10 percent over 30 years growing to about $125 million inside the policy versus about $50 million in a taxable account, a roughly 335 basis point annualized IRR uplift.
- Implementation is to move those tax-inefficient strategies into PPLI so the uplift is captured as structural alpha.
- Location addresses the tax character of the ongoing portfolio, while the variable prepaid forward and the loss-harvesting long-short address monetization and gain recognition.
- Low-turnover, loss-harvesting, or benchmarked equity strategies remain in taxable SMAs to systematically generate usable capital losses, contrasting with the high-turnover and income-heavy assets placed inside PPLI.
## Applications

- Advisors reallocate income-heavy private credit and high-turnover funds out of taxable accounts and into PPLI.
- Public strategies that would distribute taxable income in turbulent markets are located inside the policy to avoid those distributions.
- The placement is used alongside a variable prepaid forward and a tax-aware long-short so concentrated, low-basis assets can be monetized and diversified while the destination portfolio compounds with less tax drag.
- Planning discussions use the 30-year illustration to show the compounded gap between policy and taxable outcomes on the same pretax return.
