# rebalancing tax drag

## Definition

**Rebalancing tax drag** refers to the reduction in net investment performance and wealth accumulation caused by realizing taxable capital gains and incurring transaction costs when adjusting a portfolio's asset allocation back to its target weights.

## Key Characteristics

- **Performance Drag**: In conventional sleeve-based portfolio management, frequent asset realignment is estimated to cost an average portfolio approximately 24 basis points (bps) per year in lost performance.
- **Realization Friction**: Driven primarily by the involuntary realization of short- or long-term taxable capital gains when selling appreciated assets to buy underperforming ones.
- **Mitigation via Cash Flows**: Utilizing natural incoming cash flows—such as contributions, dividends, interest, and coupon payments—can reduce tax drag by approximately two-thirds, down to roughly 8 basis points annually.
- **Compounding Effect**: Over extended investment horizons, even small basis-point reductions in tax drag compound significantly into higher total after-tax wealth.

## Applications

- **Tax-Aware Wealth Management**: Assessing portfolio turnover frequency and trade scheduling to minimize unnecessary capital gains realization.
- **Direct Indexing and Multi-Asset Portfolios**: Evaluating the trade-off between target tracking error and realized tax liability across non-qualified taxable accounts.
- **Cash-Flow Allocation**: Implementing soft rebalancing protocols by routing natural liquidity and cash inflows into underweight positions instead of initiating outright asset sales.
