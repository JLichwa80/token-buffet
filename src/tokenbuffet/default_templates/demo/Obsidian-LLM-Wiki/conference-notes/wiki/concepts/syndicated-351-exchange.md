# Syndicated 351 Exchange

## Definition

A **syndicated 351 exchange** is an aggregated contribution structure open to multiple independent wealth advisors and their clients, pooling appreciated securities into a single broad-market destination exchange-traded fund (ETF). Unlike closed Section 351 structures created exclusively for a single firm's proprietary ETF, syndicated 351 exchanges aggregate capital across multiple advisory practices to collectively satisfy statutory diversification thresholds and fund launch scale requirements without triggering immediate capital gains taxes.

## Key Characteristics

- **Multi-Firm Aggregation**: Pools client portfolios from distinct, unaffiliated advisory firms rather than relying on the single-firm asset base of a closed contribution.
- **Collective Diversification Thresholds**: Enables participating investors to collectively meet statutory diversification criteria (e.g., the 25%/50% test) by aggregating complementary or offsetting single-stock concentrations into a broad destination fund.
- **Tax-Deferred Transition**: Employs IRC Section 351 nonrecognition rules, allowing investors to exchange low-basis or highly appreciated stock positions for ETF shares without incurring immediate capital gains tax.
- **Economies of Scale**: Lowers the barrier to entry for independent registered investment advisors (RIAs) who lack the individual assets under management required to seed and support a dedicated private ETF.

## Applications

- **Concentrated Stock Diversification**: Assisting clients holding concentrated legacy equities (e.g., from executive compensation, founder stock, or inherited positions) in transitioning to a diversified asset allocation without incurring a tax penalty.
- **RIA Practice Scalability**: Allowing mid-sized or independent RIA practices to offer custom ETF contribution mechanisms to their high-net-worth clients by joining a broader syndication group.
- **Tax-Aware Portfolio Restructuring**: Reallocating taxable accounts into liquid, tax-efficient ETF wrappers such as [[AAUB]] while managing household-level tax budgets.
