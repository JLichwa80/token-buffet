## Description

Cumulative Net Capital Losses (CNCLs) is an empirical performance metric defined as the total net realized capital losses generated over an investment period divided by the portfolio's initial net asset value (NAV). In tax-aware investing, the metric quantifies the volume of harvested tax losses available to defer taxes or offset external capital gains. While manager skill in long-only portfolios increases built-in gains and locks up portfolios—causing CNCLs to decline with higher alpha—the metric behaves conversely in tax-aware long-short portfolios. Over multi-year horizons, a higher Information Ratio (IR) sharply increases CNCLs in long-short strategies, with the slope steepening as leverage increases. This occurs because skilled long-short management accelerates position creation by rendering portfolios under-levered, introducing fresh cost basis alongside the negatively correlated loss-harvesting opportunities inherent to short positions.
