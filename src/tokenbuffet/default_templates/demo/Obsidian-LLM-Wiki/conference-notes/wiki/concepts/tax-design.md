# Tax Design

## Definition

Tax design is a deliberate, proactive, and engineered wealth management methodology focused on minimizing [[Tax Drag]] and optimizing family-level wealth across the entirety of a [[Household Balance Sheet]]. Contrasted with traditional investment approaches that rely on rigid capital-market models, standard product allocations, and periodic rebalancing, tax design views wealth management as an ongoing architectural process centered on maximizing [[After-Tax Outcomes]].

## Key Characteristics

- **Proactive & Engineered**: Moves away from default, one-size-fits-all allocations toward deliberate, tailored structuring.
- **Household-Level Scope**: Coordinates across the complete [[Household Balance Sheet]] rather than evaluating individual accounts or products in isolation.
- **Continuous Monitoring & Automation**: Integrates ongoing oversight and automated execution to capture tax-saving opportunities dynamically.
- **Lot-Level Precision**: Implements strategies at the individual tax-lot level to optimize gain and loss harvesting.
- **Outcome-Oriented Metrics**: Measures success by net after-tax return and preserved wealth rather than pre-tax benchmark performance.
- **Architectural Role**: Positions the advisor as an after-tax architect who aligns risk tolerance, loss utilization, liability timing, and balance sheet evolution with client goals.

## Applications

- **After-Tax Wealth Planning**: Structuring comprehensive multi-account investment portfolios to systematically mitigate [[Tax Drag]].
- **Loss Utilization & Gain Management**: Actively orchestrating capital losses to offset targeted gains and tax liabilities across the family portfolio.
- **Tax Stack Engineering**: Deploying an integrated [[Tax Stack]] to coordinate tax location, asset location, and withdrawal sequences.
- **Transition Management**: Implementing tax-lot level transitions for concentrated stock or legacy positions while minimizing realized taxable gains.
