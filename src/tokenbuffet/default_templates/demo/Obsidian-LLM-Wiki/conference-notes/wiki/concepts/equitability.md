# Equitability

## Definition

Equitability is the fund management principle of maintaining structural fairness and non-discriminatory treatment among all shareholders in an investment vehicle. It governs the structural and ethical balance between entering, holding, and exiting investors, ensuring that no single cohort benefits at the expense of another through disproportionate cost shifting or liability concentration.

## Key Characteristics

- **Cohort Impartiality**: Mandates equal and non-discriminatory treatment between early redeemers, lingering long-term holders, and incoming participants.
- **Fair Liability Distribution**: Prevents the asymmetric concentration of unrealized tax liabilities, liquidity costs, or transactional drag onto remaining shareholders.
- **Structural Integrity**: Constrains fund managers from utilizing accounting maneuvers that present illusory short-term yields while impairing long-term capital preservation.

## Applications

- **Distribution Governance**: Monitoring and structuring distributions to avoid excessive return of capital maneuvers that deplete fund basis and disadvantage remaining shareholders.
- **Redemption & Transition Structuring**: Implementing anti-dilution mechanisms, in-kind redemptions, or swing pricing to ensure exiting investors internalize their fair share of transaction and tax costs.
- **Fund Structure Auditing**: Evaluating ETF and closed-end fund mechanisms to guarantee structural equity across distinct market cycles and turnover events.
