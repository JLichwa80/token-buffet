# Whole Portfolio Unification

## Definition

Whole portfolio unification is an overarching wealth management philosophy and framework that eliminates operational and structural silos across public markets, private assets, and specialized tax overlay strategies. By harmonizing disparate vehicles—such as direct indexing, municipal fixed income, private real estate, and long-short strategies—under a single dynamic allocation model with standardized tax-adjusted Capital Market Assumptions (CMAs), it optimizes total compounding after-tax alpha across the entire investor balance sheet.

## Key Characteristics

- **De-Siloed Architecture**: Integrates traditionally segregated sleeves (public equities, fixed income, alternatives, and private assets) into a unified portfolio structure rather than managing them as disconnected product allocations.
- **Tax-Adjusted Capital Market Assumptions (CMAs)**: Standardizes asset class return, risk, and correlation expectations on an after-tax basis to establish consistent hurdle rates across diverse asset types.
- **Holistic After-Tax Optimization**: Coordinates automated loss-harvesting, asset location, and gain realization across multiple sub-strategies to maximize net compounding wealth.
- **Technology-Driven Execution & Rebalancing**: Employs centralized risk monitoring, reporting, and systematic rebalancing engines to coordinate multi-asset operations in real time.

## Applications

- **Holistic Wealth Management**: Structuring complex high-net-worth (HNW) and ultra-high-net-worth (UHNW) accounts across taxable, tax-deferred, and non-taxable entity structures to minimize comprehensive tax drag.
- **Multi-Strategy Asset Allocation**: Coordinating direct index loss harvesting with gain recognition in private assets or alternative investments to offset liabilities dynamically.
- **Transition and Migration Management**: Facilitating transitions of legacy low-basis assets into diversified multi-asset portfolios with zero or controlled net capital gains exposure.
