# Pro-Growth Reform

## Definition

Pro-growth reform is a tax policy standard evaluated through macroeconomic modeling, requiring that proposed tax changes demonstrably increase long-term capital formation and labor supply rather than serving as generalized tax reductions or political campaign slogans.

## Key Characteristics

- **Macroeconomic Validation**: Requires quantitative economic modeling demonstrating positive impacts on long-term capital formation and labor supply to qualify as pro-growth.
- **Structural Permanency**: Rejects temporary tax relief or short-term gimmicks, establishing that policies—such as corporate rate reductions—must be permanent to stimulate meaningful, sustained economic growth.
- **High Return on Investment**: Prioritizes "most bang for the buck" measures, such as the full and permanent expensing/deductibility of capital investment and research and development (R&D).
- **Distinction from Populist Rate Cuts**: Explicitly separates principled structural reforms aimed at systemic expansion from politically motivated, broad-based rate cuts tailored for campaign messaging.

## Applications

- **Tax Policy Design & Legislative Negotiation**: Used by policymakers and legislative leaders to draft and defend tax overhauls (e.g., the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act) by demanding permanent corporate rate adjustments and full expensing over short-term gimmicks.
- **Economic Evaluation of Legislation**: Applied by economic research organizations and fiscal watchdogs to objectively score proposed tax bills based on their potential for long-term growth versus temporary deficit expansion.
- **Client Advisory and Wealth Strategy**: Employed by financial and tax advisers to help clients distinguish between durable, structural tax changes and short-lived tax provisions when planning long-term capital investments.
