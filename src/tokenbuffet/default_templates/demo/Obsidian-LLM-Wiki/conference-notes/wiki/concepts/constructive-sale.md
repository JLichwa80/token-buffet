# Constructive Sale

## Definition

A constructive sale is the tax event the playbook is built to avoid when a client monetizes or hedges a concentrated position. In this discussion it is the constraint that separates an acceptable hedge from a taxable disposition: raising cash or reducing risk can still be structured so it does not create a sale for tax purposes. The panel pairs that constraint with a second friction, ongoing tax drag in whatever portfolio is bought with the proceeds.

Formally governed by statutory provisions such as IRC Section 1259, a constructive sale occurs when a taxpayer substantially eliminates both the risk of loss and the opportunity for gain on an appreciated financial position, causing it to be treated as sold for tax purposes.
## Key Characteristics

- It is the tax treatment the integrated approach is designed to avoid while a client monetizes a concentrated, appreciated position.
- It is named together with ongoing tax drag in the reinvested portfolio as one of two frictions the playbook addresses at once.
- The term is used as a planning constraint. The panel does not restate a statutory test; it marks the line between a hedge that is acceptable and a disposition that is taxable.
- A [[Variable Prepaid Forward]] is presented as monetization that does not create a sale for tax purposes, including an advance on the order of ninety cents on the dollar.
- The client bucket labeled “would sell but for taxes” is told to use a non-constructive-sale hedge and deploy the cash into long-short.
- When both constructive-sale risk and volatility matter, a variable prepaid forward is preferred over a plain financing trade that does not hedge the position.
- Structuring derivative hedges like VPFs or equity collars requires maintaining an adequate price band between put and call strikes to avoid triggering an immediate taxable event under the constructive-sale rules.
## Applications

- Monetize a low-basis concentrated holding with a [[Variable Prepaid Forward]] so the client receives cash upfront while the structure is described as avoiding a sale for tax purposes, then redeploy that cash into tax-aware long-short.
- For a client who would sell but for taxes, put on a non-constructive-sale hedge and use the unlocked cash to fund long-short, matching the hedge to the [[Client Typology for Strategy Selection]] bucket.
- Choose the instrument when comparing a [[Variable Prepaid Forward]], a [[Collar]], and a [[Box spread]]: a box spread is treated as efficient fixed-rate borrowing that generates capital losses when a hedge is unnecessary; a variable prepaid forward is preferred when the position is volatile and constructive-sale risk has to be managed.
- Keep collar design inside the same constraint. The panel treats ordinary collars as exposed to [[Straddle Rules]] and points to a single fused OTC collar, mechanically close to a variable prepaid forward, when tax efficiency matters.
