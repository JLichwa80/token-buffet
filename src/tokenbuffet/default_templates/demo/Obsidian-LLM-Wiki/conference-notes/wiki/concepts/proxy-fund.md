## Description

After-tax portfolio construction is the discipline of combining and managing investment vehicles and wrappers so the investor keeps more of the return once taxes are paid. Beyond static allocations across mutual funds, autocallable ETFs, equities, and Treasury debt, the practice systematically exploits legislative friction and wrapper mechanics to generate tax alpha. Investors can dynamically rotate out of dividend-yielding funds into highly correlated proxy funds (such as replacing [[AGG]] during ex-dividend dates) to maintain market beta while avoiding ordinary income distributions. Structuring portfolios around innovations like dual share classes and tokenized instruments allows portfolios to monetize the persistent gaps and sunsetting provisions in federal tax policy.

The useful comparison is the amount the owner retains after tax.

## Key Characteristics

- Vehicle and wrapper sit together. An open-end mutual fund and an autocallable-notes ETF can both hold market exposure, and each wrapper follows its own tax path.
- Asset mix includes listed stocks and government bonds. Equities and Treasury bonds can share one allocation while their income and gain profiles stay distinct.
- Federal statute is part of the design. The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017, also called the TCJA, is the tax legislation this wiki names as a concrete change in that backdrop.
- Firms and legislators belong in the same map. AllianceBernstein manages assets; Paul Ryan served as Speaker of the House; Steve Daines is the Montana senator tied to that legislative record.
