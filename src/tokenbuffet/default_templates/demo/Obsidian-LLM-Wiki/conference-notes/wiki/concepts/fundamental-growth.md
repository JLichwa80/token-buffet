# Fundamental Growth

## Definition

Fundamental Growth is an investment portfolio construction methodology that selects companies based on their actual business growth rates rather than valuation multiples (i.e., avoiding the conventional definition of growth as merely "expensive stocks") and weights those companies by the absolute dollar magnitude of their growth. This dual approach aims to provide systematic growth exposure while breaking the concentration and valuation drag inherent in cap-weighted benchmarks.

## Key Characteristics

- **Growth-Rate-Based Selection**: Screens and selects constituents using realized historical growth metrics rather than market pricing or price-to-multiples ratios.
- **Dollar-Magnitude Weighting**: Sizes positions proportionally to the total dollar volume of growth generated, severing the link to stock price and market capitalization.
- **Avoidance of the Correlation Trap**: Directly addresses the failure mode where fundamental stock selection is subsequently weighted by market cap, which often produces a portfolio with ~99.9% correlation to standard cap-weighted indices.
- **Symmetric RAFI Counterpart**: Serves as the growth-side complement to [[RAFI]] fundamental footprint weighting by modifying both the selection criteria and the weighting mechanism simultaneously.

## Applications

- **Growth Portfolio Construction**: Building core equity growth allocations that do not disproportionately concentrate capital in the most expensive mega-cap securities.
- **Active and Smart Beta Benchmarking**: Designing style-pure growth indices that prevent price bubbles from dominating index weights.
- **Risk and Factor Diversification**: Providing factor exposure that captures business expansion without inheriting the unintended momentum and valuation risks of standard cap-weighted growth indices.
