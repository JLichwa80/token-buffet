# After-tax benchmarking

## Definition

After-tax benchmarking is an investment evaluation framework that measures portfolio performance net of all management fees, operational costs, ongoing tax liabilities, and final liquidation taxes, comparing the outcome against an adjusted hurdle rather than a conventional pre-tax gross index.

## Key Characteristics

- **Comprehensive Net Return Accounting**: Evaluates performance net of management fees, annual taxable distributions, and the embedded capital gains tax realized upon total liquidation.
- **Realistic Hurdle Adjustment**: Acknowledges that beating a standard pre-tax index on a fully net liquidated basis is extremely difficult; it adopts a practical performance target (such as benchmark return minus ~170 basis points to account for typical drag).
- **Vehicle Sensitivity**: Highlights the structural impact of investment vehicles on after-tax outcomes, illustrating how vehicle mechanics (such as mutual fund distributions vs. ETF tax efficiencies) alter net performance hurdles.
- **Smart Beta Scrutiny**: Assesses whether the gross alpha generated by alternative indexing and factor strategies survives the sequential frictions of advisory fees and turnover-induced tax realization.

## Applications

- **Performance Evaluation for Taxable Accounts**: Establishing realistic scoreboards for taxable high-net-worth investors rather than relying on gross-of-tax index comparisons.
- **Vehicle Selection Analysis**: Comparing the relative after-tax, after-liquidation efficiency of mutual funds, ETFs, and direct indexing wrappers.
- **Strategy Feasibility Assessment**: Quantifying whether excess returns from factor-tilted strategies or smart beta survive after factoring in taxes and transaction drag.
