# Capital Market Assumptions

## Definition

Capital Market Assumptions (CMAs) are long-term forecasts of risk, expected return, and correlation across various asset classes, functioning as foundational inputs for strategic asset allocation and mean-variance optimization. In private wealth contexts, traditional CMAs require adjustment for tax drag at the asset class level to ensure expected returns reflect net after-tax outcomes rather than misleading pre-tax metrics.

## Key Characteristics

- **Long-Term Orientation**: Provides multi-year forward-looking estimates rather than short-term market timing signals.
- **Foundational Modeling Inputs**: Establishes expected return, volatility, and correlation matrices for mean-variance portfolio construction.
- **Tax Sensitivity in Private Wealth**: Standard pre-tax CMAs can distort asset allocation for taxable investors if they fail to incorporate income composition (ordinary income, qualified dividends, capital gains) and holding duration.
- **Tax-Adjusted Return Metrics**: Under tax-aware frameworks, CMAs are adjusted prior to optimization to account for asset-specific tax drag and the impact of compounding liabilities.

## Applications

- **Strategic Asset Allocation**: Defining benchmark asset class targets aligned with investor risk-return objectives.
- **Mean-Variance Optimization (MVO)**: Serving as core mathematical inputs to generate efficient frontiers.
- **Tax-Aware Portfolio Construction**: Modeling asset-level expected after-tax returns to determine optimal asset location, holding strategies, and tax-neutral transition budgets.
- **Scenario Analysis & Stress Testing**: Evaluating long-term portfolio resilience against structural shifts in inflation, interest rates, and macro returns.
