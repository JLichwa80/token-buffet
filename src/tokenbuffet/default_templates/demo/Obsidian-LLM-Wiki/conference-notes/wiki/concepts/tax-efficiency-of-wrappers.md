# Tax efficiency of wrappers

## Definition

The tax efficiency of wrappers refers to the extent to which the legal structure or vehicle (such as an ETF vs. a traditional mutual fund) holding an underlying economic exposure minimizes tax drag and prevents the premature distribution of realized taxable capital gains to investors, thereby altering after-tax performance compared to pre-tax tracking differences.

## Key Characteristics

- **Structural Tax Drag Disparity**: Legal wrappers holding identical underlying assets can yield significantly divergent after-tax returns; pre-tax tracking differences are often dwarfed by after-tax distributions.
- **Capital Gain Distribution Shielding**: More tax-efficient vehicles (such as ETFs utilizing in-kind creation and redemption mechanisms) limit distributed realized capital gains compared to conventional open-end mutual funds.
- **Persistent Post-Liquidation Gap**: Even after accounting for eventual liquidation taxes, structural tax efficiency maintains a substantial net performance advantage over less tax-efficient alternatives.
- **Scoreboard Metric**: Evaluates investment efficacy based on after-tax, post-liquidation returns rather than purely pre-tax benchmark tracking.

## Applications

- **Vehicle Selection for Taxable Accounts**: Prioritizing ETFs or other tax-advantaged wrappers over mutual funds when implementing equivalent passive or factor strategies in non-registered or taxable portfolios.
- **After-Tax Performance Attribution**: Measuring and presenting portfolio manager performance using after-tax and post-liquidation metrics to reflect true investor net wealth creation.
- **Portfolio Implementation Design**: Structuring underlying strategies to complement the wrapper mechanics, ensuring asset location and realization events align with vehicle tax strengths.
