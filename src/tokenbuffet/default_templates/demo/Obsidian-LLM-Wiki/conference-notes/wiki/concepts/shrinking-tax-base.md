# Shrinking Tax Base

## Definition

A shrinking tax base describes the progressive narrowing of the pool of taxable income and taxpayers within the federal tax system. Driven by bipartisan populist policies, targeted carve-outs, and exemptions, it removes broad segments of the population and income categories from the tax rolls, heavily concentrating the fiscal burden onto a small cohort of high-income earners and corporations.

## Key Characteristics

- **Bipartisan Carve-Outs and Exemptions**: Narrowed through targeted popular relief proposals, such as exemptions for tips, overtime pay, and senior income.
- **Extreme Burden Concentration**: Relies on a highly concentrated tax distribution where the top 20 percent of earners pay 71 percent of income taxes, while the bottom 60 percent account for only 11 percent.
- **Fiscal Instability**: Elevates structural fiscal fragility by relying on volatile high-bracket revenues while excluding stable, broad-based revenue mechanisms.
- **Political Entrenchment**: Accompanied by persistent political resistance against broad, durable taxation, leaving few viable mechanisms to close deficits.
- **Doom Loop Component**: Acts as an aggravating input to structural fiscal distress, compounding debt service costs, rising interest burdens, and growing deficits.

## Applications

- **Tax Policy and Reform Analysis**: Used by fiscal analysts to evaluate the trade-offs between politically popular exemptions and long-term budgetary solvency.
- **Macroeconomic Risk Assessment**: Applied in macroeconomic forecasting to assess systemic revenue stability and vulnerability to economic downturns when tax receipts depend predominantly on high-earner windfalls.
- **Legislative and Deficit Modeling**: Informs fiscal policy simulations regarding the sustainability of entitlement funding and federal borrowing trajectories.
