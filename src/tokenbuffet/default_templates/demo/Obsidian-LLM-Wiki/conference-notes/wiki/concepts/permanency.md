# Permanency

## Definition

Permanency is the deliberate legislative and fiscal policy design principle that establishes tax incentives and cost recovery rules without expiration or sunset dates, providing the regulatory certainty necessary to alter corporate investment behavior and drive sustained long-term economic growth.

## Key Characteristics

- **Long-Term Capital Allocation Driver**: Shifts corporate decision-making from timing-based arbitrage toward structural, multi-year investments by guaranteeing stable return horizons.
- **Behavioral Impact over Temporary Cuts**: Avoids the limitations of temporary or sunsetting tax reductions, which provide short-term liquidity windfalls without stimulating lasting economic expansion.
- **Focus on High-Return Cost Recovery**: Prioritizes structural durability for foundational growth provisions, particularly the full expensing of research and development (R&D) and capital expenditures.
- **Bipartisan Policy Stability**: Relies on cross-party consensus and strategic legislative negotiation to protect core business incentives from being used as expiring revenue-raising offsets.

## Applications

- **Strategic Advisory**: Distinguishing between structural permanent provisions and temporary statutory incentives when structuring client capital outlays and multi-year tax planning.
- **Capital Expenditure and R&D Planning**: Committing major capital to long-lead innovation and facility development under predictable cost-recovery rules.
- **Legislative and Coalition Advocacy**: Championing permanent expensing over short-term tax cuts during fiscal policy negotiations to secure enduring economic frameworks.
