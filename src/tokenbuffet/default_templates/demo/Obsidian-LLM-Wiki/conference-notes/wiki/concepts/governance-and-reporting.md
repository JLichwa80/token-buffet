# Governance and Reporting

## Definition

Governance and reporting is the operational and communication framework in tax-aware wealth management that tracks, validates, and articulates implementation decisions. It translates portfolio mechanics—such as transition management, lot-level trading, and budget allocation—into transparent roadmaps and structured client discussions, converting technical after-tax execution from a black box into deliberate strategy.

## Key Characteristics

- **Decision Transparency**: Exposes the explicit trade-offs of portfolio transitions, including the tax cost of moving, the risk of staying, the velocity of change (moving too fast vs. too slowly), and taxes paid upfront versus deferred long-term benefits.
- **Coordination Mechanism**: Uses quantitative budgets (gain, risk, liquidity, and time) as collaborative tools to align the advisor, CPA, estate attorney, legal counsel, investment team, custodian, and platform.
- **Integration Across the Tax Stack**: Sits alongside household planning, lot-level tracking, and transition roadmaps to ensure tax-aware strategies remain intentional and verifiable over time.
- **Client Alignment**: Provides a structured roadmap that ensures clients understand that portfolio changes and realization events are deliberate rather than arbitrary.

## Applications

- **Portfolio Transitions**: Structuring multi-period portfolio reallocations with explicit tax and risk tracking, demonstrating why positions are transitioned over specific time horizons.
- **Professional Team Alignment**: Serving as the central dashboard and conversational baseline during joint reviews with external accountants, estate planners, and legal advisors.
- **Ongoing Tax Budgeting**: Monitoring realization limits and tracking progress against predetermined gain, liquidity, and risk budgets throughout the tax year.
