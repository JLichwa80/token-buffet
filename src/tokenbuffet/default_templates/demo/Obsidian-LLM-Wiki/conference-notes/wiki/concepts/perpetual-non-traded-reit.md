# Perpetual Non-Traded REIT

## Definition

A perpetual non-traded REIT is a private real estate investment trust structure designed to hold stabilized, income-generating commercial real estate indefinitely without public market exchange listing. It provides investors with tax-advantaged income distributions, limited regular liquidity, and a destination vehicle for tax-deferred real estate rollover strategies.

## Key Characteristics

- **Tax-Advantaged Cash Flow**: Investors may qualify for the 20% Qualified Business Income (QBI) deduction on regular dividend distributions.
- **Return of Capital Treatment**: Embedded depreciation can reclassify a portion of distributions as [[Return of Capital]], deferring ordinary income taxation until the final sale of shares and converting the deferred amount into long-term capital gains at the cost of a reduced cost basis.
- **Limited Periodic Liquidity**: Operates with structural redemption limits, typically offering periodic liquidity caps (such as monthly redemptions up to 2% of Net Asset Value (NAV)).
- **Passive Management**: Serves as a mechanism to preserve steady real estate income exposure while relinquishing active property management obligations.
- **Estate Planning Utility**: Enables fractional transfer of operating partnership units or shares to heirs with potential stepped-up basis advantages.

## Applications

- **Tax-Neutral Exit / Destination Vehicle**: Acts as the ultimate endpoint in an [[upREIT]] strategy, enabling property owners transitioning out of a [[1031 Exchange]] into a Delaware Statutory Trust (DST) to contribute units into the REIT without triggering immediate capital gain realization.
- **Tax-Aware Income Generation**: Employed by high-net-worth investors seeking passive income that benefits from pass-through depreciation and QBI deductions in private commercial real estate markets.
