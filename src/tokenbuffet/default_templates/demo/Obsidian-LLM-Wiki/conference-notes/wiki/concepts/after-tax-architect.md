# After-Tax Architect

## Definition

The after-tax architect is an advisory operating role in which the wealth manager coordinates a household’s tax, legal, and portfolio work instead of stopping at generic product selection. The advisor sits at the center of the client’s CPA, estate counsel, legal advisors, and implementation engines, and turns an explicit tax design into a client-specific roadmap bounded by gain, risk, liquidity, and time budgets.

## Key Characteristics

- The advisor is the coordinator of CPA, estate counsel, legal counsel, client behavior, and portfolio implementation, rather than a selector of off-the-shelf products.
- Work is organized around the whole household balance sheet: accounts, tax statuses, asset classes, liquidity needs, and transition paths.
- Gain, risk, liquidity, and time budgets are explicit constraints that also serve as the agenda for client and CPA conversations.
- Lot-level automation and institutional infrastructure carry the operational load so the advisor can apply judgment about which levers to pull.
- The role depends on behavioral understanding: complex tax design is translated into actions the client will actually take.

## Applications

- Open a taxable relationship by diagnosing tax drag across the household, then set yearly gain, risk, liquidity, and time budgets with the client and CPA.
- Use the platform’s lot-level engine to build transition roadmaps, while the advisor decides sequencing and explains the tradeoffs.
- Align estate counsel and legal advisors when trusts, concentrated holdings, or liquidity events change the tax design.
- Keep the advisor in the client conversation, with automation reconciling accounts, loading lot files, and preparing implementation in the background.
