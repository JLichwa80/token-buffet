# tax-free policy loans

## Definition

Tax-free policy loans are lifetime borrowings against a life insurance policy that give the holder access to policy value without treating the advance as a taxable distribution. In the panel’s account of institutionally priced [[Private Placement Life Insurance]], they are the in-life liquidity feature that sits alongside tax deferral while the portfolio accumulates and a tax-free distribution at death.

## Key Characteristics

- They are described as available during life on institutionally priced, skinny policies that have no surrender charges and whose liquidity mirrors the underlying assets.
- Loan proceeds are characterized as tax-free, so the holder can use value without a taxable distribution from the policy.
- They leave income-heavy or high-turnover strategies invested inside the policy instead of forcing a distribution of taxable gains.
- They are part of the wrapper’s claimed structural benefit: continued compounding inside the policy, with access to value before death.

## Applications

- A holder of [[Private Placement Life Insurance]] borrows against the policy to spend or redeploy value while the underlying portfolio stays inside the contract.
- [[Asset location]] places income-heavy exposures such as [[Private credit]], and strategies such as a [[High-turnover fund]], inside the policy so short-term gains and ordinary income are not distributed currently. Policy loans are the means of using that value during life.
- The panel’s case study invests $10 million over four years and, at a 10 percent IRR over 30 years, contrasts roughly $125 million inside the policy with roughly $50 million in a taxable account. Loans are the feature that lets the client use value while that compounding continues.
