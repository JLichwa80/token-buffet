# upREIT

## Definition

An upREIT (Umbrella Partnership Real Estate Investment Trust), also referred to as a Section 721 exchange, is a tax-deferred real estate structure in which property owners or trust beneficiaries contribute real estate assets or Delaware Statutory Trust (DST) interests into an operating partnership of a REIT in exchange for partnership units rather than cash.

## Key Characteristics

- **Tax Deferral**: Facilitates the transfer of appreciated real property into a diversified portfolio without triggering immediate capital gains realization.
- **Two-Stage Transition**: Often serves as a second-stage structure following a property sale and an initial 1031 exchange into a Delaware Statutory Trust (typically held for 2–3 years) before converting into operating units of a perpetual non-traded REIT.
- **Passive Income Generation**: Enables active property managers and owners to transition into passive real estate ownership while preserving continuous income streams.
- **Liquidity Mechanism**: Upon conversion into a perpetual non-traded REIT structure, offers periodic liquidity (typically up to 2 percent of Net Asset Value per month).
- **Estate Planning Utility**: Operating partnership units can be held and passed to heirs, preserving deferral benefits and allowing stepped-up basis advantages upon inheritance.

## Applications

- **Active-to-Passive Real Estate Conversion**: Used by real estate investors seeking to exit direct, active property management operations while preserving continuous cash flow and deferred tax liability.
- **Estate Planning**: Utilized to divide real estate wealth smoothly among multiple heirs through divisible operating partnership units rather than physical real estate parcels.
- **Portfolio Diversification**: Employed to shift concentrated single-asset or local property risk into broad, professionally managed multi-property institutional REIT portfolios.
