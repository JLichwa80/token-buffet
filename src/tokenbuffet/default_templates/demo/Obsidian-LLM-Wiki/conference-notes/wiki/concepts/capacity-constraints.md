# Capacity Constraints

## Definition

Capacity constraints refer to the practical upper limit on the total volume of assets an investment strategy or asset manager can deploy before market friction, liquidity limitations, and market impact begin to diminish performance and erode returns.

## Key Characteristics

- **Market Liquidity Dependency**: Tightly coupled to the overall size, depth, and trading volume of the underlying market or asset class.
- **Market Impact & Friction**: Scaling beyond capacity limits leads to increased transaction costs, wider bid-ask spreads, and unfavorable execution prices.
- **Strategy & Vehicle Scalability**: Systematic and automated execution across broad fixed income universes allows vehicles such as separately managed accounts (SMAs) to achieve substantial scale compared to highly niche or concentrated strategies.
- **Asset Class Disparity**: Massive asset markets (such as the multi-trillion-dollar global debt market) provide significantly higher capacity headroom than smaller, less liquid asset classes.

## Applications

- **Fixed Income Strategy Scaling**: Assessing capacity boundaries when managing large-scale, systematic, and tax-optimized bond portfolios across diverse debt sectors.
- **Asset Allocation & Fund Capacity Planning**: Evaluating when an investment vehicle or manager should close to new capital to preserve alpha and execution quality.
- **Execution & Liquidity Analysis**: Modeling trade size thresholds against market depth to prevent market impact and adverse selection.
