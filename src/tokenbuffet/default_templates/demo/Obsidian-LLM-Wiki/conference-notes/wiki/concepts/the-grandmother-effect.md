# The Grandmother Effect

## Definition

The Grandmother Effect is an intuitive fiduciary evaluation heuristic used by financial advisors to cut through marketing assertions and determine whether complex investment products or derivative structures are genuinely suitable for everyday clients. The core standard evaluates whether an advisor would comfortably place their own grandmother's capital into the investment, emphasizing capital preservation, fairness, and transparency over headline yields.

## Key Characteristics

- **Fiduciary Heuristic**: Acts as a practical sniff test and moral gut check beyond minimal legal compliance or disclosure requirements.
- **Capital Preservation Focus**: Prioritizes protecting principal and mitigating tail-risk over chasing attractive headline yields or aggressive options-generated cash flows.
- **Simplicity and Transparency**: Filters out overly opaque, convoluted, or multi-layered derivative and structured strategies that cannot easily be explained to or understood by a retail investor.
- **Alignment of Interests**: Forces direct advisor empathy and alignment with the client's long-term financial security.

## Applications

- **Product Due Diligence**: Evaluating novel or complex exchange-traded products, structured notes, and derivative-income funds prior to adding them to client model portfolios.
- **Client Suitability Assessments**: Screening out products that present asymmetric downside risks or severe volatility drag for risk-averse retail and wealth management clients.
- **Fiduciary Governance**: Establishing an ethical benchmark within investment advisory committees when reviewing marketing claims versus underlying net structural payoff risks.
