# ETF

## Definition

An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a pooled investment vehicle that trades continuously on secondary stock exchanges throughout the trading day at market-determined prices, utilizing an institutional in-kind creation and redemption mechanism to achieve tax efficiency and operational scale.

## Key Characteristics

- **Intraday Liquidity**: Shares trade continuously on stock exchanges at market prices rather than settling at end-of-day net asset value (NAV).
- **In-Kind Creation/Redemption**: Authorized participants exchange underlying baskets of securities for fund shares (and vice versa), minimizing portfolio turnover and shielding shareholders from realized capital gains distributions.
- **Tax Efficiency**: Structural insulation from mutual-fund-style capital gains pass-through distributions via the primary creation and redemption process.
- **Market Scale & Evolution**: A market exceeding $14 trillion expanding into next-generation structures, including tokenization, dual share classes alongside mutual funds, and strategies targeting tax-free income generation.

## Applications

- Core liquid asset allocation across diversified asset classes and benchmark indices.
- Tax-efficient wrapper deployment for active and passive investment strategies.
- Dual share class structuring to harmonize mutual fund and ETF liquidity and distribution channels.
- On-chain tokenization integration for institutional cash management and yield products.
