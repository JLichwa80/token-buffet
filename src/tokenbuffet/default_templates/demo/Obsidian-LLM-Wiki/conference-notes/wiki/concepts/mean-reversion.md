# Mean reversion

## Definition

Mean reversion is a financial and statistical theory suggesting that asset prices and historical returns eventually return to their long-term average or fundamental baseline over time, causing market overshoots and price dislocations to self-correct.

## Key Characteristics

- **Cyclical Price Correction**: Overextended valuations driven by market noise or sentiment eventually revert toward economic fundamentals.
- **Foundation for Contrarian Alpha**: Provides the economic justification for rules-based rebalancing by allowing strategies to profit from systematically trimming overvalued assets and accumulating undervalued ones.
- **Decoupled from Fundamentals**: The correction occurs when market price movements diverge from underlying fundamental accounting metrics and subsequently normalize.
- **Complement to Diversification**: Characterized as an essential source of portfolio return alongside traditional risk dispersion ("diversification and mean reversion").

## Applications

- **Systematic Rebalancing**: Harvesting [[Rebalancing alpha]] across non-market-cap-weighted strategies by continuously leaning against price swings.
- **Fundamental Indexing**: Anchoring portfolio weights to company fundamentals via strategies like [[RAFI]] to systematically capture return when market pricing diverges and mean-reverts.
- **Equal Weight Portfolio Construction**: Exploiting periodic asset dislocations through scheduled rebalancing back to equal target weights.
- **Tax-Aware Value Strategies**: Executing disciplined, contrarian portfolio shifts while managing capital gains realization to preserve the underlying rebalancing premium.
