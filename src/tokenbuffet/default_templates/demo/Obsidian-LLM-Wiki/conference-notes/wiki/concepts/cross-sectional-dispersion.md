# Cross-Sectional Dispersion

## Definition

Cross-sectional dispersion refers to the degree of variance in returns across individual securities within the broader market over a given period. In after-tax wealth management and direct indexing, it describes how specific asset lots experience price declines even during overall market gains, providing regular opportunities to harvest tax losses without relying on a broad market downturn.

## Key Characteristics

- **Independent of Market Direction**: Losses occur and can be harvested in bull, sideways, or bear markets because individual security paths diverge from index-level performance.
- **Enabled by Direct Ownership**: Realizing the benefits of cross-sectional dispersion requires owning individual underlying constituents rather than a single pooled vehicle (e.g., standard ETF or mutual fund).
- **Core Engine for Loss Inventory**: Supplies the raw material for building a pool of tax-loss carryforwards across multiple tax years.
- **Conversion of Volatility into Assets**: Reframes idiosyncratic risk and price volatility from a client liability into a valuable tax asset.

## Applications

- **Tax-Loss Harvesting**: Actively harvesting depreciated tax lots to generate capital losses continuously throughout market cycles.
- **Building a Loss Inventory**: Storing realized losses to offset future recognized capital gains or rebalance portfolios tax-efficiently.
- **Transition Management**: Utilizing harvested losses to cushion the tax cost of migrating concentrated or legacy low-basis portfolios into modern target allocations.
- **Liquidity Event Planning**: Offsetting large upcoming realized capital events (such as business sales or real estate exits) using accumulated losses.
