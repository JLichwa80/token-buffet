# Partisanship

## Definition

Partisanship in the context of fiscal and tax policy refers to the tendency for major tax legislation to be enacted primarily or exclusively along party lines—passing as Democrat-only or Republican-only measures rather than enduring bipartisan consensus. Consequently, significant tax restructuring occurs primarily under conditions of unified government, with policy priorities oscillating sharply depending on which political party holds control.

## Key Characteristics

- **Unified Government Dependency**: Substantive legislative overhauls require single-party control of both the executive and legislative branches to pass.
- **Pendulum Policy Shifts**: Tax priorities alternate between ideological extremes across administrations (e.g., state property tax repeal proposals on the right versus high-net-worth wealth taxes on the left).
- **Fiscal Instability**: Absence of durable cross-party consensus results in non-permanent, unstable tax law subject to repeal or revision upon power transitions.
- **Asymmetric Bipartisanship**: Federal bipartisan agreement is predominantly observed in spending increases rather than the establishment of a cohesive, sustainable fiscal design.

## Applications

- **Scenario Planning**: Anticipating policy outcomes by modeling two distinct legislative states (unified Republican control vs. unified Democratic control) instead of awaiting compromise draft bills.
- **Wealth and Tax Advisory**: Structuring long-term estate, income, and business tax strategies to withstand recurring shifts between high-net-worth levies and deregulation/tax cuts.
- **Fiscal Risk Assessment**: Evaluating the longevity and vulnerability of recently passed tax statutes in financial and corporate planning.
