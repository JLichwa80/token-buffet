# Time Budgets

## Definition

A Time Budget is a strategic framework that establishes the pace, milestones, and total duration over which a legacy or concentrated portfolio transitions toward a designated target portfolio. Rather than executing an immediate liquidation or relying on ad-hoc staged selling, setting a structured time horizon permits multi-year tax-loss harvesting and tax-budgeted reallocations, balancing prompt risk mitigation against the realization of immediate tax liabilities.

## Key Characteristics

- **Multi-Period Reallocation**: Spreads asset disposition over multiple tax years to maximize annual tax-loss harvesting offsets and smooth realized capital gains.
- **Tax vs. Risk Trade-Off**: Serves as the temporal governor balancing tracking error/concentration risk against upfront tax drag.
- **Systematic Pacing**: Replaces arbitrary or emotional market timing with defined milestones tied to tax periods and market conditions.
- **Coordination with Capital Gains Budgets**: Functions in tandem with capital gains expenditure budgets to optimize after-tax terminal wealth.

## Applications

- **Concentrated Stock De-risking**: Gradually unwinding large single-stock positions resulting from executive compensation, founder equity, or inherited assets over a deliberate multi-year window.
- **Legacy Portfolio Transitions**: Transitioning inherited or legacy taxable accounts into modernized direct-indexing or core-satellite allocation strategies without incurring prohibitive tax hits in a single tax year.
- **Tax-Aware Wealth Advisory**: Establishing clear client expectations and milestone roadmaps during the onboarding and portfolio restructuring process.
