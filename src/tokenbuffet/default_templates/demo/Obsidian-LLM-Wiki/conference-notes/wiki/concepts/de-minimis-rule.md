# de minimis rule

## Definition

The **de minimis rule** is an Internal Revenue Service (IRS) regulation defining a discount threshold for debt securities below which market discount accretion is taxed as ordinary income rather than capital gains at redemption or sale. The statutory threshold is calculated as par value minus 0.25% per full year remaining until maturity. Bonds priced below this threshold encounter the "de minimis cliff," significantly elevating the investor's tax liability.

## Key Characteristics

- **Threshold Calculation Formula:** The cutoff is established at 0.25% per full year remaining until maturity below par (100 - (0.25 × Years to Maturity)).
- **Tax Classification Shift:** Market discounts equal to or smaller than the threshold qualify for capital gains treatment; discounts exceeding the threshold subject the accrued discount to ordinary income tax rates.
- **De Minimis Cliff:** A sharp drop in after-tax yield and liquidity that occurs when a bond's market price crosses beneath the statutory threshold.
- **Rate Sensitivity:** Rising interest rate environments increase the proportion of secondary market issues trading below par and susceptible to the threshold.

## Applications

- **Municipal Portfolio Optimization:** Establishing quantitative guardrails in portfolio management to prevent unexpected ordinary income tax events for high-net-worth investors holding municipal bonds.
- **Discount Valuation & Relative Value Analysis:** Pricing the tax drag of bonds trading below the cliff relative to comparable par or premium issues.
- **Tax Loss Harvesting & Rebalancing:** Selecting replacement bonds during tax loss harvesting workflows that avoid adverse de minimis tax treatment.
