# Wealth Tax

## Definition

A wealth tax is a direct tax levied on an individual's accumulated net wealth or total asset base rather than on their annual income or transaction flow. In modern fiscal debates, it serves as a high-net-worth levy and a prominent left-populist policy proposal aimed at targeting accumulated wealth to fund government expenditure and offset narrowing income tax bases.

## Key Characteristics

- **Levied on Net Assets**: Assessed against the total market value of assets owned (cash, real estate, equities, private business interests) minus liabilities, rather than recurring income.
- **Left-Populist Fiscal Mechanism**: Frequently positioned as a political counterweight to popular income exemptions and regressive tax measures, concentrating revenue collection on top wealth brackets.
- **Base Concentration**: Imposes heightened tax burdens on a narrow cohort of high-net-worth individuals and corporate entities that already account for a significant share of income tax revenue.
- **Subnational Experimentation**: Often piloted or debated at the state level (e.g., California, Washington) as a testing ground before potentially ascending to federal legislative debate.

## Applications

- **Subnational Policy Prototyping**: Serves as a state-level policy experiment that previews potential shifts in national fiscal policy.
- **Counter-balancing Tax Base Erosion**: Employed in policy proposals to capture revenue when conventional income-tax bases contract due to carve-outs, exemptions, or shifting economic behavior.
- **Wealth Redistribution and Deficit Coverage**: Proposed as a vehicle to fund social initiatives and compress wealth inequality through targeted surtaxes on ultra-high-net-worth households.
