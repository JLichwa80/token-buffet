# Household Balance Sheet

## Definition

The household balance sheet is the comprehensive, aggregated view of a family's financial assets evaluated across all account structures, tax statuses, legal entities, liquidity constraints, and long-term objectives. It serves as the foundational diagnostic object that financial advisors must analyze prior to designing any portfolio strategy.

## Key Characteristics

- **Multi-Account & Multi-Entity Scope**: Encompasses all family holdings, including taxable accounts, IRAs, trusts, 529 plans, donor-advised funds (DAFs), concentrated private or public equity, and business interests.
- **Tax Status Differentiation**: Delineates assets by their specific tax treatment, cost basis, embedded capital gains, and exposure to tax drag.
- **Holistic Diagnostic Focus**: Shifting the definition of success from the standalone performance of an individual account to the unified, household after-tax destination.
- **Identification of Hidden Inefficiencies**: Uncovers where tax drag, liquidity mismatches, and legacy product inefficiencies (e.g., legacy mutual funds) are concealed across multiple holdings.

## Applications

- **Pre-Design Portfolio Diagnosis**: Serving as the mandatory initial diagnostic step before implementing customized asset location or tax transition strategies.
- **Locating Tax Drag**: Systematically identifying embedded capital gains, high-turnover legacy assets, and suboptimal asset placements across accounts.
- **Household Wealth Transitions**: Guiding the orderly diversification of illiquid or concentrated positions (such as private business equity) into tax-advantaged and diversified allocations.
- **Goal-Aligned Wealth Architecture**: Aligning distinct asset pools (e.g., trusts for generational wealth, 529s for education, DAFs for philanthropy) into a coordinated family wealth strategy.
