# wrapper

## Definition

An investment wrapper is a legal and regulatory structure—such as an ETF, mutual fund, Separately Managed Account (SMA), Collective Investment Trust (CIT), hedge fund, or tokenized share—that houses investment assets and governs operational responsibilities, investor eligibility, and tax flow-through.

## Key Characteristics

- **Tax Translation**: Bridges pre-tax strategy performance to holdable after-tax returns; the tax code unintentionally favors certain wrapper structures over others.
- **Regulatory & Operational Container**: Dictates operational overhead, regulatory reporting, and distribution eligibility across institutional and retail channels.
- **Structural Evolution**: Spans traditional fund structures to modern post-wrapper innovations, including tokenized shares and dual-share-class architectures.

## Applications

- Translating pre-tax investment alpha into maximized net after-tax yield for end investors.
- Determining appropriate vehicle selection (e.g., ETF vs. SMA vs. CIT) based on liquidity, investor profile, and operational costs.
- Deploying next-generation asset structures, such as tokenized T-bills or hybrid multi-class fund formats.
