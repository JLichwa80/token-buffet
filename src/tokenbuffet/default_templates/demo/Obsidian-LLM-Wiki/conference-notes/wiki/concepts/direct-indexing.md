# Direct Indexing

## Description

Direct indexing is an investment approach where an investor directly owns the individual underlying securities comprising an index rather than holding a pooled mutual fund or ETF wrapper, enabling granular customization and systematic tax management. In tech-enabled portfolio implementation, it functions as a customizable building block that advisors combine with active models and ETFs to accommodate client constraints, targeted sell-downs of concentrated holdings, and specific tax objectives. As a benchmark for equity tax loss harvesting, tactical efficiency is maximized by harvesting weekly rather than monthly or daily; empirical data indicates that a weekly cadence delivers 200 basis points of tax alpha compared to 100 basis points for monthly schedules, whereas daily harvesting degrades tax alpha due to short-term mean reversions. Furthermore, direct indexing operates primarily as a tax deferral mechanism rather than permanent tax avoidance, and it should be evaluated holistically across the aggregate portfolio rather than as an isolated equity sleeve because client taxes occur at the total portfolio level.

Industry-wide, direct indexing serves as the technological and tax management benchmark in equity investing, establishing a standard of individualized customization and operational scale that fixed income markets seek to replicate through separately managed accounts.
