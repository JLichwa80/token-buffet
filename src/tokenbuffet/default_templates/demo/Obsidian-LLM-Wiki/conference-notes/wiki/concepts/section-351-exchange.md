# Section 351 Exchange

## Definition

A Section 351 Exchange is a tax-deferred transaction governed by Section 351 of the Internal Revenue Code (enacted in 1954), permitting the contribution of property—specifically exchange-traded stocks and ETFs—into a corporate entity structured as an ETF in exchange for shares of that corporation, without immediately triggering capital gains taxes on built-in gains.

## Key Characteristics

- **Statutory Framework**: Grounded in a longstanding section of the Internal Revenue Code since 1954, operating as a defined tax code provision rather than an unintended loophole.
- **Eligible Contributors**: Restricted to U.S. individual taxpayers; corporations are excluded from contributing.
- **Eligible Assets**: Limited strictly to exchange-traded equities and ETFs; cash, mutual funds, digital assets/cryptocurrency, commodities (e.g., [[GLD]]), and government securities are excluded.
- **Look-Through & Diversification**: Contributed portfolios are subject to strict household diversification tests, and existing ETF contributions are evaluated on a look-through basis down to their underlying constituent holdings.
- **Basis & Lot Preservation**: Custodians (such as [[Schwab]]) map the investor's original tax lots, preserving original acquisition dates and cost basis onto the newly received ETF shares.

## Applications

- **Ossified Taxable Accounts**: Transitioning legacy, highly appreciated taxable portfolios into diversified strategies without triggering taxable events.
- **Concentrated Stock De-risking**: Trimming significant single-stock equity concentrations without an outright sale.
- **Retirement Strategy Transitions**: Transitioning active stock-picking accounts into broader, systematically managed retirement asset allocations.
