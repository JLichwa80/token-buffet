# High-turnover fund

## Definition

A high-turnover fund is a portfolio whose trading realizes short-term gains and therefore creates tax drag when it is held in a taxable account. In this discussion the relevant attribute is the tax character of those gains. The source does not name a manager, a turnover threshold, or a holding period. The investment can stay the same while the account type changes, so short-term gains compound inside an insurance wrapper instead of being distributed and taxed.

## Key Characteristics

- Trading realizes short-term gains, which are taxed unfavorably in a taxable account.
- The issue is location: which account owns the strategy, rather than which manager or security to select.
- The panel groups high-turnover funds with [[Private credit]] and with public strategies that distribute during turbulent markets as holdings best placed inside [[Private Placement Life Insurance]].
- The source gives no turnover ratio, holding-period cutoff, or named product.
- The action points describe the same idea as high-turnover strategies that should be reallocated into the policy.

## Applications

- Hold high-turnover strategies inside [[Private Placement Life Insurance]] so short-term gains compound in the wrapper instead of being distributed and taxed each year.
- Reallocate high-turnover strategies into the policy as part of the illustrated path toward about 335 basis points of annualized IRR uplift, the case the panel ties to [[Structural Alpha]].
- Apply the same location choice to income-heavy [[Private credit]] and to public strategies that would otherwise make taxable distributions in turbulent markets.
- Leave the strategy in place and change only the account type when the tax character of the gains is the constraint.
