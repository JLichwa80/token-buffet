# 130/30 Strategy

## Definition

A 130/30 strategy is an equity portfolio archetype that holds long positions equal to 130% of portfolio capital and short positions equal to 30%. Net market exposure stays at 100%, while gross exposure is 160%, so the portfolio can tilt actively without dropping below full market participation. In Aaron's keynote it is presented as a high-conviction form of [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] that can produce more than 300 basis points of [[Tax Alpha]] by accelerating loss realization.

## Key Characteristics

- Long exposure of 130% and short exposure of 30% produce 100% net market exposure and 160% gross exposure.
- The long and short sleeves support active factor tilting while the net position remains fully invested in the market.
- Short positions and leverage expand the set of positions that can realize losses, treating [[concepts/losses-are-inventory|Losses Are Inventory]] that can be used against gains.
- The cited tax-aware case is 300+ basis points of tax alpha on a 130/30 strategy, with more potential as leverage increases.
- Deployment depends on the client's comfort with the alpha thesis, short exposure, leverage, complexity, and a restructuring of the household equity balance sheet.

## Applications

- Advisors use a 130/30 structure as a tax-aware long-short implementation when the goal is accelerated loss realization and measurable after-tax excess return.
- The structure fits clients who can use harvested losses and who accept gross exposure above 100%.
- Because of leverage and short exposure, the strategy is underwritten before it is used: the alpha thesis, a plan for the losses, tolerance for complexity, and willingness to make the strategy a core equity exposure rather than a small sleeve.
