# Optionality

## Definition

Optionality is the panel’s term for keeping more than one path from a concentrated holding to a diversified, tax-aware portfolio. It names the practical flexibility to change how a position is funded, hedged, or exited without forcing an immediate taxable sale of the low-basis asset.

## Key Characteristics

- In [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]], the account can be funded with cash or with appreciated assets, including single-name equities, so exposure can move from A to B without an immediate taxable sale.
- In the [[Laddered Diversification Framework]], losses are lined up against forward maturities, and the client can still sell whatever concentrated stock remains, described as cash free and clear.
- For crypto, optionality means applying [[Variable Prepaid Forward|VPFs]] and [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] to capital that had been stuck outside traditional tools once an ETF on-ramp exists.
- “Optionalities” sits in the session’s working vocabulary next to bridge, octane, and structural alpha.
- It is a planning choice among structures, not a single contract or a guaranteed after-tax result.

## Applications

- Fund a tax-aware long-short account with appreciated single-name equities and transition the exposure over time when a full [[Variable Prepaid Forward]] ladder is more structure than the client wants.
- Ladder [[Variable Prepaid Forward|VPFs]] against a loss-generating long-short allocation, then sell the remaining concentrated stock once offsetting losses are in place.
- After an in-kind move onto the [[Crypto ETF Bridge]], apply [[Variable Prepaid Forward|VPFs]] and [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] to [[Bitcoin]] that previously sat outside those tools.
- Place the same toolkit beside [[Private Placement Life Insurance]] inside the [[ETS Playbook]] when the goal is a path from the source asset to a tax-aware destination.
