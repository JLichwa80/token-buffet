# Dual Share Class

## Definition

A dual share class structure places a mutual fund share class and an ETF share class on one portfolio, with the same NAV and the same shareholder rights. Mutual fund buyers stay in the mutual fund channel while receiving ETF trading efficiency, including in-kind operational advantages.

## Key Characteristics

- One portfolio supports multiple wrappers, so the mutual fund class and the ETF class share the same holdings, NAV, and rights.
- [[FM Investments]] describes its version as the first Vanguard-approved and issued form of this design.
- The intended investor experience is equivalent across wrappers. Overlapping total-return lines for the T-Bill and [[TBMax]] products are used to illustrate that claim.
- In-kind creation and redemption can carry ETF operational advantages into the mutual fund share class.
- The speaker treats the setup as a rare [[SEC]] arrangement in which the investor always wins.
- Leverages the ETF in-kind creation and redemption mechanism across the unified structure to eliminate tax leakage specifically for mutual fund holders.
## Applications

- Offer clients the same strategy in both a mutual fund wrapper and an ETF wrapper.
- Map existing holdings to available dual share products, such as T-Bill and [[TBMax]], and migrate positions where the dual share class is suitable.
- Give mutual fund buyers ETF trading efficiency and in-kind operational advantages without requiring them to leave the mutual fund channel.
- Use the overlapping total-return record to explain that the two wrappers are meant to deliver the same investment result.
