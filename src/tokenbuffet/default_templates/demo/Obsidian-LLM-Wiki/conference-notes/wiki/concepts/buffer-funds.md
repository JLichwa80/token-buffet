# Buffer Funds

## Definition

Buffer funds (commonly known as Defined Outcome ETFs) are structured exchange-traded funds that utilize options contracts (such as FLEX options) to provide downside protection against market losses up to a predetermined percentage buffer over a specific outcome period, in exchange for a capped maximum upside return over that same period. Packaged as registered 1940 Act funds, they allow advisors and investors to implement systematic risk hedges and asymmetric return profiles without managing or trading individual option legs directly.

## Key Characteristics

- **Defined Outcome Period**: Operates across a specific holding period (typically one year), after which the options reset with new cap and buffer levels.
- **Downside Buffer**: Provides protection against market declines up to a stated percentage (e.g., first 10%, 15%, or 20% of losses).
- **Capped Upside Potential**: Limits maximum return during the outcome period to finance the purchase of the downside put options.
- **Packaged Implementation**: Structured under the Investment Company Act of 1940, eliminating the need for clients or advisors to manage option margin, exercise, or roll mechanics manually.
- **Path Dependency**: Intra-period purchases or sales will experience differing effective buffers and remaining caps depending on prevailing underlying asset performance and remaining time to maturity.

## Applications

- **Risk Mitigation for Pre-Retirees/Retirees**: Shielding equity allocations from sequence of returns risk without completely giving up equity market appreciation.
- **Equity Substitute in Conservative Portfolios**: Replacing traditional fixed income or conservative equity allocations with a bounded-risk equity vehicle during periods of low bond yields or elevated volatility.
- **Systematic Hedging**: Providing institutional or retail wealth managers a turn-key solution to systematically manage downside volatility without trading derivative overlays.
