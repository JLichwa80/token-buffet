# Washington Designed Your Portfolio

## Definition

“Washington Designed Your Portfolio” is the claim that the tax code and its regulations shape investment wrappers and investor behavior more than investment theory does, and that they do so by accident. Product design is then the work of turning that unintended architecture into structures that favor the investor.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats the tax code as an accidental portfolio architect: rules written at different times, by different Congresses, still determine which wrappers and behaviors are available.
- Measures the claim by the scale of the statute and its regulations. The tax code is described as roughly as long as the Harry Potter series, and the regulations are put at 7.7 million words.
- Treats that volume as operational, not literary. The speaker says representatives now paste a bill into ChatGPT to learn what it means for constituents.
- Holds that planning written against today’s fragments may need revision within years, because sunsets and incremental rules keep changing the design.
- Frames the investor response as translation: regulation becomes product architecture that can produce tax alpha.

## Applications

- Client communication. The talk turns the line into a brief: explain that products are engineered to convert regulatory complexity into tax alpha, using “Washington designed your portfolio. It didn’t mean to, but it did.” Room notes record nods when the line landed, which the summary treats as the memorable statement of the thesis.
- Product design that answers the code’s shape: dual share classes that put ETF trading efficiency into a mutual-fund wrapper, tokenization that cuts operational layers, and income structures such as CPMG that use distribution rules to change the character of return.
- Portfolio review. Holdings built for current fragments are checked against sunset risk, because the same accidental design can be rewritten on a short notice.
