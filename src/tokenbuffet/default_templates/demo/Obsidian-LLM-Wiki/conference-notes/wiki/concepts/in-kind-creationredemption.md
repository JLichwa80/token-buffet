# In-Kind Creation/Redemption

## Definition

In-kind creation and redemption is the ETF mechanism that lets authorized participants exchange baskets of securities for shares. It is a core source of ETF tax efficiency and is treated in the talk as a standing feature of the ETF wrapper, listed with [[wrapper]], [[Tax Alpha]], [[Dual Share Class]], and step-up basis.

## Key Characteristics

- It is named among the presentation’s key terms, alongside wrappers, tax alpha, dual share class, and step-up basis.
- The speaker groups political attacks on ETF in-kind creation and redemption with carried-interest repeal and calls those attacks dead on arrival.
- The talk does not describe basket construction or creation-unit size.
- [[Dual Share Class]] is presented as the way to extend ETF trading and tax efficiency, including this mechanism’s benefits, to mutual fund buyers.
- For concentrated crypto holdings such as Bitcoin, in-kind creation functions as an on-ramp to transfer low-basis digital assets directly into traditional ETF wrappers and standard regulatory accounts without triggering an immediate taxable disposal.
- It permits exchange-traded funds to wash out low-cost-basis securities through the creation/redemption process, eliminating capital gains realizations at the fund level.
## Applications

Advisors are told to keep using the in-kind mechanism because Congress is not expected to dismantle it. Teams are also told to learn in-kind mechanics, together with sections 351 and 1031 and the alternative minimum tax, so they can spot incentives the tax code creates. [[Dual Share Class]] structures then carry that trading and tax efficiency into portfolios bought as mutual funds, under rules the speaker credits to the [[SEC]].

Holders of concentrated low-basis [[entities/bitcoin|Bitcoin]] can utilize in-kind ETF creations to transition into an ETF wrapper, pairing the structure with [[entities/variable-prepaid-forward|Variable Prepaid Forwards]] to manage the timing of gain recognition while funding tax-aware long-short strategies for continuous loss harvesting.
