# Deleveraging Path

## Definition

The deleveraging path is the planned multi-year unwind and step-down of leverage used to manufacture tax losses during a portfolio transition, moving from an aggressive temporary long-short allocation back toward a conventional, lower-leverage long-term target structure.

## Key Characteristics

- **Temporary Exposure Management**: Recognizes aggressive gearing (such as 250/150 long-short portfolios) as a temporary tactical posture to offset large capital gains rather than a permanent asset allocation.
- **Pre-Commitment & Advance Planning**: Emphasizes agreeing on the unwind schedule prior to implementation rather than treating the unwind as an afterthought.
- **Risk Transition Protocol**: Addresses the distinction between structural long-short risks and standard market risks by establishing explicit exit criteria once the target tax offset is achieved.
- **Target Normalization**: Models a multi-stage migration pathway back toward normalized exposure benchmarks (e.g., a 115/15 book).
- **Cost & Structural Risk Containment**: Targets rapid tax-loss harvesting through elevated postures (e.g., 250/150 or 275/275) while mandating a structured unwind to eliminate ongoing borrowing costs and persistent short risk.
## Applications

- **Tax-Neutral Transition Design**: Structuring client onboarding proposals that demonstrate how leverage will be reduced once embedded capital gains are fully offset.
- **Client Risk Communication**: Setting clear client expectations regarding leverage lifecycle, drawdown risks, and expected timeframes for normalization.
- **Portfolio Implementation**: Providing advisors with an actionable playbook for unwinding derivative and long-short exposure systematically without generating unwanted tax friction.
