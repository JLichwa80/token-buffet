# permanent tax avoidance

## Definition

Permanent tax avoidance in fixed income refers to the complete elimination of future capital gains tax liabilities rather than merely postponing or deferring them. In this strategy, realized capital losses from sold bonds are offset by replacing them with premium bonds whose purchase premium naturally amortizes down to par over their remaining lifespan, returning par value at maturity with zero capital gain realization.

## Key Characteristics

- **Liability Elimination vs. Deferral**: Unlike equity tax-loss harvesting—which lowers the cost basis and primarily defers tax obligations until a future sale—fixed income harvesting with premium bonds permanently removes capital gains exposure.
- **Premium Amortization Mechanism**: Replacement bonds acquired at a premium naturally adjust down to par value over time through amortization, avoiding a terminal taxable capital gain upon maturity.
- **Predictable Maturity Value**: Because fixed income instruments return par value at a designated maturity date, the ultimate tax impact and terminal basis alignment can be modeled with high certainty.

## Applications

- **Fixed Income Tax Alpha**: Executed during periods of rising interest rates to harvest capital losses against other taxable income or capital gains while maintaining portfolio duration and credit exposure.
- **Premium Bond Swaps**: Replacing underwater bonds with premium issues, particularly in high-bracket taxable or municipal bond portfolios, to lock in tax-loss benefits without setting up an offsetting deferred tax burden at maturity.
