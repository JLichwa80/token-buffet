# Tax-Aware Long-Short

## Definition

Tax-aware long-short is a quantitative equity portfolio built to stay aligned with a broad equity benchmark, seek potential alpha, and deliberately realize large, ongoing capital losses. Speaker 3 describes it as an old institutional technique that individuals can now hold in separately managed accounts and single-member series LLCs without at-risk limitations. Those losses are meant to offset gains elsewhere in the portfolio, including gains recognized as [[Variable Prepaid Forward|variable prepaid forwards]] mature.

## Key Characteristics

- Benchmark-aligned quantitative long-short equity, with an explicit aim of realizing substantial and persistent capital losses.
- Held in vehicles individuals can use—separately managed accounts and single-member series LLCs—without at-risk limitations on the losses.
- Funded with cash or with appreciated assets, including single-name equities, so a client can move from exposure A to exposure B over time.
- Leverage and active risk, called the allocation’s octane, are set either to free cash or to maximize loss generation and are matched to the forward ladder.
- A direct-funding path transitions a single-name position into the long-short over roughly 13–24 months when a full forward structure is more complexity than the client wants.
- Portfolios go long replacement holdings and short other securities to realize losses that offset gains from selling appreciated assets, utilizing customized leverage ratios (such as 250/150 or 275/275 market-neutral) to dial transition speed.
- Employs daily loss harvesting, proprietary signals to avoid crowded trades, funding flexibility with marginable assets, and an intentional multi-year deleveraging path.
- Capable of generating over 300 basis points of potential tax alpha in a 130/30 implementation, with tax alpha expanding as leverage scales.
- Deployment requires satisfying four qualification criteria: advisor acceptance of the alpha thesis and short-exposure risk, a concrete client use for capital losses, client acceptance of gross exposure exceeding 100%, leverage, and operational complexity, and willingness to restructure the overall household equity portfolio.
- Carries tax-compliance risk under "shorting against the box" rules if mismanaged, which can disallow harvested losses and frequently necessitates deploying the strategy as core equity exposure rather than a satellite allocation.
- Unlike long-only direct indexing where higher alpha locks up portfolios with built-in gains, higher pretax alpha in tax-aware long-short strategies acts as a tax engine by inducing an under-levered state that prompts position creation into fresh holdings with new cost basis, expanding cumulative net capital losses and boosting after-tax wealth.
- Between 2023 and 2026, assets under management in tax-aware long-short strategies grew exponentially from approximately $9 billion to over $150 billion.
- Common implementation structures evaluated relative to the Russell 1000 benchmark include 150/50, 200/100, and 250/150 gross leverage configurations targeting a beta of one with informed tracking error driven by an alpha model.
## Applications

- Inside the [[ETS Playbook]], proceeds and hedges from a [[Variable Prepaid Forward]] fund the long-short so losses shield taxes as each rung of a [[Laddered Diversification Framework]] rolls off.
- Clients who would sell a concentrated stock but for tax can deploy unrestricted forward proceeds into the long-short while keeping a diversified equity core.
- Appreciated single names can seed the account directly, shifting the holding toward the benchmark portfolio without an immediate sale of the whole position.
- [[Bitcoin]] that has moved into the traditional system can use the same loss-harvesting allocation alongside forwards.
- The panel treats the approach as less effective for [[Washington State capital gains]], where short-term losses do not offset long-term gains, so the federal benefit still needs a separate state plan.
- The same client conversation often places tax-inefficient accumulation in [[Private Placement Life Insurance]], whose benefit the panel calls [[Structural Alpha]].
- Facilitates tax-neutral transitions and systematic sell-downs for concentrated stock positions and major liquidity events, such as business sales (e.g., offsetting up to 75% of capital gains within five months post-sale).
- Serves as an inventory generation tool for pre-liquidity planning and concentrated stock wind-downs, though strategy fees are unjustified if the portfolio lacks realized gains to absorb harvested losses.
