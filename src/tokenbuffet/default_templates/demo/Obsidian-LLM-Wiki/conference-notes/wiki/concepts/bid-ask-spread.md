# Bid-Ask Spread

## Definition

The bid-ask spread is the difference between the price at which a security can be bought (the ask) and the price at which it can be sold (the bid). It is one component of fixed-income trading costs.

## Key Characteristics

- A narrower spread generally means a lower cost of trading.
- Technology and electronic trading have reduced bond trading costs, including bid-ask spreads.
- ETF trading activity and electronic markets can contribute to greater liquidity.
- In fixed income markets, wide bid-ask spreads historically penalized individual investors and manual trading desks navigating over-the-counter trades.
- Tighter spreads compressed by quantitative firms and bond ETFs reduce execution costs, yet fundamental pricing dispersion persists for active managers to exploit.
## Applications

Investors and traders use the spread to assess transaction costs and market liquidity when buying or selling securities, including bonds and ETFs.
