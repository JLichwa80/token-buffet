# Net Information Ratio

## Definition

Net Information Ratio (Net IR) measures a manager's risk-adjusted active return after fully deducting all strategy costs, including financing, transaction drag, and management fees. It quantifies net manager skill and indicates whether pretax active returns are sufficient to overcome the underlying cost burden.

## Key Characteristics

- **Cost-Adjusted Metric**: Evaluates performance strictly after accounting for total financing expenses, trading friction, and management fee drag.
- **Vulnerability to Financing Drag**: In extended long-short structures lacking gross alpha, high borrowing and turnover expenses can drive the metric significantly negative (e.g., as low as -0.6).
- **Threshold for Flywheel Viability**: A positive Net IR requires demonstrable [[Pretax Alpha]] exceeding the strategy's [[Cost Stack]], serving as an essential prerequisite for tax-aware compounding and wealth generation.

## Applications

- **Manager Skill Evaluation**: Assessing whether an active manager generates genuine alpha rather than returns absorbed by implementation drag.
- **Feasibility Testing for Long-Short Strategies**: Stress-testing overlay and 130/30 extended long-short portfolio designs against expected financing and turnover costs.
- **Due Diligence and Performance Attribution**: Determining if active risk budgeting reliably translates into net value creation for investors.
