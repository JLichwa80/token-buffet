# 351

## Definition

351 is shorthand, in this talk, for a U.S. tax-code provision that sits in the “spider’s web of acronyms” shaping investment [[wrapper]] and the incentives around them. It is introduced with [[1031]], [[AMT]], and [[concepts/in-kind-creationredemption|In-Kind Creation/Redemption]] as vocabulary the audience is expected to know. In ordinary tax usage the label points to Internal Revenue Code Section 351, under which a transfer of property to a corporation in exchange for stock can qualify for nonrecognition when the statutory control test is met (a 351 exchange). The notes do not walk through that statute. Here, 351 is a node on a policy-arbitrage map: a rule product designers and advisors should recognize because it collides with other rules, rather than a worked example of a corporate contribution.

## Key Characteristics

- Treated as terminology to learn, grouped with [[1031]], [[AMT]], and in-kind creation and redemption, not as a step-by-step statutory case.
- Part of a set of rules that were not written at the same time or by the same [[Congress]], so they collide, [[sunset-clause|sunset]], and leave gaps.
- Those gaps are described as something product designers can use when they build [[wrapper]].
- On the action list, teams are told to train on 351 mechanics specifically so they can find incentives that can be used within the law.
- In this source it functions as a marker on the path to [[Tax Alpha]], not as a completed transaction write-up.

## Applications

- Training advisor teams on 351 alongside [[1031]], [[AMT]], and [[concepts/in-kind-creationredemption|In-Kind Creation/Redemption]] so they can spot legal incentives in wrapper design.
- Mapping where older and newer tax rules fail to line up, including sunset dates and uncovered gaps, when structuring products.
- Using 351 as a checklist item in policy-arbitrage work: know the provision well enough to see where a wrapper can rely on it, without treating the talk as a Section 351 opinion or a contribution checklist.
