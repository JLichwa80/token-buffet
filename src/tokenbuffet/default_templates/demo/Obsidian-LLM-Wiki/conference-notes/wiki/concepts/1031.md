# 1031

## Definition

1031 is a tax-code acronym that the presentation pairs with [[351]] inside a web of rules the industry is told to treat as a source of [[Tax Alpha]], rather than as a set of obstacles. The talk lists 351/1031 among the session’s key terms and later names 1031 again as one of the inconsistent rules that produce perverse incentives and continual change. It gives no exchange timeline, qualified-property test, or real-estate example. What it asks the audience to retain is that 1031 belongs to a jumble written by different [[Congress|Congresses]], and that planning has to anticipate [[sunset-clause]].

## Key Characteristics

- Appears with [[351]] in the session’s key terminology, alongside [[AMT]], tokenized T-bills, and [[In-Kind Creation/Redemption]].
- Sits in the “spider’s web of acronyms” that the speaker says creates perverse incentives and constant change.
- Is grouped with rules that were not written together or by the same [[Congress]], which is the opening the speaker says the industry gets to use.
- Is tied to [[sunset-clause]]: planning must expect provisions to expire, and the industry should look for permanence where the code does not provide it.
- Is an operational training item, not a worked example: teams are told to learn 1031 with [[351]], [[AMT]], and [[In-Kind Creation/Redemption|in-kind mechanics]].

## Applications

[[FM Investments]] frames this cluster of rules as material for [[Tax Alpha]]. The implementation note is to host a quarterly policy-arbitrage workshop and keep a living playbook that includes 1031 alongside [[351]], [[AMT]], and [[In-Kind Creation/Redemption|in-kind mechanics]], so the team can spot incentives it can use legally as policy shifts.
