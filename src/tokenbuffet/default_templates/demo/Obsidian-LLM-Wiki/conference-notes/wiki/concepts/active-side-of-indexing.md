# Active side of indexing

## Definition

The active side of indexing refers to the marginal slice of annual turnover inside an otherwise passive capitalization-weighted index fund. While the vast majority (e.g., ~95%) of a cap-weighted portfolio functions as buy-and-hold, the traded portion (e.g., ~5%) represents a non-neutral, active investment decision that systematically buys appreciating securities at higher multiples and sells depreciating securities at lower multiples.

## Key Characteristics

- **Non-Neutral Marginal Turnover**: Although turnover in cap-weighted indices is typically low (around 5%), the trades executed constitute an active portfolio decision rather than a passive holding.
- **Pro-Cyclical Trading Dynamics**: Marginal index turnover inherently buys companies whose market values have recently appreciated (often momentum or growth stocks at high valuation multiples) and sells companies whose market values have dropped (often value stocks at deep discounts).
- **Extreme Momentum Metaphor**: Described colloquially by proponents of fundamental weighting as operating "like Cathie Wood on crystal meth" due to its aggressive reinforcement of recent price trends.
- **Measurable Tax Alpha Potential**: Serves as a quantifiable baseline for tax-loss harvesting and rebalancing simulations, delivering approximately 170 basis points of tax alpha in US large-cap simulations (2008–2023), closely trailing [[Fundamental Indexing]]/[[RAFI]]'s ~190 basis points.
- **Index Design as Active Choice**: Highlights that selecting index rules and weighting schemes is fundamentally an active investment decision rather than an absence of management.
- **Foundational Research Provenance**: Formally articulated and detailed in published CFA Institute Research Foundation literature, specifically the research papers titled *"The Active Side of Indexing"* and *"Trifecta"*.
## Applications

- **Index Architecture & Evaluation**: Assessing the structural mechanics and implicit trading biases embedded within standard market-cap benchmark designs.
- **Tax-Loss Harvesting & Tax Alpha Modeling**: Modeling and benchmarking tax alpha generation in direct indexing and portfolio construction simulations.
- **Comparative Strategy Benchmarking**: Comparing the performance, turnover characteristics, and factor exposures of market-cap weighting against alternative strategies such as fundamental or rebalanced indexing.
