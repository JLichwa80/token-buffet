Voter Math is the presentation’s model of why taxes stay in the political backseat. The notes say representation comes from uneven vote distributions: about 200,000 votes typically send someone to Congress, only about 18 districts often determine control of the chamber, and U.S. turnout is low relative to global standards. From that arithmetic the speaker draws a policy conclusion. Carried-interest “elimination” is called a perennial nonstarter, and attacks on ETF in-kind creation and redemption are called dead on arrival, because taxes rarely top the agenda that actually decides elections. The learning point is aimed at product builders. Do not wait for sweeping reform; build for a world of incremental changes, sunsets, and one-year notice. Voter Math is the electoral half of the same argument as the Policy Formation Map: Washington designs portfolios indirectly, through what a thin and uneven electorate will not prioritize.
