# Common Denominator vs. Individual Optimization

## Definition

A distinction between managing multiple separately managed accounts (SMAs) by manually allocating a bond chosen to work across many accounts, and optimizing trades separately for each portfolio according to its own constraints and tax profile.

## Key Characteristics

- The common denominator approach seeks a bond that can serve many accounts, then allocates it manually.
- Manual allocation is labor-intensive and may not account fully for differences among clients’ constraints and tax profiles.
- Individual optimization treats each portfolio as a distinct problem and searches for its best trade.
- Proprietary technology can make individual optimization practical across a larger number of accounts.

## Applications

Use individual optimization when managing multiple SMA portfolios with different client constraints and tax profiles. It can support account-specific trade selection at scale, while the common denominator approach describes a manual alternative.
