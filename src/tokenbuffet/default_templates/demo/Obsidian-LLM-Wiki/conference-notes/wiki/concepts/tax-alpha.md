# Tax Alpha

## Definition

Tax alpha is investment result attributed to wrappers and tax rules rather than to security selection alone. In the FM Investments framing, the gap between Wall Street practice and Washington policy is a place to harvest that result from complicated, often temporary provisions, then lock it into products meant to compound for 20–30 years.

More broadly, it represents the measurable after-tax excess return generated through deliberate tax stack design and execution—often delivering hundreds of basis points of client-visible value that can be evaluated in information-ratio terms.
## Key Characteristics

- It is produced by legal containers and statutory mechanics, not by picking different securities inside an otherwise identical portfolio.
- Wrappers are treated as the conversion layer: they turn investment alpha into outcomes that can be held in a tax-aware way.
- The tax code is treated as a toolkit. Sunsets, acronyms, and statutes written at different times by different Congresses are the raw material for product design.
- The intended client outcome is lower taxes, less operational friction, and lower trading costs, explained as complexity that has been engineered into the product.
- The concrete tools named for capturing it are dual share classes, tokenization, and CPMG/CPHY.
- It delivers predictable excess returns framed in information-ratio terms, turning tax design into dependable, client-visible value rather than a cosmetic exercise.
- Potential after-tax excess returns range from 50 to 100 basis points for ETF and fund models, 15 to 200 basis points for direct and custom indexing, and 300 or more basis points for tax-aware 130/30 long-short strategies, scaling higher with leverage.
- It also encompasses an implementation edge achieved through disciplined loss harvesting, controlled gain realization, and eliminating careless execution, noting that roughly 98% of investors experience negative tax alpha due to sloppy trading.
- Historical and simulated spans from 2008 to 2023 demonstrate empirical excess returns, generating approximately 170 basis points of tax alpha in cap-weighted US large cap and 190 basis points in fundamental indexing (RAFI), compounding on top of pre-tax structural alpha.
- Beyond equity direct indexing, it encompasses fixed income and multi-asset rebalancing, where pairing municipal bond loss harvesting with soft cash-flow rebalancing accumulates substantial value over time.
- Implementation frequency critically dictates return: empirical data shows weekly equity harvesting yields roughly double the tax alpha (~200 bps) of monthly harvesting (~100 bps) with lower tracking error, whereas daily harvesting degrades returns due to short-term mean reversions.
- While equity loss harvesting functions primarily as tax deferral, bond loss harvesting can convert into permanent tax avoidance because replacement premium bonds amortize their basis down to par, extinguishing capital gains at maturity.
## Applications

- Dual share class portfolios put one strategy in both a mutual fund wrapper and an ETF wrapper, with the same net asset value and the same rights, so mutual fund buyers can receive ETF trading efficiency.
- Tokenized T-bills are presented as a way to cut counterparties and operational layers, tighten spreads, and add around-the-clock liquidity while keeping or improving the tax result.
- CPMG/CPHY bond sleeves accrue income in net asset value and rotate into proxy funds around ex-dates so ordinary-income distributions are reduced, with gains aimed at long-term character if the position is held long enough.
- Client communication uses the idea to explain why a portfolio is built around regulatory structure: a short note that shows how dual share classes, tokenization, and CPMG sit together.
- Direct and custom indexing strategies capture tax alpha through tax-lot level loss harvesting and tax-budgeted tracking error, delivering approximately 15 to 200 basis points of after-tax excess return.
- Tax-aware active overlays and 130/30 long-short implementations exploit loss inventory and shorting mechanics to harvest up to 300+ basis points of tax alpha.
- Municipal bond portfolios provide tax-preferred income overlays that integrate into broader allocations to enhance total after-tax yield.
- Fundamental indexing (RAFI) and cap-weighted indexing apply tax alpha mechanics where modest gain realization is offset by harvested losses, preserving rebalancing alpha net of taxes and trading costs.
- Systematic municipal bond management captures an estimated 36 basis points of annualized alpha, while eliminating rebalancing tax drag via soft cash-flow and trigger-point rebalancing recovers up to 16 basis points.
