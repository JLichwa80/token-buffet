# CITs

## Definition

CITs (collective investment trusts) are one of the legal containers in the presentation’s wrapper map, listed with mutual funds, ETFs, SMAs, hedge funds, and tokenized shares. In that map, a wrapper decides who can buy, how taxes flow, and where operational responsibility sits. The talk treats a CIT as a retirement-channel wrapper whose growth is part of the current landscape. It does not give CITs a statutory definition or a tax mechanic of their own, and it does not present a CIT as a product [[FM Investments]] claims to have invented.

## Key Characteristics

- CITs sit in the same wrapper set as mutual funds, ETFs, SMAs, hedge funds, and tokenized shares.
- Their role in the map is functional: access, tax flow, and operational responsibility, shared with the other containers rather than defined separately for CITs.
- On market scale, mutual funds are put at about $31 trillion and ETFs at about $14 trillion. CITs are described as rising in retirement, while tokenized shares are only emerging.
- That placement supports the thesis that advisors still have to master today’s wrappers while building a bridge toward post-wrapper structures such as tokenized shares.

## Applications

Advisors use CITs as a retirement-channel wrapper inside the current wrapper landscape, alongside mutual funds and ETFs, while they prepare for structures such as [[Tokenized T-Bills]]. The talk places CIT growth in that present landscape. It does not assign CITs a distinct tax-alpha mechanic. Tax conversion of investment alpha is discussed at the level of [[Wrappers]], including the [[Dual Share Class]] case, rather than as a CIT-specific design.
