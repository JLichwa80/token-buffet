# Partisanship, Populism, and Protectionism

## Definition

Partisanship, Populism, and Protectionism (often referred to as the "Three P's" or "the three forces") is an analytical framework used to evaluate and anticipate United States tax and fiscal policy. It posits that modern tax legislation is primarily driven by three political dynamics rather than coherent, modeled economic theory: partisan alignment, populist political rhetoric that erodes the tax base, and protectionist trade measures applied to fiscal problems.

## Key Characteristics

- **Partisanship Driving Division:** Major tax legislation passes almost exclusively along strict party-line votes, creating sharp policy swings depending on which party holds unified government, while genuine bipartisanship primarily manifests as deficit-financed spending.
- **Populism Eroding the Tax Base:** Low policy expertise combined with electoral politics favors catchy, slogan-based exemptions (such as "no tax on tips, overtime, or senior income" or "tax the rich") over sound, modeled reforms, progressively shrinking the tax base.
- **Protectionism as a Fiscal Tool:** Reliance on historic and elevated tariff levels by executive administrations as an alternative mechanism to address fiscal and trade questions.
- **Diagnostic Priority:** Serves as a primary diagnostic lens requiring analysts to identify which of the three political forces motivates a proposal before evaluating its substantive economic merits.

## Applications

- **Tax Policy Analysis:** Evaluating new congressional or executive tax proposals by diagnosing the underlying political pressures before modeling their economic impacts.
- **Legislative Risk Assessment:** Forecasting policy stability, longevity, and reversal risks across election cycles based on whether legislation was enacted through narrow partisan reconciliation versus consensus.
- **Corporate Fiscal Planning:** Enabling corporate strategy and finance teams to organize disjointed headlines and prepare scenario plans for tax rate shifts, base narrowing, and tariff exposures.
