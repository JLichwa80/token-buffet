# Bond Tax Loss Harvesting

## Definition

Bond tax loss harvesting is an active fixed income investment strategy that involves selling depreciated debt securities to realize capital losses and immediately redeploying the capital into suitable replacement bonds. Unlike equity tax loss harvesting, which typically defers capital gains tax liabilities to the future, bond tax loss harvesting can achieve permanent tax avoidance when replacement securities purchased above par naturally amortize their premium down to par value over maturity without generating taxable capital gains.

## Key Characteristics

- **Permanent Tax Avoidance Potential**: Allocating harvested proceeds into replacement bonds trading at a premium allows the bond premium to amortize down to par value over its remaining maturity, permanently eliminating taxable capital gains that usually accompany deferral strategies.
- **Dynamic Cost Basis**: Continuous regular issuance of new debt across corporate and municipal markets prevents fixed income cost bases from ossifying, providing recurring tax-loss harvesting opportunities over time.
- **Capital Loss Realization**: Capital losses realized from fixed income sales can offset taxable capital gains across other investment assets (including equities) and reduce ordinary taxable income up to statutory limits.
- **Immediate Reinvestment**: Proceeds are rapidly reinvested into alternative fixed income instruments to maintain continuous yield, portfolio duration, credit quality, and cash-flow characteristics without violating wash-sale rules.

## Applications

- **Systematic Tax Alpha Generation**: Systematically offsetting realized capital gains from broader portfolio rebalancing or equity liquidation events through coordinated fixed income loss capture.
- **Fixed Income Rebalancing**: Upgrading credit quality or adjusting duration and sector exposure while turning unrealized paper losses into realized tax shields.
- **High-Net-Worth Portfolio Optimization**: Enhancing after-tax yields and net total return for taxable accounts by exploiting amortizable bond premiums and continuous debt issuance cycles.
