# At-risk limitations

## Definition

At-risk limitations are the tax constraint that, on this panel, used to keep [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] from working for individuals. The strategy is described as an old technique whose new feature is that it now sits in vehicles individuals can consume, without those limitations. The source does not restate the statute or explain how at-risk amounts are computed. Its point is practical: once the limitation is out of the way, substantial and persistent capital losses from the long-short can shelter gains elsewhere in the portfolio.

## Key Characteristics

- They are presented as the constraint that historically blocked individual use of tax-aware long-short loss utilization.
- The claimed change is the vehicle, not a new quantitative engine: wrappers individuals can hold are said to operate without the limitation.
- [[Single-member series LLC]] structures and separately managed accounts are the wrappers named for enabling loss utilization without at-risk limitations.
- With the limitation out of the way, persistent capital losses can shelter other portfolio gains, including gains recognized as variable prepaid forwards mature.
- The source does not define the at-risk amount or how the limitation is calculated.

## Applications

The panel uses the absence of at-risk limitations to explain why an ETS-style playbook is usable for taxable clients. Separately managed accounts and a [[Single-member series LLC]] are said to let individuals consume [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] allocations and use the resulting capital losses against gains elsewhere in the portfolio, including gains that appear as variable prepaid forwards mature. That vehicle change is presented as the development that makes the playbook available, rather than any change to the quantitative engine.
