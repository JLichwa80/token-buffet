# Rebalancing alpha

## Definition

Rebalancing alpha refers to the excess return generated from a systematic, rules-based contrarian rebalancing process when asset prices overshoot their intrinsic economic scale and subsequently mean-revert, particularly when such price movements are unconfirmed by underlying business fundamentals.

## Key Characteristics

- **Contrarian Mechanism**: Systematically trims positions following unconfirmed price surges ("taking gains") and increases exposure to assets experiencing severe price declines absent fundamental deterioration ("buying discounts").
- **Driven by Mean Reversion**: Exploits market overshooting and subsequent mean reversion rather than pure fundamental security selection.
- **Shared Underlying Engine**: Functions as the primary performance driver across non-cap-weighted strategies, including equal weight and fundamental weighting.
- **Cost and Tax Sensitivity**: Subject to substantial return erosion from turnover, transaction costs, management fees, and tax consequences (e.g., in equal-weight strategies, estimated at ~1.1% gross, ~0.4% net of fees, ~0.1% after tax, and negative post-liquidation).
- **Theoretical Basis**: Builds upon Harry Markowitz's extended framework pairing portfolio diversification directly with mean reversion.

## Applications

- **Fundamental Indexing**: Dynamically realigning portfolio holdings back to fundamental weights (e.g., sales, cash flow, book value, dividends) to capture mean reversion when market prices detach from economic scale.
- **Equal-Weight Strategies**: Periodically resetting assets to equal target weights to capture the rebalancing premium across constituents.
- **Rules-Based Rebalancing Frameworks**: Structuring contrarian cash-flow or calendar-driven rebalancing bands to harvest volatility without engaging in subjective market timing.
