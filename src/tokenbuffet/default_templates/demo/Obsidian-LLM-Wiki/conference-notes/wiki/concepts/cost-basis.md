# Cost Basis

## Definition

Cost basis represents the original purchase price or acquisition value of securities held in an investment portfolio. It serves as the baseline reference value against which capital appreciation, depreciation, and future realized gains and losses are calculated for accounting and tax purposes.

## Key Characteristics

- **Benchmark for Realized Outcomes**: Acts as the foundation against which gains or losses are recognized upon the sale or disposition of a security.
- **Replenishment via Position Turnover**: In actively managed or long-short portfolios, creating new positions introduces holdings with updated market-rate cost basis.
- **Valuation Reset**: New positions enter at prevailing market prices rather than legacy appreciated values, avoiding embedded unrealized gains.
- **Mitigation of Portfolio Ossification**: The continuous addition of fresh cost basis securities keeps the portfolio dynamic, preventing it from locking up due to prohibitive tax friction.

## Applications

- **Tax-Loss Harvesting**: Securities with fresh, unappreciated cost basis offer immediate sensitivity to market downturns, providing prime candidates for tax-loss harvesting should their market value fall below acquisition price.
- **Tax-Aware Long-Short Investing**: Strategically deploying capital into new positions replenishes high/fresh cost basis stock, optimizing after-tax returns and sustaining ongoing tax alpha across multi-year investment horizons.
- **Capital Gains Management**: Allows portfolio managers to trace specific lot acquisitions to minimize tax liabilities when unwinding or rebalancing positions.
