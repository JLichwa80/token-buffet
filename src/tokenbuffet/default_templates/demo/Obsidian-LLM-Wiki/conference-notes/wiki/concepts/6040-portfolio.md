# 60/40 portfolio

## Definition

A classic asset allocation framework and benchmark consisting of 60% equities and 40% fixed income. It serves as a foundational baseline designed to balance capital appreciation from stocks with income generation and capital preservation from bonds.

## Key Characteristics

- **Standard Baseline**: Widely used across wealth management as the reference target allocation for moderate-risk investors.
- **Vulnerability to Drift**: Market movements cause portfolio weights to shift away from the target baseline (e.g., drifting from 60/40 to 61/39 or 66.5%), introducing unintended risk and factor exposures.
- **Tax Drag Sensitivity**: Conventional rebalancing through taxable liquidations of appreciated equity portions generates significant capital gains tax drag.
- **Rebalancing Testing Ground**: Acts as the primary case study for optimizing rebalancing techniques, including soft rebalancing via new cash flows and mathematical trigger-point thresholds.
- **Mathematical Rebalancing Threshold**: The optimal math-based rebalancing trigger point occurs at roughly 6.5% drift (a 66.5% equity weight), beneath which trading friction and tax costs outweigh tracking error mitigation benefits.
## Applications

- **Asset Allocation Benchmarking**: Providing an institutional standard to measure multi-asset portfolio performance and risk-adjusted returns.
- **Tax-Aware Rebalancing Protocols**: Implementing cash-flow routing and trigger-based rebalancing strategies to defend target allocations without incurring unnecessary taxable capital gains.
- **Risk Management**: Mitigating equity drawdown risk while dampening tracking error through systematic fixed income integration.
