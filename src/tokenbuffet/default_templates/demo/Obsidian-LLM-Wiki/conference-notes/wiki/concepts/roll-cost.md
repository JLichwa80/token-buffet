## Description

After-tax investing is the practice of evaluating and selecting investments based on the wealth an investor retains after accounting for federal and state taxes on interest, dividends, and realized gains. The retained amount depends heavily on investment vehicle structure, underlying asset class characteristics, and statutory tax rates established by legislation such as the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act. Tax-advantaged implementation strategies exploit regulatory and policy nuances to harvest tax alpha, such as utilizing dual share class structures or systematic dividend avoidance mechanisms like proxy rotations. Systematic implementation requires monitoring frictional expenses—such as roll costs incurred when rotating between primary and proxy instruments around distribution dates—to ensure execution drag does not erode the tax benefits of converting ordinary income into long-term capital gains.
