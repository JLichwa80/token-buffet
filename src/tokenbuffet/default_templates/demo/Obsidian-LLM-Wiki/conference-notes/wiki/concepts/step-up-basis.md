# Step-Up Basis

## Definition

Step-up basis is the estate-tax rule under which unrealized gains can disappear for heirs at death because the inherited asset’s cost basis is reset. In the presentation it is a planning result worth treating as meaningful, if morbid: holding appreciated assets until death can avoid tax payments entirely. The talk gives it no statutory section number and no numerical example.

## Key Characteristics

- Cost basis resets at death, so gains that were never realized during the owner’s life need not be recognized by the heirs.
- The speaker treats that reset as a meaningful planning achievement and compresses the incentive into the phrase “race to die.”
- It appears in the same terminology list as 351, 1031, [[AMT]], and in-kind redemption, as part of a jumble of rules written by different Congresses at different times.
- The talk uses it to show that tax character and timing, not only pre-tax return, shape portfolios.
- The source does not assign a code section or walk through a dollar example.

## Applications

- Estate and portfolio planning that keeps appreciated assets in place until death so the basis reset, rather than a lifetime sale, determines the tax result.
- Explaining why investors may defer realization: the “race to die” is a race to deny the tax authority any payment on those gains.
- Framing [[Tax Alpha]] as more than pre-tax performance. When the basis changes with the owner’s death, the character and timing of tax can dominate the outcome of holding the asset.
- Reading the rule as one fragment in a code assembled across Congresses, alongside 351, 1031, [[AMT]], and in-kind redemption, rather than as a single coordinated regime.
