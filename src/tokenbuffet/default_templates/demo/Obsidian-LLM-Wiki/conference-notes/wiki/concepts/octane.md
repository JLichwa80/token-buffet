# Octane

## Definition

Octane is the panel’s term for the leverage and active risk at which a tax-aware long-short is run. It is an explicit setting—“leverage/active risk”—chosen so the strategy can free cash and realize capital losses on a schedule that lines up with variable prepaid forwards maturing on successive January 1 dates.

## Key Characteristics

- Octane is the leverage and active-risk dial on the long-short, stated as such rather than left as a metaphor.
- Higher octane can free more cash or generate more losses; lower octane is for clients less comfortable with the extra risk.
- In the ladder design, the level is set so loss realization lines up with forwards that mature on successive January 1 dates.
- It connects the long-short to the forward ladder and is chosen separately from whether to use a variable prepaid forward at all.
- The level is tailored to the client. The structure is not run as one standard long-short.

## Applications

Advisors set octane when they build a ladder that combines immediate sales, variable prepaid forwards settling on January 1 across years, and a matched tax-aware long-short. The practical choice is the leverage and active-risk level that either frees cash or maximizes loss generation so taxes can be shielded as each forward matures. Clients who want more liquidity or more scheduled losses are run at a higher octane. Clients who are less comfortable with that risk are run at a lower octane, as part of matching the structure to the client.
