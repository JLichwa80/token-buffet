# Structural Alpha

## Definition

Structural alpha is the panel’s term for after-tax outperformance that comes from where an investment sits, rather than from a higher pre-tax return. In [[Private Placement Life Insurance]], removing tax drag is what produces the edge: a 10 percent IRR is illustrated as turning $10 million into roughly $125 million over the horizon, against about $50 million in a taxable account, or roughly 335 basis points of annualized IRR. The same idea covers persistent long-short losses and tax-free compounding that keep working for decades. Asset location is part of the definition: private credit, high-turnover funds, and public strategies that would otherwise distribute taxable income are treated as especially well suited to the policy.

## Key Characteristics

- The uplift is after-tax. It does not require a higher pre-tax return than the same strategy held in a taxable account.
- The illustrated case is a 10 percent IRR on $10 million, growing to roughly $125 million inside the policy versus about $50 million if taxed, or about 335 basis points of annualized IRR.
- The edge compounds for decades through tax-free accumulation and through persistent losses from [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]].
- Private credit, high-turnover funds, and public strategies that distribute taxable income are the holdings the panel identifies as the best fit.
- The action is a reallocation of those income-heavy and high-turnover strategies into [[Private Placement Life Insurance]] to capture the uplift.

## Applications

- Place income-heavy private credit and high-turnover strategies inside [[Private Placement Life Insurance]] so distributions and short-term gains are not taxed along the way.
- Hold public strategies that would otherwise throw off taxable income in the policy, including during turbulent periods when distributions would otherwise drag on the account.
- Pair the location choice with the [[ETS Playbook]]: long-short losses and tax-free compounding are described as an edge that compounds for decades.
- Use the illustrated 335 basis point IRR gap as the planning comparison between the policy and a taxable account when deciding which sleeve to move.
