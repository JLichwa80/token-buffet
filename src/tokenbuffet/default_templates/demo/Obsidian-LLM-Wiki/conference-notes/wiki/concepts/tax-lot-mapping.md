# Tax Lot Mapping

## Definition

Tax lot mapping is the operational accounting and custodial process carried out following a [[Section 351 Exchange]]. In this workflow, the original acquisition dates, holding periods, and cost basis figures from contributed individual securities are directly mapped and ported over to the corresponding newly issued exchange-traded fund (ETF) shares. This ensures complete continuity of the client's tax history and facilitates the lawful deferral of embedded capital gains liabilities.

## Key Characteristics

- **Cost Basis Preservation**: Inherits the exact cost basis of contributed assets rather than stepping up or resetting the basis to current market value.
- **Holding Period Continuity**: Tacks the holding period of the original securities onto the new ETF shares, maintaining eligibility for long-term capital gains treatment.
- **Custodial Execution**: Operates at the brokerage or institutional custodian level through specialized accounting ledgers and lot-allocation systems.
- **Tax-Deferred Structural Alignment**: Acts as the operational backbone for Section 351 transactions by ensuring tax reporting reflects non-recognition treatment under Internal Revenue Code provisions.

## Applications

- **Custom ETF Conversions**: Executing bespoke single-stock or concentrated portfolio transitions into ETF vehicles without triggering immediate taxable events.
- **Concentrated Wealth De-risking**: Structuring transitions for high-net-worth individuals holding legacy low-basis shares into diversified ETF baskets.
- **Custodial Asset Reconciliation**: Ensuring accurate reporting on Form 1099-B and internal cost-basis books across retail and institutional brokerages.
