# Cash-flow rebalancing

## Definition

Cash-flow rebalancing is a tax-aware portfolio management technique where routine contributions and withdrawals are directed toward underweight and overweight assets respectively, and combined with tax-loss harvesting to realign portfolio targets without triggering unnecessary taxable sales of appreciated assets.

## Key Characteristics

- **Tax-Aware Adjustments**: Prioritizes new cash inflows and distributions to adjust exposures rather than selling existing appreciated holdings.
- **Systematic Loss Harvesting**: Integrates opportunistic loss harvesting alongside regular cash flows to offset gains.
- **Forecast-Free Implementation**: Serves as a repeatable mechanical lever to generate tax alpha without relying on market timing or return forecasts.
- **Execution Sensitivity**: Generates positive tax alpha when executed methodically, but risks negative tax alpha if realized gains are triggered through sloppy order routing or timing.
- **Wrapper Alignment**: Pairs with a structural preference for tax-efficient investment vehicles and wrappers when underlying economic exposure is identical.
- **Natural Cash-Flow Redirection**: Systematically routes organic portfolio cash flows—such as equity dividends, bond coupons, and maturing principal—across asset sleeves to purchase underweight holdings without triggering realization events.
- **Empirical Drag Reduction**: Cuts rebalancing tax friction by roughly two-thirds, reducing typical annual drag from approximately 24 basis points to around 8 basis points in an average portfolio.
## Applications

- Rebalancing taxable private wealth or institutional portfolios where realized capital gains face substantial tax drag.
- Deploying recurring client deposits into underweight portfolio sleeves while funding withdrawal requests from overweight or loss-generating positions.
- Maintaining disciplined target weights alongside systematic indexing strategies without incurring ongoing liquidation penalties.
- Directing incoming cash flows (such as dividends hitting an equity sleeve) into underweight asset classes (like bonds in a drifting 60/40 allocation) to nudge weights back toward target without liquidating stocks.
