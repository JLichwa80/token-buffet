# Protectionism

## Definition

Protectionism refers to economic policies and proposals that utilize tariffs, quotas, and other trade barriers to restrict foreign imports. In the context of fiscal policy, it describes the deployment of trade barriers not merely for domestic market shielding, but as an ostensible revenue or fiscal instrument—one that risks introducing broad economic distortions rather than resolving underlying structural budget deficits.

## Key Characteristics

- **Tariff-Driven Engine**: Relies on historic tariff levels and trade restrictions as its primary policy and political instrument.
- **Fiscal Distortions**: Framed as a revenue-raising mechanism, yet tends to distort supply chains, market prices, and broader economic activity.
- **Downstream Burden**: Sits alongside party-line tax structures and populist exemptions as a political force that poses potential collateral damage to businesses and household finances.
- **Truncated Operational Detail**: Often presented with strong political rhetoric but fewer granular operational solutions for addressing long-term fiscal imbalance.

## Applications

- **Trade and Import Barriers**: Implementation of blanket or targeted tariffs on foreign goods to deter external competition and raise customs duties.
- **Fiscal Proposal Labeling**: Serves as a category in policy playbooks for initiatives whose political mechanism is trade restriction framed as revenue enhancement.
- **Economic & Investment Risk Analysis**: Used by businesses and households to assess margin compression, supply chain realignment, and policy risk stemming from cross-border trade intervention.
