# Liquidity Budgets

## Definition

Liquidity Budgets are a financial strategy used in wealth management to identify, forecast, and schedule anticipated client cash inflows and outflows throughout the year to optimize after-tax portfolio implementation and rebalancing.

## Key Characteristics

- **Proactive Cash Flow Planning**: Accounts for expected deposits and withdrawals in advance rather than treating liquidity events as ad-hoc disruptions.
- **Empirically Grounded**: Bridges the gap with academic tax-loss harvesting models (which often assume static portfolios) by addressing the 60% to 70% of taxable accounts that experience real-world annual cash movements.
- **Tax-Sensitive Execution**: Coordinates with portfolio tax budgets to fund distributions without realizing unnecessary taxable capital gains.
- **Drift Absorption**: Directs incoming cash contributions strategically toward underweight asset classes or lots to resolve portfolio drift tax-free.

## Applications

- **Tax-Efficient Withdrawal Management**: Timing and lot-selecting distributions from taxable accounts by pre-budgeting cash needs across specific tax periods.
- **Inflow Allocation & Rebalancing**: Directing new cash deposits into drift-correcting positions to avoid selling appreciated assets.
- **Wealth Advisor Workflows**: Structuring annual client reviews and cash schedule agreements to coordinate portfolio management with personal liquidity timelines.
