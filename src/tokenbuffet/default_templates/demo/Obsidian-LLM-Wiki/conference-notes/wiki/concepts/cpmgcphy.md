# CPMG/CPHY

## Definition

CPMG/CPHY is a bond strategy summarized by the speaker as income without income. It uses 1940 Act distribution rules and ETF accounting so that daily income accrues in net asset value (NAV), while the holder rotates out of the distributing fund before the ex-date and thereby avoids receiving the ordinary-income dividend.

## Key Characteristics

- NAV on bonds such as AGG or high yield accrues daily income, and tax is described as arising only when a dividend is received.
- Before the ex-date the position rotates into a [[Proxy Fund]], stays out for about two days, then reverses, keeping price appreciation and avoiding the ordinary-income distribution.
- Claimed results are no 1099 capital-gains distribution, ordinary income avoided, and gains that can become offsettable long-term capital gains after a 366-day hold.
- Stated economics are a roll cost of about 1 basis point per month and an add-on fee of about 25 basis points.
- Treasury and BDC versions are described as weeks or months away.
- The pattern is set against the [[Sawtooth Pattern]] of scheduled dividends, with the aim of a smoother total return.

## Applications

The talk presents CPMG/CPHY as a way to hold bond exposure, including aggregate and high-yield ETFs, while limiting taxable distributions. A portfolio accrues income in NAV, exits into a proxy ETF for the short ex-date window, and returns afterward. The same rotation is described as extendable to Treasury and business-development-company (BDC) versions once those products are available. Advisors are urged to explain the strategy alongside [[wrapper]] and dual share classes when discussing [[Tax Alpha]] in taxable bond allocations.
