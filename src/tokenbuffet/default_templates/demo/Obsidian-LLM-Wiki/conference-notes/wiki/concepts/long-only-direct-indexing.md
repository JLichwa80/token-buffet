# Long-Only Direct Indexing

## Definition

Long-Only Direct Indexing is an investment portfolio archetype that directly holds individual benchmark securities (typically configured as 100% long and 0% short) to match the performance and risk profile of a target index, targeting a beta of approximately 1.0 while maintaining low uninformed tracking error to systematically harvest tax losses and defer capital gains.

## Key Characteristics

- **Long-Only Replication**: Maintains a 100% long exposure without short positions, holding individual underlying securities rather than commingled index funds or ETFs.
- **Index Beta and Tracking Error Targets**: Targets a portfolio beta of ~1 to an underlying benchmark (such as the [[Russell 1000]]) while operating within tight tracking error parameters (e.g., ~1%).
- **Tax-Loss Harvesting & Gain Deferral**: Systematically sells declining securities to realize losses for tax offsets while deferring the realization of winning positions.
- **Gain Lock-Up / Ossification**: Over multi-year horizons, portfolios tend to experience portfolio ossification and gain lock-up as positions accumulate substantial unrealized gains, leading to higher percentage unrealized gains and diminished capacity to harvest further losses.

## Applications

- **Tax-Managed Wealth Management**: Utilized by taxable high-net-worth investors seeking broad market exposure with enhanced after-tax returns via ongoing loss harvesting.
- **Customized Benchmark Exposure**: Serving as an alternative to mutual funds or ETFs, enabling investors to reflect specific tilts, exclusions, or tax-transition mandates directly at the individual stock level.
