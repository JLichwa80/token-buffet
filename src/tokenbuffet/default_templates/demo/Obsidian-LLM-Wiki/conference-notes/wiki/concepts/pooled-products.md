# Pooled Products

## Definition

Pooled products are investment vehicles—such as mutual funds and other commingled funds—where investor capital is aggregated into a single shared portfolio. In the context of after-tax wealth management, they represent a primary source of tax drag because taxable income and capital gain distributions are passed through to shareholders regardless of whether an individual investor sold shares or realized gains.

## Key Characteristics

- **Detached Tax Realization**: Generates unpredictable capital gains and taxable income distributions driven by fund-level transactions and external investor flows rather than the client's own trading behavior.
- **Lack of Granular Control**: Contrasts with direct ownership of securities, preventing advisors from implementing customized tax-loss harvesting, individualized security restrictions, factor tilts, or bespoke cash-flow management.
- **Look-Through Complexity**: Requires complex look-through procedures that complicate portfolio optimization, asset allocation modeling, and technology infrastructure.
- **Lock-In Tendency**: Legacy pooled vehicles with substantial embedded capital gains create barriers to exit, contributing to an ossified portfolio because liquidating the position triggers immediate tax liabilities.

## Applications

- **Tax Drag Analysis**: Evaluating the drag imposed by pass-through distributions on after-tax compound returns compared to direct indexing or separately managed accounts (SMAs).
- **Tax-Aware Transition Analysis**: Formulating transition strategies that weigh the friction and cost of liquidating legacy pooled funds against the long-term benefits of moving to customized, directly held security structures.
- **Portfolio Unlocking**: Addressing embedded gain barriers in client onboarding to transition legacy portfolios toward tax-managed accounts without incurring punitive one-time tax hits.
