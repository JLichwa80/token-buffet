# Realized Gains and Losses

## Definition

Realized Gains and Losses refers to the recognized capital gains and losses resulting from asset sales, liquidity events, or portfolio reallocations. Within tax-aware wealth management, it represents the third of Aaron's five tax-drag problems, emphasizing that generating capital losses has limited standalone utility unless actively coordinated and paired against realized capital gains across a client's broader household balance sheet.

## Key Characteristics

- **Pairing Dependency**: Harvested losses act as an inventory that delivers economic value primarily when matched against realized taxable gains.
- **Household-Level Scope**: Realization management extends beyond isolated account mechanics, functioning as a holistic household planning discipline.
- **Transition Alignment**: Directly integrates with portfolio transitions, where lot-level harvesting generates the loss capacity required to offset gains incurred when reallocating toward target asset destinations.
- **Strategic Utility**: Mitigates tax drag by ensuring that capital gains from liquidity events or strategic divestments are systematically neutralized.

## Applications

- **Portfolio Transitions**: Offsetting embedded capital gains incurred while shifting legacy portfolios into target asset allocations without incurring substantial tax penalties.
- **Liquidity Event Planning**: Coordinating with clients facing concentrated stock sales, private business exits, or other major taxable capital gain events to deploy accumulated loss inventory.
- **Tax-Aware Long-Short Strategies**: Aligning short-side loss generation with a concrete realization roadmap to ensure harvested losses are meaningfully utilized rather than left stranded.
