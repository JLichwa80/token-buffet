# Tokenized T-Bills

## Definition

Tokenized T-bills are Treasury bills issued or held in token form, presented as the next wrapper-and-operations step after [[Dual Share Class]]. Tokenizing Treasury bills and other assets is described as removing counterparties and operational layers, improving NAV efficiency, allowing fractional spreads, and supporting 24/7 trading. The same source frames the change as a way to cut the operational cost of traditional ETFs and to show that saving in NAV.

## Key Characteristics

- Positioned after dual share classes as a further wrapper and operations change, extending tokenization beyond T-bills to other assets and, in the speaker’s view, to ETFs.
- Said to remove counterparties and operational layers and to improve NAV efficiency.
- Traditional ETFs at scale are cited at roughly 3–6 basis points of operational cost. Tokenization is supposed to reduce that cost, with the saving reflected in NAV.
- Trading spreads are described as falling from basis points toward fractions of a penny, framed as breaking the penny spread.
- Supports, and is expected to include, 24/7 trading. The speaker allows that round-the-clock trading may not always be a good idea, but expects it to exist.
- A recent executive order directing the [[SEC]] and the [[CFTC]] to create tokenization schemes is cited as the policy tailwind. [[FM Investments]] is described as filing a second application the next day.
- Firms are advised to prepare custody, pricing, trading windows, and client communication before the structure is in wide use.

## Applications

- Treasury sleeves and cash management, where spread compression and continuous liquidity are expected to have clear value.
- Fixed-income operations: custody setup, pricing, defined trading windows, and client communication for tokenized T-bills.
- Product filings that follow regulatory tokenization schemes, including the second application attributed to [[FM Investments]].
- Client explanations of how dual share classes, tokenization, and related income strategies fit together in a portfolio.
