# Washington State capital gains

## Definition

Washington State capital gains is the panel’s term for Washington’s capital-gains and income-tax rules as they apply to harvested losses. Under those rules, short-term losses cannot offset long-term capital gains, and loss offsetting and charitable deductions are narrower than under federal and California norms. The same losses can still be useful on a federal return, so state residence changes whether a harvested loss actually shields a gain.

## Key Characteristics

- Short-term losses cannot be used in Washington against long-term capital gains, which limits tax-aware long-short at the state level.
- Loss offsetting and charitable deductions are limited relative to federal and California treatment.
- Federal techniques, including variable prepaid forward deferral, can remain effective on the federal return.
- Washington clients need extra design and, in some cases, different tools than the standard federal playbook.
- The constraint matters at maturity: state residence can decide whether harvested losses shield a forward when it settles.

## Applications

Advisors use this terminology when a client lives in Washington and a plan depends on loss harvesting to offset long-term gains. In the [[ETS Playbook]], a [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] allocation is often sized to a [[Laddered Diversification Framework]] so losses are available as each [[Variable Prepaid Forward]] matures. That match works less well for Washington state tax, even when the federal result is intact. Planning then keeps the federal benefit, redesigns the state result, and considers other tools. The panel’s description of the room reaction is a collective “oof” once the constraint was spelled out.
