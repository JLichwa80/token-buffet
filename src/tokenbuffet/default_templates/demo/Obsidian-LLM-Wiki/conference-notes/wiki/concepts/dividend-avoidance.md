# dividend avoidance

## Definition

Dividend avoidance is a systematic trading strategy designed to evade taxable cash dividend distributions while retaining continuous economic exposure to an underlying asset or asset class. By temporarily rotating or swapping out of a target security into a closely correlated [[proxy fund]] immediately prior to its ex-dividend date, an investor eliminates ordinary taxable dividend income. Once the ex-dividend date passes and the cash distribution is stripped from the target security's price, the original holding is restored, effectively transforming taxable income drag into deferred capital growth.

## Key Characteristics

- **Pre-Ex-Date Rotation**: Systematically divests or substitutes the dividend-paying instrument immediately prior to the ex-dividend date.
- **Continuous Market Exposure**: Employs a highly correlated [[proxy fund]] to track underlying benchmark performance and minimize tracking error while avoiding cash distributions.
- **Tax Arbitrage & Optimization**: Converts high-drag ordinary income distributions into favorable, unrealized long-term or short-term capital appreciation, generating [[tax alpha]].
- **Curve Smoothing**: Eliminates the mechanical drop and periodic [[sawtooth pattern]] characteristic of high-yielding fixed income and dividend-focused funds.

## Applications

- **Taxable Portfolio Management**: Utilized by high-net-worth and taxable institutional investors seeking yield asset exposure (e.g., fixed income, tokenized debt, REITs) without incurring regular income tax liabilities.
- **Systematic Income Stripping**: Implemented within algorithmic and quantitative wrapper structures (such as [[concepts/cpmgcphy|cpmgcphy]]) to automate fund swaps around distribution schedules.
- **Benchmark Tracking Optimization**: Applied to broad bond or equity allocations (such as [[AGG]]) to mirror core index returns while sidestepping yield leakage.
