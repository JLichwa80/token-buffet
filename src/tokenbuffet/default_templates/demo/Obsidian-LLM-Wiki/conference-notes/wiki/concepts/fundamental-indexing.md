# Fundamental Indexing

## Definition

Fundamental Indexing (RAFI) is an index construction methodology introduced in 2004 that weights securities according to measures of their real economic footprint rather than their market capitalization. By calculating company size using core accounting metrics, it breaks the structural link between portfolio weight and market price, avoiding the tendency of cap-weighted indexes to overweight overvalued assets.

## Key Characteristics

- **Economic Footprint Weighting**: Portfolio weights are calculated using fundamental accounting measures, typically including adjusted sales, cash flow, dividends plus share buybacks, and book value.
- **Severing Weight from Price**: Disconnects security weighting from market price fluctuations, preventing automatic overweighting of overvalued equities and underweighting of undervalued ones.
- **Structural Value Tilt**: Inherently tilts the portfolio toward value characteristics relative to traditional market-cap benchmarks.
- **Systematic Rebalancing**: Generates excess returns (rebalancing alpha) by systematically trimming securities that have appreciated relative to their fundamentals and adding to those that have depreciated.

## Applications

- **Core Equity Indexing**: Utilized as a smart beta or alternative index foundation for passive core equity exposure.
- **Systematic Value Investing**: Deployed as an objective, rules-based strategy to capture value premiums without requiring active fundamental stock picking.
- **Benchmark Alternative**: Serves as an alternative benchmark to traditional market-cap weighted indexes for assessing fund performance and factor exposure.
