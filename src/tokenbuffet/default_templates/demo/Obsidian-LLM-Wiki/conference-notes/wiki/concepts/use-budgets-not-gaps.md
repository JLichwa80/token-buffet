# Use Budgets, Not Gaps

## Definition

"Use budgets, not gaps" is a portfolio transition and tax management framework emphasizing structured, proactive limits across four operational dimensions—gain, risk, time, and liquidity—rather than relying on reactive target gap measurements. Developed as a principle for high-net-worth wealth management, it frames portfolio constraints as structured client-conversation and decision-making tools.

## Key Characteristics

- **Proactive Early-Year Alignment**: Assumes high-net-worth clients, financial advisors, and accountants have sufficient visibility into the annual tax profile to establish explicit transition parameters as early as January.
- **Four Dimensional Budgets**:
  - **Gain Budget**: Defines the total acceptable realized capital gain for the tax year (ranging from zero to a positive figure when offset by available loss inventory).
  - **Risk Budget**: Dictates acceptable tracking error or drift tolerance away from the benchmark target model.
  - **Time Budget**: Establishes the expected schedule and pace required to migrate legacy holdings to the target allocation.
  - **Liquidity Budget**: Accounts for planned intra-year cash inflows and withdrawals, reflecting real-world portfolio friction where 60–70% of individual accounts experience net cash flows annually.
- **Constraints as Communication Levers**: Treats portfolio management constraints not merely as algorithmic bounds, but as transparent governance levers for client discovery, education, and expectation setting.

## Applications

- **Tax-Aware Portfolio Onboarding & Transitions**: Managing the rebalancing of appreciated legacy assets into target investment models over multi-year horizons without triggering excessive taxable gains.
- **Annual Tax Planning & Advisory Dialogues**: Structuring formal Q1 planning meetings between clients, CPAs, and wealth managers to synchronize gain tolerances with anticipated tax deductions or losses.
- **Dynamic Cash Flow Integration**: Adjusting transition timelines and tax-loss harvesting models to accommodate regular contributions or capital withdrawals rather than assuming static, closed portfolios.
