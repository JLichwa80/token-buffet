# Pre-Tax Product Selection

## Definition

Pre-Tax Product Selection is a traditional wealth management paradigm centered on constructing portfolios through pre-tax capital market expectations and standard model products rather than customized, client-level after-tax implementation. In this framework, advisors rely on static, one-size-fits-all allocations and quarterly rebalancing, offering undifferentiated product selection that does not account for complex household tax situations or existing legacy assets.

## Key Characteristics

- **Product and Model Centric**: Emphasizes assembling portfolios around "best-in-class" products and pre-tax capital market forecasts rather than after-tax outcomes.
- **One-Size-Fits-All Execution**: Deploys standardized model allocations broadly across clients, routinely rebalancing on fixed quarterly schedules.
- **Commoditization and Low Differentiation**: Offers simple asset allocations (such as generic ETF mixes) that clients can increasingly assemble on their own, eroding the traditional basis of advisor value.
- **Inability to Handle Legacy Asset Friction**: Struggles to efficiently accommodate complex real-world client holdings, including concentrated stock positions, legacy mutual funds, trusts, retirement accounts, donor-advised funds, and liquidity events.

## Applications

- **Legacy Portfolio Construction**: Used historically by wealth managers to deploy centralized, model-based asset allocations across broad client bases.
- **Commoditized Asset Allocation**: Applied in turnkey, automated, or off-the-shelf investment platforms delivering generic pre-tax investment exposures.
- **Comparative Baseline**: Serves as the legacy operational model that modern after-tax wealth platforms contrast against when demonstrating the value of tax alpha and customized account implementation.
