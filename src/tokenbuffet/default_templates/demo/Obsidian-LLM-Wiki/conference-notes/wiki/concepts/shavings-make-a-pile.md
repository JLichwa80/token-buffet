# shavings make a pile

## Definition

"Shavings make a pile" is a wealth management philosophy asserting that small, systematic, and continuous tax efficiencies harvested across all portfolio sleeves compound into substantial wealth preservation and outperformance over long investment horizons. Rather than relying on sporadic, high-risk tactical wins, this framework focuses on eliminating routine friction points such as tax drag and transaction costs.

## Key Characteristics

- **Compounding Marginal Gains**: Operates on the premise that minor basis-point improvements (e.g., 36 bps in bond tax harvesting, 16 bps in disciplined rebalancing) aggregate into material portfolio alpha over time.
- **Holistic Integration**: Unifies distinct account sleeves—such as fixed income tax-loss harvesting and equity rebalancing—into a coordinated tax-efficient structure rather than managing them in isolation.
- **Systematic Friction Reduction**: Prioritizes regular, rules-based mitigation of tax leakage and transaction costs over market timing or directional bets.
- **Multi-Cycle Focus**: Designed to keep maximum capital deployed and working uninterrupted through all phases of market and interest-rate cycles.

## Applications

- **Unified Fixed Income & Equity Rebalancing**: Coordinating bond tax-loss harvesting programs with portfolio rebalancing protocols to offset realized gains dynamically.
- **Advisor Practice Management**: Reorienting client reporting and wealth advisory services from individual security performance toward comprehensive, post-tax net compounding.
- **Transition and Asset Location Planning**: Systematically unwinding concentrated positions or reallocating across taxable and tax-advantaged accounts while minimizing one-off tax realization events.
