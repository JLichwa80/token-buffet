# adjusted basis

## Definition

Adjusted basis is the ongoing, dynamic cost basis of a fixed income instrument that adjusts systematically over its lifespan toward par value. In premium bonds, the purchase premium amortizes downward to par over time, preventing taxable capital gains from being realized upon maturity.

## Key Characteristics

- **Dynamic Amortization**: Systematically adjusts toward par value throughout the holding period rather than remaining fixed at the initial purchase price.
- **Premium Amortization**: For bonds purchased at a premium, amortizes downward to eliminate artificial capital gains at maturity.
- **Tax Avoidance Mechanism**: Serves as a foundational accounting mechanism enabling permanent tax avoidance in fixed income portfolio management.
- **Continuous Monitoring**: Requires active, ongoing tracking to maintain accurate cost basis and execute truly tax-aware management.

## Applications

- **Tax-Aware Fixed Income Management**: Accurately tracking basis to avoid unintended tax liabilities when replacement securities mature.
- **Tax-Advantaged Rebalancing**: Coordinating with tax loss harvesting to maximize after-tax returns without triggering unexpected capital gains.
- **Municipal Bond Portfolio Accounting**: Accurately reporting amortized premium values across state and federal tax-exempt instruments.
