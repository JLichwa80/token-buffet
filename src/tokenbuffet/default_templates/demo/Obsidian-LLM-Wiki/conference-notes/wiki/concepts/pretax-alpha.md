# Pretax Alpha

## Definition

Pretax alpha is the active excess return generated by an investment portfolio or strategy relative to its benchmark before accounting for tax liabilities. In active management, it reflects raw manager forecasting and stock selection skill before tax consequences, financing rates, and transaction frictions are applied.

## Key Characteristics

- **Benchmark-Relative Metric:** Measures performance in excess of a designated market index or benchmark prior to tax adjustments.
- **Dual Engine Function:** In tax-aware long-short architectures, it serves as a dual engine by building pretax wealth while simultaneously generating turnover and position creation necessary for tax-loss harvesting.
- **Friction Absorption:** Provides the necessary baseline excess return required to offset short-borrow costs, financing spreads, and trading execution fees.
- **Leverage and Position Dynamics:** Directly influences portfolio leverage ratios and triggers the position creation mechanism that unlocks subsequent tax alpha.

## Applications

- **Tax-Aware Long-Short Strategies:** Deployed as the core driver to fund ongoing portfolio rebalancing and facilitate systematic tax-loss generation.
- **Manager Performance Evaluation:** Utilized by institutional investors to evaluate gross active skill independently of an investor's specific marginal tax bracket.
- **Capital Budgeting & Friction Coverage:** Assessed to ensure an active trading strategy produces sufficient excess spread to absorb short financing and operational costs.
