# Fama–French alpha

## Definition

Fama–French alpha represents the factor-adjusted, or "true," excess return of an investment strategy after controlling for exposure to established systematic risk factors (such as market beta, size, and value). In empirical portfolio evaluation, it serves as the baseline measure of outperformance that sits independently of implementation frictions, execution costs, and tax effects.

## Key Characteristics

- **Factor-Adjusted Baseline**: Isolates structural outperformance from passive risk factor exposures, distinguishing genuine skill or strategy design from mere factor tilts.
- **Independent Layer in Total Return**: Functions as an additive layer of return alongside operational and implementation effects such as tax alpha and transaction drag.
- **Evaluation Differentiator**: Serves as the critical net edge when comparing strategies whose post-fee tax alphas converge or appear comparable.
- **Robustness Check**: Separates raw factor outperformance from the layered impacts of management fees, turnover costs, and tax liabilities that can otherwise erode or reverse paper gains in smart beta strategies.

## Applications

- **Strategy Performance Attribution**: Evaluating whether smart-beta or fundamental indexing strategies generate true factor-adjusted outperformance beyond simple capitalization-weighted benchmarks.
- **Layered Return Analysis**: Benchmarking gross strategy alpha against subsequent implementation layers—specifically measuring how much gross Fama–French alpha survives after accounting for fees, annual tax drag, and eventual post-liquidation taxes.
- **Product Comparison**: Comparing investment vehicles (e.g., active vs. passive or fundamental vs. cap-weighted strategies) to identify where the net investor edge originates.
