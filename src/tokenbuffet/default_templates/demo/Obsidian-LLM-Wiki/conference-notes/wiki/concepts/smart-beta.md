# Smart beta

## Definition

Smart beta is a family of rules-based portfolio construction strategies that systematically depart from traditional market-capitalization weighting. It encompasses approaches such as multifactor, equal weight, low volatility (or minimum variance), and fundamental indexation.

## Key Characteristics

- **Non-Cap-Weighted Construction**: Replaces or modifies market-cap weighting with alternative weighting schemes or systematic factor exposures.
- **Rules-Based & Systematic**: Employs transparent, disciplined indexing methodologies rather than discretionary stock picking.
- **Gross vs. Net vs. After-Tax Performance Drag**: Over approximately 25 years, the aggregate smart beta group delivers roughly 120 basis points per year gross of costs. In live funded accounts, fees reduce this edge to around 50 basis points net, and taxes can cause the category to trail standard benchmarks by about 30 basis points after tax.
- **Tax Sensitivity**: Conventional style and factor buckets (growth vs. value, large vs. small, momentum, quality) frequently fail to maintain positive excess return after taxes, often flipping a pre-tax edge into a net loss.
- **Fundamental Indexing Differentiation**: [[Fundamental Indexing]] stands out as an exception within the category, delivering approximately 2.8% annually gross and retaining an estimated 50 basis points of outperformance even after taxes and liquidation.

## Applications

- **Alternative Core Equity Allocation**: Used by institutional and retail investors seeking systematic factor tilts (value, quality, low volatility, size) without full active management fees.
- **Systematic Factor Harvesting**: Deploying rules-based factor strategies to target long-term risk premia while maintaining transparent governance.
- **Tax-Aware Portfolio Construction**: Evaluating and selecting smart beta implementations—such as [[Fundamental Indexing]]—that can sustain net positive alpha after accounting for turnover, transaction costs, and capital gains taxes.
