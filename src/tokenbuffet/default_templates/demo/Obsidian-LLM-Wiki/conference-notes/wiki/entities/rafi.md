# Equities

## Description

Equities are an asset class of ownership claims on businesses, usually held as common stock that gives the holder a residual claim on earnings and assets after creditors have been paid. Returns arrive as price changes and as dividends, and they rise or fall with company profits, market valuations, and the business cycle. Investors obtain that exposure by holding shares directly, by owning a professionally managed [[entities/mutual-fund|mutual-fund]], or by buying a single listed name such as [[entities/apple|apple]], whose exchange price is posted continuously and can be traded in small amounts. In a multi-asset allocation, equities function as the growth sleeve alongside income securities such as a [[entities/treasury-bond|treasury-bond]], and the mix is maintained by reinvestment of dividends, new contributions, and periodic rebalancing. Because tax is generally assessed when a share is sold, realized losses can offset taxable gains, and unrealized appreciation stays in the position until a later sale, which makes turnover a direct cost of managing an equity portfolio.
