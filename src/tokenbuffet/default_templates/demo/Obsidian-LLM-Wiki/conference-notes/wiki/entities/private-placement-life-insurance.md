# Private Placement Life Insurance

## Description

Private Placement Life Insurance (PPLI) is presented as the vehicle that addresses the back half of the concentrated-wealth tax problem after liquidity has already been created, often following a [[Variable Prepaid Forward]]. Inside the policy, investment returns compound without current tax. At death the distribution can be tax-free, and loans against the policy during life are described as tax-free. A case study invests $10 million over four years at a 10 percent IRR for 30 years and contrasts roughly $125 million inside PPLI with roughly $50 million in a taxable account, an annualized pickup of about 335 basis points that Speaker 4 calls [[Structural Alpha]]. Practical features cited include institutional pricing, skinny policies, no surrender charges, and liquidity that mirrors the underlying assets. The panel locates income-heavy private credit, high-turnover strategies that produce short-term gains, and even public strategies inside PPLI so taxable distributions are avoided during turbulent markets, placing the policy beside the [[ETS Playbook]] and [[Tax-Aware Long-Short]] as a destination for proceeds.

Technically, it is structured as a variable universal life insurance wrapper specifically tailored for high-net-worth investors seeking tax-efficient asset placement.
