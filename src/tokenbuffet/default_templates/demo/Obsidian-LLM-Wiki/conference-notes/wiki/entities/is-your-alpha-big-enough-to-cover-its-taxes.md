# Is Your Alpha Big Enough to Cover Its Taxes?

## Description

"Is Your Alpha Big Enough to Cover Its Taxes?" is a foundational 1993 research article co-authored by [[Rob Arnott]] and Tad Jordanrey that helped initiate a broader shift toward tax-aware investing. The paper posits that management fees represent only a fraction of total investment costs, demonstrating that investment taxes introduce substantial friction that can easily overwhelm active-management performance. Analyzing 25 years of returns across approximately 4,000 mutual funds tracked by [[Morningstar]], the authors found that while funds trailed their benchmarks by roughly 20 basis points before fees, that underperformance expanded to roughly 1 percent net of fees, 2.5 percent net of taxes, and approximately 3 percent per year after liquidation. In wealth management discussions, the paper is widely cited as early empirical evidence that [[Tax Drag]] routinely eliminates active returns in traditional [[Pooled Products]].
