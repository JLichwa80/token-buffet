# Riley

## Description

Riley is the widow of [[Herr Schmaltz]] and serves as a classic client archetype representing investors trapped in highly appreciated, concentrated stock positions without a viable traditional exit. In 2006, her financial advisor presented an ultimatum between selling her concentrated [[Wachovia]] position to face substantial capital gains taxes or holding the stock unconditionally. Riley chose to hold, but during the 2008 financial crisis, the position—originally valued at $50–$60 per share—plummeted and was liquidated at approximately $7 per share. [[Sam]] later reflected on Riley's case, wishing modern financial technology had existed then to implement a [[Tax-Neutral Transition]]. Financial advisors utilize Riley's cautionary experience as a conversation starter to introduce automated, tax-advantaged portfolio transition strategies to clients facing similar exposure.
