# Equities

## Description

Equities, also called stocks, are securities that represent an ownership stake in a company. Shareholders hold a residual claim on earnings and assets after creditors are paid, and they may receive dividends and voting rights according to the share class. Public equities trade on exchanges, so an investor can own a single issuer such as [[entities/apple|Apple]] or obtain diversified exposure through a pooled vehicle such as a [[entities/mutual-fund|Mutual Fund]]. Return comes from price change and from income distributed by the issuer. Those cash flows are not contractually fixed, which is why equities are generally more volatile than a high-grade [[entities/treasury-bond|Treasury Bond]] and are used as the growth allocation in a balanced portfolio. Issuers range from large listed companies to smaller firms, and the shares may be common or preferred. Over long periods, investors have used equities to seek growth that can outpace inflation, while accepting the risk of large drawdowns. The same shares can produce different after-tax results once dividends, realized gains, and the investor’s tax rates are taken into account. Portfolio decisions then cover how large the equity allocation should be, how concentrated the holdings are, and how often gains are realized. *(No source content available for this page)*
