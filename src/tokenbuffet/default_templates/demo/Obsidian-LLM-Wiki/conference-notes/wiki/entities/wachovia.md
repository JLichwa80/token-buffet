# Wachovia

## Description

Wachovia is the financial institution and bank stock serving as the central cautionary case study in [[Sam]]'s presentation on concentrated-wealth risks. Originally accumulated by her grandfather, [[Herr Schmaltz]], as a localized bank holding, the position evolved through successive waves of banking industry consolidation into a major concentrated stake in Wachovia. In 2006, his widow [[Riley]] faced a binary decision: liquidate the position and trigger severe capital gains liabilities, or hold the shares to defer tax realization. Following the choice to hold, shares that traded between $50 and $60 plummeted to $7 during the 2008 financial crisis, illustrating the perils of unhedged single-stock exposure without active transition planning.

A major American banking corporation, Wachovia collapsed and was acquired in the fallout of the 2008 financial crisis, leading to the eventual liquidation of the concentrated position at $7.
