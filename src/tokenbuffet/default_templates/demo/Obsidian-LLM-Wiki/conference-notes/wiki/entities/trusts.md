# Trusts

A trust is a fiduciary arrangement that allows a third party, or trustee, to hold and manage assets on behalf of beneficiaries, frequently used in high-net-worth estate planning. In the keynote presentation, trusts are identified as one of the key account structures that add layers of legal and tax complexity to client balance sheets. They possess distinct tax brackets, compressed rate schedules, and distribution rules that must be coordinated with personal taxable accounts. Effectively designing around trusts requires the advisor to act as an after-tax architect working in tandem with estate planning attorneys.

> "The industry was built around pre-tax portfolios, one-size-fits-all models, and broad product selection, but high-net-worth clients arrive with concentrated stock, legacy mutual funds, donor-advised funds, trusts, 529s, IRAs, business interests, and pending liquidity events." — [[sources/10-09-keynote_-after-tax-wealth-management-and-tax-stack-design-summary-1_d01a81]]
