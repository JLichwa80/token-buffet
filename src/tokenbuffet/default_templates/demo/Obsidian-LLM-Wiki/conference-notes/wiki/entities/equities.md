# Equities

## Description

Equities are securities that represent an ownership claim on a corporation. Buyers of common stock share in residual profits after lenders are paid, through dividends and through changes in the share price. Public equities trade on exchanges with visible prices, which lets investors rebalance, harvest losses, and measure risk directly in a way that is harder in many over-the-counter bond markets. In household portfolios they are the main growth allocation and sit beside fixed income such as [[entities/treasury-bond|Treasury Bond]]. Pooled products, including a [[entities/mutual-fund|Mutual Fund]], package many equities into one professionally managed vehicle. A concentrated position in a single issuer such as [[entities/apple|Apple]] can dominate both pretax risk and the tax bill when shares are sold. Equity cash flows are taxed as dividends and as realized capital gains, so two investors with the same pretax return can keep different amounts after tax. Holding period, tax lot selection, and loss harvesting decide how much of that pretax result remains. Federal rules on dividends and long-term gains, including the framework of the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act, change that after-tax outcome. Advisors compare equities with structured products and with bond allocations when they design an after-tax mix.
