# Congress

## Description
Congress is the United States legislative body that the presentation treats as the accidental architect of investor portfolios. The speaker argues that Congress gives capital-gains policy little bandwidth, that laws are written by different Congresses at different times, and that the resulting jumble of rules is where product designers find arbitrage. Structural constraints matter as much as intent: [[sunset-clause]] follow parliamentary procedure, and the speaker says most policies will sunset because sixty people in sixty percent of the House cannot agree. Electoral math is part of the same picture—about 200,000 votes typically send someone to Congress, and only about 18 districts often determine control—so taxes rarely stay at the top of the agenda. For [[FM Investments]], that combination of thin attention, fragmented authorship, and temporary law is the reason wrappers have to be built for incremental change rather than for a durable tax reform.
