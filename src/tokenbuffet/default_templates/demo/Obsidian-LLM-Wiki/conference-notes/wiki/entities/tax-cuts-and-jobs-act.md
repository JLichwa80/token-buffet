# Tax Cuts and Jobs Act

## Description

The Tax Cuts and Jobs Act is major federal tax legislation cited as tangible proof that years of preparatory economic modeling and think-tank groundwork can directly influence statutory policy. During legislative debates, House Speaker [[entities/paul-ryan|Paul Ryan]] consulted the [[entities/tax-foundation|Tax Foundation]] regarding the economic impact of a temporary corporate tax rate reduction, learning that temporary cuts fail to generate long-term growth. This finding prompted policymakers, guided by House Ways and Means Chairman [[entities/kevin-brady|Kevin Brady]], to pivot toward enacting a permanent corporate rate cut aimed at [[concepts/pro-growth-reform|Pro-Growth Reform]] rather than short-term political adjustments. While achieving corporate permanence, the law included sunset provisions for full expensing of research and development as well as capital investments, initiating subsequent campaigns to secure [[concepts/permanency|Permanency]]. The enactment of the statute exemplifies successful preparatory policy modeling, an achievement viewed as increasingly challenging to replicate in an era marked by heightened partisanship and populism.
