# Equities

## Description

Equities are securities that represent an ownership interest in a corporation, most often as common stock, with a residual claim on earnings and assets after creditors are paid. Shareholders may receive dividends, and the market price of the shares changes with expectations for growth, profits, and risk, so the asset class is the usual growth allocation in a long-term portfolio. Public shares of an issuer such as [[entities/apple|Apple]] trade on exchanges where prices are visible throughout the day, which makes individual companies easy to compare and to hold directly. A [[entities/mutual-fund|Mutual Fund]] pools capital from many investors and can hold a diversified basket of these shares under professional management, and an investor can also own the same companies as separate lots. Investors commonly pair that ownership exposure with fixed income such as a [[entities/treasury-bond|Treasury Bond]], because bond cash flows and price behavior differ from the residual claim of a shareholder. In taxable accounts, selling shares can realize a capital gain or loss, so equity lots are tracked when advisors measure after-tax results. *(No source content available for this page)*

## Related Concepts

Equities are the ownership building block inside growth allocations, pooled funds, and direct stock holdings. Their returns combine price change and dividends, and those cash flows can be redirected when a portfolio is rebalanced toward bonds or other asset classes. Because a sale can realize a gain or a loss, equity lots are a central input to after-tax portfolio decisions.
