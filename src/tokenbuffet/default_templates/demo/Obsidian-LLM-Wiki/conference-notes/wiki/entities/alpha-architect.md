# Equities

## Description

Equities are securities that represent an ownership interest in a company, most often issued as common stock. Shareholders hold a residual claim on earnings and assets after creditors are paid, and they generally receive voting rights plus dividends when the board declares them. Listed equities change hands on exchanges, so investors can observe a continuous public price throughout the trading day. In a balanced portfolio, equities supply the growth allocation that sits alongside income holdings such as a [[entities/treasury-bond|Treasury Bond]]. Many households and advisors obtain that growth exposure through a [[entities/mutual-fund|Mutual Fund]], which pools capital and holds a diversified basket of shares under professional management. A single large issuer such as [[entities/apple|Apple]] can still account for a material share of an index or a household portfolio, so company-level valuation, earnings, and dividend policy remain part of equity analysis. Price appreciation and dividends are the two return channels, and both respond to corporate actions, market cycles, and the tax rules that apply to the holder. *(No source content available for this page)*
