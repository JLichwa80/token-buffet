# California

## Description

California is the panel’s headline high-tax jurisdiction for the cost of selling a zero-basis concentrated position outright. The stated illustration is a $10 million zero-basis sale that leaves roughly $6 million after taxes, which the chapter on [[Two Big Tax Frictions]] treats as the first friction, with a second friction because reinvestment choices can create further taxable events. That arithmetic is why the discussion frames a bridge from source assets such as [[SpaceX]] stock to tax-aware destinations rather than an immediate sale, using a [[Variable Prepaid Forward]] as the connecting instrument inside the [[ETS Playbook]]. Audience note-taking clustered when California and [[Washington State capital gains]] were contrasted, placing California as the familiar combined federal-and-state drag and Washington as the jurisdiction where loss harvesting works differently. The state is therefore the numeric motive for variable prepaid forwards, tax-aware long-short shielding, and PPLI (private placement life insurance), not a separate planning regime the panel fully specifies.
