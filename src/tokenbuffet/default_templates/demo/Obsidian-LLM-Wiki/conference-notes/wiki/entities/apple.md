Apple is a major multinational technology corporation known for issuing widely traded corporate bonds. In the presentation, Apple's corporate debt is cited as a prime example of the opacity and fragmentation of bond pricing. The speaker challenges advisors by noting that almost none of them could accurately name the real-time price of Apple's debt without complex market lookup tools. This emphasizes the fundamental difference between liquid, centrally quoted equities and the over-the-counter debt market.
