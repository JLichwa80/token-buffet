- Original file: [[_Inbox/10-09 Keynote The Tax Trap in Bond Investing.md]]

## Core Content

This keynote argues that fixed income is often managed as a static allocation, which can cause tax-sensitive investors to miss better after-tax opportunities. Using Massachusetts as an example, the speaker challenges the assumption that residents of high-tax states should always hold home-state municipal bonds. The keynote cites a 25-year comparison in which national municipal bonds outperformed Massachusetts munis for a Massachusetts resident about 53% of the time, and says supply and demand can make taxable bonds competitive after taxes as well. It presents the fragmented over-the-counter bond market as a place where active managers may find mispriced securities. The speaker also argues that separately managed accounts (SMAs) allow direct ownership and account-level tax customization, while technology can help scale that work across many portfolios. Investors should compare fixed income choices against their own tax profiles and review allocations as conditions change. These claims and figures are those presented in the keynote.

## Main Points

- Fixed income allocations benefit from active review rather than a static “set-it-and-forget-it” approach.
- Home-state municipal bonds are not automatically the best after-tax choice; compare national munis and taxable bonds using the investor’s tax situation.
- Directly held bonds in SMAs can support account-level customization and tax-loss harvesting.
- The bond market’s large, fragmented structure may create opportunities to identify mispriced securities.
- The speaker presents technology as a way to evaluate bond opportunities against each account’s constraints at scale.
