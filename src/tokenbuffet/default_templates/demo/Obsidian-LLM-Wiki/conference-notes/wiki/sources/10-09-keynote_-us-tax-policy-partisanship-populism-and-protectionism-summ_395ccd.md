## Core Content

This keynote presentation delivered on behalf of the [[entities/tax-foundation|tax-foundation]] explores the deteriorating landscape of federal fiscal policy driven by [[concepts/partisanship-populism-and-protectionism|the-three-ps-of-modern-tax-policy]]—specifically [[concepts/partisanship|partisanship]], [[concepts/populism|populism]], and [[concepts/protectionism|protectionism]]. The speaker emphasizes that effective [[concepts/pro-growth-reform|pro-growth-reform]] must demonstrably expand [[concepts/capital-formation|capital-formation]] and [[concepts/labor-supply|labor-supply]], requiring [[concepts/permanency|permanency]] in core tax structures rather than temporary adjustments or a [[concepts/sunset-clause|sunset-clause]]. 

The discussion details how outside macroeconomic modeling informed former House Speaker [[entities/paul-ryan|paul-ryan]] and Chairman [[entities/kevin-brady|kevin-brady]] during the passage of the [[entities/tax-cuts-and-jobs-act|tax-cuts-and-jobs-act]], and how Senator [[entities/steve-daines|steve-daines]] later secured permanent [[concepts/r&d-expensing|r&d-expensing]]. Furthermore, the speaker warns that a reliance on each [[concepts/party-line-vote|party-line-vote]], combined with left-wing proposals like a [[concepts/wealth-tax|wealth-tax]] advocated by [[entities/bernie-sanders|bernie-sanders]] in states like [[entities/washington-state|washington-state]] and right-wing "no tax on" exemptions, results in a [[concepts/shrinking-tax-base|shrinking-tax-base]] while national debt interest absorbs one-fifth of federal revenue. Subnational tax policies across [[entities/indiana|indiana]] and [[entities/florida|florida]] serve as early indicators of future national fiscal battles.

## Main Points

- **The Three P's Framework**: Modern US tax policy is primarily dictated by partisanship, populism, and protectionism rather than long-term economic planning.
- **Standards for Pro-Growth Reform**: Sound tax reform is evaluated by its empirical contribution to long-term capital formation and labor supply rather than cosmetic rate adjustments.
- **Necessity of Permanency**: Temporary tax policies fail to stimulate sustained investment; legislative victories such as permanent R&D expensing demonstrate the need to avoid sunset provisions.
- **Partisan Volatility**: Increasing reliance on party-line votes creates sharp policy swings between administrations, while bipartisan consensus largely defaults to increased spending.
- **Base Erosion via Populism**: Simplistic proposals on both sides—ranging from wealth taxes to broad carve-outs like "no tax on" tips or overtime—narrow the tax base and concentrate tax obligations on fewer payers.
- **Fiscal Strain and Debt Servicing**: With 20% of tax revenue committed to debt interest payments, compounding deficits leave little flexibility as major statutory provisions approach expiration.
