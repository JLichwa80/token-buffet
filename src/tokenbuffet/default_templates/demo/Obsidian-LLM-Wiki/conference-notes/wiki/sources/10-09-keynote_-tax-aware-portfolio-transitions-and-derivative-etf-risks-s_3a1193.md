The presentation outlines practical pathways for executing a [[concepts/section-351-exchange|section-351-exchange]] and [[concepts/syndicated-351-exchange|syndicated-351-exchange]], allowing advisors to transition an [[concepts/ossified-portfolio|ossified-portfolio]] into broad-market vehicles such as [[entities/aaub|aaub]] without triggering immediate capital gains. ## Main Points

- Section 351 exchanges offer a compliant mechanism to transition appreciated, ossified portfolios into diversified destination ETFs like [[entities/aaub|aaub]] without triggering immediate taxable events.
- Eligibility requires meeting household-level diversification rules (maximum 25% in a single position and 50% in the top five positions), with contributed ETFs evaluated via look-through assessment.
- Custodians map original cost basis and acquisition dates directly to newly issued ETF shares, preserving client tax histories.
- Packaging options in 1940 Act funds and establishing in-kind option exchange capabilities have driven significant growth across buffer and outcome-oriented ETFs.
- Synthetic borrowing through short option box spreads achieves institutional financing rates where interest expense is treated as a capital loss, lowering effective borrowing costs.
- Fully covered call strategies face acute oscillation risk, creating permanent NAV decay during market whipsaws due to capped upside and full downside capture.
- Single counterparty over-the-counter derivative structures introduce severe pricing drag and dealer markups.
- Advisors should use fiduciary frameworks such as the Grandmother Effect to avoid inequitable return of capital manipulations and hidden derivative risks.
