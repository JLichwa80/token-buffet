## Core Content

This source summarizes a tax-conference keynote addressing real-world investment returns after accounting for fees, taxes, and liquidation drag. The presentation highlights that conventional active and [[concepts/smart-beta|smart-beta]] strategies frequently fail to beat benchmarks on an [[concepts/after-tax-benchmarking|after-tax benchmarking]] scoreboard, largely due to tax friction and inefficient vehicle structures. Cap-weighted portfolios are framed as possessing an [[concepts/active-side-of-indexing|active side of indexing]] that systematically purchases expensive growth stocks and sells discounted value holdings, leading to [[concepts/sectorcountry-distortion-under-cap-weights|sector/country distortion under cap weights]]. 

In contrast, [[entities/rafi|fundamental indexing]]—exemplified by [[entities/rafi|RAFI]] and supported by institutional vendors like [[entities/schwab|Schwab]]—weights companies by economic footprint rather than market capitalization. This systematic approach captures [[concepts/rebalancing-alpha|rebalancing alpha]] by trading against price moves unconfirmed by business fundamentals, capitalizing on [[concepts/mean-reversion|mean reversion]]. When implemented through disciplined [[concepts/cash-flow-rebalancing|cash-flow rebalancing]], ongoing [[concepts/tax-loss-harvesting|loss harvesting]], and high [[concepts/tax-efficiency-of-wrappers|tax efficiency of wrappers]], investors capture significant [[concepts/tax-alpha|tax alpha]] over baseline [[concepts/fama–french-alpha|Fama–French alpha]] rather than suffering [[concepts/negative-tax-alpha|negative tax alpha]].

## Main Points

- The meaningful evaluation standard for any investment strategy is performance net of all fees, taxes, and final liquidation, against which most conventional factor and active strategies underperform.
- Vehicle structure critically impacts net performance: mutual funds lag benchmarks significantly after taxes and liquidation (~3% to 3.5% drag), whereas ETFs retain substantially more pre-tax return (~40 bps to 1.1% drag).
- Standard [[concepts/cap-weighted-indexing|cap-weighted indexing]] is not purely passive; its marginal turnover functions as an active momentum strategy buying overvalued stocks and selling undervalued ones.
- [[entities/rafi|Fundamental indexing]] ([[entities/rafi|RAFI]]) breaks the price-weight link by sizing holdings based on sales, cash flows, dividends/buybacks, and book value, harvesting durable [[concepts/rebalancing-alpha|rebalancing alpha]].
- Implementation discipline—leveraging cash flows for rebalancing and harvesting losses continuously—enables low-turnover strategies to generate around 170–190 bps of [[concepts/tax-alpha|tax alpha]], whereas unmanaged realization yields [[concepts/negative-tax-alpha|negative tax alpha]].
