## Core Content

This conference keynote analyzes the interaction between [[concepts/pretax-alpha|pretax alpha]], manager skill measured by [[concepts/information-ratio-ir|information ratio]], and long-term [[concepts/after-tax-outcomes|after-tax wealth]] within [[concepts/tax-aware-long-short-overlay|tax-aware long-short overlay]] strategies. Across 21 rolling 15-year simulations, the findings demonstrate that while higher manager skill paradoxically locks up [[concepts/long-only-direct-indexing|long-only direct indexing]] portfolios by accumulating [[concepts/embedded-gains|built-in gains]] and exhausting opportunities for [[concepts/tax-loss-harvesting|tax-loss harvesting]], long-short structures experience the opposite effect. In leveraged long-short accounts, excess alpha causes portfolios to drift under-levered, systematically triggering [[concepts/position-creation|position creation]]. This rebalancing injects fresh securities with full [[concepts/cost-basis|cost basis]], continuously supplying fuel for tax-loss harvesting and sharply increasing [[concepts/cumulative-net-capital-losses-cncls|cumulative net capital losses (CNCLs)]]. However, overlays must overcome a substantial [[concepts/cost-stack|cost stack]], requiring sufficient gross alpha to avoid a negative [[concepts/net-information-ratio|net information ratio]] across targeted levels of [[concepts/tracking-error|tracking error]].

## Main Points

- Tax-aware long-short overlay strategies grew rapidly from ~$9 billion in 2023 to over $150 billion in AUM by 2026.
- In long-only direct indexing, high manager alpha accelerates unrealized capital gains, locking up the portfolio and diminishing future loss-harvesting opportunities.
- In tax-aware long-short architectures, positive pretax alpha acts as a tax engine: it makes the portfolio under-levered, forcing position creation through rebalancing.
- Position creation introduces new long and short lots at full cost basis, systematically refreshing harvestable loss capacity regardless of market direction due to the negative market correlation of shorts.
- Multi-decade simulations show that higher manager Information Ratios (IR) substantially scale cumulative net capital losses and multiply ending after-tax wealth by 3x to over 6x in extended leverage configurations (e.g., 250/150).
- Operating long-short overlays entails financing, borrow, and transaction expenses; without demonstrable gross alpha, a manager's net Information Ratio can deteriorate to -0.6 or lower.
