## Core Content

This tax-conference presentation, titled “Wrappers, Washington, and What’s Next,” argues that the distance between markets and Washington is a field of arbitrage. [[entities/fm-investments|FM Investments]] treats fragmented, often temporary tax rules as a source of [[concepts/tax-alpha|tax alpha]], using [[concepts/wrappers|wrappers]] to turn policy into durable portfolio results. The speaker treats [[entities/congress|Congress]] as an accidental portfolio architect: provisions such as the [[concepts/amt|alternative minimum tax]] can backfire, and [[concepts/sunset-clause|sunsets]] mean most rules expire. Three product themes follow. A [[concepts/dual-share-class|dual share class]], described as the first design approved and issued with [[entities/vanguard|Vanguard]], places a mutual fund and an ETF on one portfolio with the same NAV and rights, illustrated by overlapping T-Bill and [[entities/tbmax|TBMax]] total returns. [[concepts/tokenized-t-bills|Tokenized T-bills]] are the next operational step, with an executive order pressing the [[entities/sec|SEC]] and the [[entities/cftc|CFTC]]. [[concepts/cpmgcphy|CPMG/CPHY]] rotates around bond ETF ex-dates so NAV can accrue income while ordinary-income distributions are avoided. These claims are the speaker’s.

## Main Points

- FM Investments treats the Wall Street–Washington gap as arbitrage and builds wrappers that turn temporary tax rules into tax alpha.
- Policy formation is described as 40% career technocrats, 30% elected officials, 20% lobbyists, and 10% think tanks, with sunsets and about one year of notice rather than durable reform.
- The dual share class, claimed as the first Vanguard-approved design, puts a mutual fund and an ETF on one portfolio with the same NAV and rights. T-Bill and TBMax total returns are shown overlapping.
- The speaker says attacks on ETF in-kind creation and redemption are dead on arrival, and calls the SEC’s dual share class rules a case where the investor always wins.
- Tokenizing T-bills is expected to cut ETF-scale operating costs of about 3–6 basis points, break the penny spread, and allow 24/7 trading, backed by a recent executive order aimed at the SEC and the CFTC.
- CPMG/CPHY rotates into a proxy ETF around ex-dates so bond NAV can accrue income without ordinary-income distributions. Cited costs are about 1 basis point per month to roll and a 25 basis point add-on fee, with long-term capital gains if held 366 days.
- AMT is the Cobra-effect example: aimed at 155 wealthy families, it now reaches many more taxpayers, so products should assume second-order effects and sunsets.
